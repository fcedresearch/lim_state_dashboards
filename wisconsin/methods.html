Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results count as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before. A launch from April 1 through September 15 starts that coming school year, which is Year 1. A launch from September 16 through March 31 comes after the year was underway: that year is Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and the next year is Year 1. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day. An August 2023 launch counts from 2023-24; an October 2024 launch has 2024-25 as its Year 0 and counts from 2025-26.
  • A school's baseline is its last spring test before launch. A Leader in Me school's years before its launch count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time; its Year 0 never does.
  • 3 school-years on this page are a Year 0.
  • 2 Wisconsin schools on the roster carry no start date. Their launch cannot be placed, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 06, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 18 of the 54 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 4 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Wisconsin's own measures join through the state district and school codes NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited by name within district against the WISEdash files' own names and against DOMO's dropout rate; a school with no state code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Wisconsin measures.
  • Chronic absence and the graduation rate stay DOMO's own: the state folder carries neither as a rate, only the report card's component scores, which sit in the research pack labelled as scores. Nothing is filled and nothing is overwritten.
  • The attendance rate sits on the rows by band from the file's own grade rows; a rate of exactly 100 or 0 is read as unreported. State-suppressed cells ('*', '--', '[Data Suppressed]') stay missing, never zero; a school-grade the file publishes with no removal row had none.
  • Suspensions per 100 are removals from the 2016-17 and later actions files over the third-Friday enrollment; the 2010-11 to 2015-16 students-suspended shares are a different measure and never join the line. A rate on fewer than ten students, test-takers, exams or completers is left missing.
  • The report card's private Choice-program rows are set aside, schools under alternate accountability carry no score, the On-Track and Postsecondary Readiness score (to 2018-19) never joins the On-Track to Graduation line, and the ACT graduates file's classes of 2015 and earlier are excluded by name; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Wisconsin card, row and pack record.

Judgement calls we had to make

The places the rules needed a threshold. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

Launch year (Year 0 and Year 1)
September 15 (decided October 6, 2026). A school that launched from April 1 through September 15 counts that coming school year as its Year 1; its baseline is the spring test before launch. A school that launched from September 16 through March 31 has that school year as its Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and counts from the next school year. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day (most of the Middle Atlantic and, by law, most of Minnesota). The line replaced July 1, which put June and August launches for the same school year a full year apart.
School year boundary for exits
July 1. An expiration in June belongs to the year that just ended; one in July belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 41 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 0 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 37% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 28 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 13 pairs (0 unmatched).

4 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): N-Vision Learning Center, Northland Pines Montessori Learning Center (NPMLC), Racine Alternative Learning, Waupaca Learning Center Elementary.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 131,587 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 100.0% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 1.0% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 3.1% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.9% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 68.8% of rows; the years and subjects that do not add up (2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math, 2014-15 Social Studies, 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science, 2015-16 Social Studies, 2016-17 ELA, 2016-17 Math, 2016-17 Science, 2016-17 Social Studies) carry no level values.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Wisconsin's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 68.8% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values: 2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math, 2014-15 Social Studies, 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science, 2015-16 Social Studies, 2016-17 ELA, 2016-17 Math, 2016-17 Science, 2016-17 Social Studies), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard. The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Wisconsin that is 1,137 Title I schools, 234 charter schools, 8 magnet schools and 16 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Wisconsin's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Math: Mathematics; ELA: English Language Arts; Social Studies: Social Studies; Science: Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Wisconsin publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 54 Leader in Me schools and 7 of 2,017 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 1 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 3.7% of Leader in Me schools and 10.8% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 50 of the 54 roster schools carry a date, and 18 were past it by more than 30 days on October 06, 2026. Exits by year: 2021: 1, 2022: 1, 2024: 3, 2025: 10, 2026: 3. 4 roster schools have no expiration date (3 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 3 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 8.32 against comparison 12.68 (p = 0.003, 37 vs 1730 schools), and with them 8.79 against 12.68 (p = 0.006, 40 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2014-15 to 2015-16, 52.1 to 43.5 (-8.6); ELA 2022-23 to 2023-24, 40.4 to 51.0 (+10.6); Math 2022-23 to 2023-24, 40.3 to 51.9 (+11.6). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Joining Wisconsin's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Wisconsin's WISEdash downloads and report card files key on the state's four-digit district and school codes instead (Abbotsford is 0007, its elementary school 0020), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 2,084 id pairs for 2,070 of the 2,071 schools in Wisconsin's DOMO records (22 schools carry more than one state code across the years, a renumbering or a move between districts, and join under each), including 54 of 54 Leader in Me schools and 2,016 of 2,017 comparison schools; the one comparison school it cannot reach appears in none of NCES's directories from 2016-17 on and under no matching name in the WISEdash files. The map was audited a second way, by name: the WISEdash files' own school names agree with DOMO's on 2,083 of 2,084 crosswalked schools (the exception is DOMO's 'KM Global School' against the state's 'Kettle Moraine Global School for Global Leadership and Innovation', the same school under a longer name), and an independent name-within-district join that uses no id at all speaks for 2,057 schools, both speak for 2,048 and agree on 2,048; 8 schools NCES has never listed (closed before its 2016-17 directory) join by the name join alone, each on exactly one unclaimed state id of the same name. An independent value check from a field the map was not built from: DOMO's own dropout rate is the WISEdash dropout rate truncated to one decimal on 100.0% of the 7,728 school-years both carry (within 0.1 on 100.0%, r = 0.9999), so the ids line up and the two are one figure; DOMO's student count against the attendance file's correlates at 0.958 across 16,697 school-years, half of them within 10.5%.
  • Every Leader in Me school carries a state code. All 54 Leader in Me schools in Wisconsin's DOMO records appear under a Wisconsin district and school code in NCES's directory, and 54 of them in the WISEdash attendance files; none is named as unreached.
  • Chronic absence and graduation stay DOMO's; the state folder carries neither as a rate. None of the Wisconsin files in the folder carries a chronic-absence rate or a graduation rate by school. The report card carries a chronic absenteeism component score (2020-21 on) and a graduation rate component score, which are points toward the On-Track priority area, not the rates: against DOMO's chronic absence on the same school-years the score correlates at -0.836 across 7,371 pairs and is its complement within 0.15 on only 2.9% of them; against DOMO's graduation rate the score correlates at 0.579 across 3,327 pairs and equals it on 6.3%. So the core's two measures stand as DOMO carries them (chronic absence 2015-16 to 2024-25, graduation 2015-16 to 2024-25), nothing is filled and nothing is overwritten, and the two component scores sit in the research pack labelled as scores. The state's attendance rate, which the folder does carry, is on the rows and the card as the uncapped companion of chronic absence.
  • The attendance rate: by band from the file's own grade rows. The state's certified attendance files (2010-11 to 2024-25) carry possible and actual days for every school, whole and by grade, and the published rate rebuilds from the day counts on every numeric row. The rows carry the rate by band: for a school that spans both, grades PK to 5 on the primary band and 6 to 12 on the secondary band, each summed from the band's own grade rows (a grade the state suppresses for fewer than six students is left out of the sum; the grade rows rebuild the whole-school rate on 100.0% of the 26,430 school-years where every grade is published); a one-band school carries its whole-school rate. The card and the pack carry the whole-school rate. 834 whole-school rates across the years read exactly 100 or 0; they are virtual academies reporting every possible day attended, or none, and are read as unreported. The closure years are left out of every comparison.
  • Suspension is a measure, not a levels card, and Wisconsin's discipline files come in two eras. From 2016-17 the state's discipline actions files count removals by type (out-of-school suspension, expulsion with and without services) with the third-Friday enrollment beside them, so out-of-school suspensions per 100 students is a per-school rate that runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test). It is counted per action: a student suspended five times counts five. The 2010-11 to 2015-16 files count students suspended (the share of students with at least one suspension), a different measure; those years (2014-15, 2015-16 inside this page's window) are in the research pack under their own name and never on the suspension line, and 28 of their published rates pass 100 (a share of students cannot) and are read as unreported. A school that spans both bands is split from the file's own grade rows, which rebuild the school total wherever no grade is suppressed: 5,712 school-year-bands exactly, 808 by subtraction from the published school total where only the other band is fully published, and 1,416 left to the whole-school rate; a one-band school carries its whole-school rate. A school with fewer than ten students enrolled (183 school-years) carries no rate. Incidents by behavior type and expulsions per 100 are in the research pack.
  • The report card's scores: one scale, three formulas, and no card for two years. Wisconsin's school report cards (2011-12, 2012-13, 2013-14, 2015-16, 2016-17, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25) give every public school an overall accountability score from 0 to 100 and a rating on five cut points that have not moved since 2011-12; no card was issued for 2014-15 (a legislated pause) or 2019-20 (the closures). The overall score and the growth priority-area score are on the rows and the card; the achievement, target group, on-track and closing-gaps areas and the attendance, chronic-absenteeism and graduation components are in the research pack. The formula has changed under the scale: from 2017-18 the weight on achievement against growth varies with a school's economically-disadvantaged share; the 2020-21 redesign replaced Closing Gaps with Target Group Outcomes and On-Track and Postsecondary Readiness with On-Track to Graduation; and the 2023-24 Forward Exam standard is lower than before, which lifts achievement scores from that card on. The straight-numbers window at the top of the page sits inside the current formula; the full-history window scores each school against its own year, which absorbs a statewide shift but does not make the eras one series, so the score is read beside the growth test rather than in its place. The growth area is the state's value-added measure and has kept one scale since the 2015-16 card (statewide mean 66.6 that year, 65.68 in 2024-25); high schools carry it from 2018-19 (1,499 schools with a growth score in 2017-18, 1,887 in 2018-19). The On-Track to Graduation and Target Group Outcomes lines on the card run from 2021-22, inside the redesign. 4,057 private Choice-program rows across the files (one or two per private school) sit under no public school code and are set aside; 1,791 school-years under alternate accountability ("AR") and 412 not rated carry no score; a caret the 2016-17 to 2018-19 files append to 425 ratings, which the field description does not explain, is stripped and the rating read as written.
  • The ACT: the grade-11 census on the card, the graduating class in the pack. Since spring 2015 every Wisconsin junior takes the ACT, and the statewide file (2014-15 to 2024-25) carries each high school's mean composite with the number tested and enrolled, so the line is a whole-cohort figure on one population definition throughout; a school with fewer than ten test-takers (384 school-years) carries no mean, and the college-ready shares by subject and the participation rate are in the research pack. The graduates file gives the ACT of each graduating class instead, and its population changes with the census: the class of 2015 was the last to take the test by choice (41,530 graduates tested statewide, mean 22.11), the class of 2016 the first whose juniors all sat it (60,620 tested, mean 20.32), so the class of 2015 and earlier are excluded by name from the graduates' composite in the pack.
  • First-fall college enrollment is a ratio of two state counts, and says which. The WISEdash postsecondary file is the state's National Student Clearinghouse match: for each graduating class, the number of graduates found enrolled in college by timing (first fall, second fall, later) and by institution level, location and type. It carries no count of graduates, and the folder holds no completion file, so the page divides the first-fall enrollees by the dropout file's count of seniors who completed the year (its grade-12 completers, published for the same school and year): the ratio runs a few points below the state's own share of graduates, the same way for every school, and is labelled per 100 grade-12 completers wherever it appears. Statewide it reads 36,091 first-fall enrollees for the class of 2011 and 32,944 for the class of 2025. The newest class has had one fall so far, so its later enrollment is not yet in the file; the first-fall figure is complete for every class and is the one drawn. A school with fewer than ten completers carries no rate. The 4-year and 2-year splits and the in-state share are in the research pack.
  • AP: the share of exams passed, with the counts in the pack. The certified AP files (2010-11 to 2024-25) carry, for every school, the students tested, the exams taken and the exams scored 3 or above, all exams together; the published share rebuilds from the counts on every row, and the state suppresses a school's share where the counts are small. A school with fewer than ten exams carries no share here. The statewide mean of the school shares sat between 51.19 and 58.19 through 2022-23, then 63.45 in 2023-24 and 70.17 in 2024-25: the College Board recalibrated the scoring of many exams in those two years, so the last two points are a different standard from the years before for every school alike and are read apart from them rather than as a trend. It is a level that travels with who takes the exams, which is why it sits on the card and not on the rows.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 140 surveys across 42 Wisconsin schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 26 answered by more than one group (blended), 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (42 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 5 of the 34 schools counted as Leader in Me in Wisconsin meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2024-25, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.

Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.

66 measures were screened, grouped into 17 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 4 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Wisconsin publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Wisconsin's suspension measure counts removals, not students, so a school's rate cannot separate one student suspended five times from five students suspended once, and districts differ in how they record; a school's own direction over time is firmer ground than a gap between schools.
  • The report card's overall score has kept its 0-to-100 scale and rating cut points while its formula changed in 2017-18, 2020-21 and 2023-24, so it is a level to describe beside the growth test, never a growth claim of its own.
  • First-fall college enrollment is divided by the dropout file's grade-12 completers because the folder carries no graduate count; it runs a few points below the state's own share of graduates for every school alike.
  • The state folder carries no chronic-absence or graduation rate, so those two measures are DOMO's alone on this page.
  • The ACT composite and the AP pass share are means and shares among the students who tested; the counts are in the pack.
