State Definitions of College and Career Readiness

20 states: the 19 whose pages carry a readiness measure, plus Illinois. Definitions as of November 12, 2026, from each state's own documents, with All4Ed's July 2025 review where a state document did not give the detail.

Every state decides for itself what counts as a high school student being ready for college, a career or the military, and the rules differ more than the shared name suggests. A Texas graduate counts as ready with one AP exam score of 3, while an Illinois student needs a grade point average, an attendance rate and a set of academic and career indicators all at once. We keep each state's definition here, next to the measure our page carries, so a readiness number on a state page can always be read with its rules beside it.

Across the country, 42 states use at least one college and career readiness indicator in accountability, and 12 publish how students met it among the separate measures (All4Ed and Urban Institute, July 2025). The route categories below are ours, so we can group similar measures across states on the national page while every comparison stays inside one state.

Readiness results compare within one state and within one set of rules

A readiness rate is only as comparable as the rules behind it. Our state pages compare Leader in Me schools with comparison schools in the same state, on the same rules, and read each class under the rule of its year. Several definitions changed recently or will soon: Mississippi's new Readiness Index starts in 2025-26, Ohio's current component counts from 2023-24, New York's index returned to accountability in 2025-26, Colorado's indicator changes with the 2027 frameworks, and South Carolina's changes with the 2027 report cards.

Some definitions count what students did and others count how well they did it. Oklahoma's indicator and the participation half of Nevada's count taking an advanced course or a CTE program; Florida, Kentucky and Texas count an exam score, college credit or a credential. When we group measures on the national page, we keep participation routes and performance routes apart.

Two definitions reach past test scores and coursework. New York counts a Seal of Civic Readiness and Ohio counts an honors diploma in social science and civic engagement. Illinois's guidance counts community service, organized activities, attendance and a named career interest, although the state does not yet use the indicator in accountability.
