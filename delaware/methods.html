Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 4 Delaware schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 20 of the 37 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 3 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Delaware's own measures join through the state district and school codes NCES's directory carries beside the federal id (the school code is unique statewide and is the key), audited against the state files' own school and district names and against DOMO's chronic absence; a school with no state code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Delaware measures.
  • Every Delaware file is read on its all-students row only, and its School Year is the year that ended (2025 is 2024-25); 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Delaware card and row.
  • The state's own chronic-absence rate fills chronic absence only where DOMO has no value, never overwriting one, having been checked as the same measure (DOMO's Delaware figure is the state's to a tenth of a point); redacted rows and exact-zero shells stay missing; the attendance rate is rebuilt from average days enrolled and absent and carried as a whole-school measure on each band.
  • Out-of-school and in-school suspension are the share of students with at least one such action, read from the state's total row for each, published where five or more students and left missing below that (never zero); expulsions and the alternative-program placement share go to the research pack.
  • Teacher retention is the one-year same-school rate of the classroom-teacher roll-up, shown on the year retained into, on ten or more teachers; the 2023-24 year (an empty shell) is excluded by name and exact zeros are read as unreported.
  • The state's growth measure (average share of the growth target met) is levels on the card and values in the research pack, never a row measure.
  • 2025-26 grades 3-8 reading and math (from the Delaware Open Data Portal's Student Assessment Performance dataset) extend the reading-and-math figure; proficiency is the state's percent proficient, as in prior years; no achievement levels.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 17 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 10 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start -0.2 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median -0.1), and 41% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 16 pairs (9 unmatched); Secondary: 1 pairs (1 unmatched).

1 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Hodgson (Paul M.) Vocational Technical High School.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 11,458 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 98.3% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.8% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.7% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.7% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 29.9% of rows; the years and subjects that do not add up (2016-17 Math, 2016-17 Science) carry no level values.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. Delaware's file reports achievement levels that cannot stand as measures here: the latest year's reading and math levels do not add up (four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2; levels add up on 29.9% of rows). The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Delaware that is 135 Title I schools, 24 charter schools, 3 magnet schools and 2 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Delaware's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English Language Arts; Math: Mathematics; Social Studies: Social Studies; Science: Science. Left out because they are not achievement tests: Essay.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Delaware publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 37 Leader in Me schools and 12 of 163 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 12 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 8.1% of Leader in Me schools and 12.9% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 34 of the 37 roster schools carry a date, and 20 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2019: 1, 2022: 2, 2023: 1, 2024: 7, 2025: 6, 2026: 3. 3 roster schools have no expiration date (3 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 3 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 0.16 against comparison 1.86 (p = 0.501, 12 vs 99 schools), and with them 1.76 against 1.86 (p = 0.965, 15 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Joining Delaware's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Delaware's files key on the state's district and school codes instead (Capital School District is 13, Central Middle School its school 644), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 199 id pairs for 199 of the 200 schools on this page, plus 1 school NCES gives no state code that the state files name once within the district (Moyer (Maurice J.) Academy -> 75-574), including 37 of 37 Leader in Me schools and 163 of 163 comparison schools. Delaware's school code is unique statewide, so the join is on the school code with the district code as a check; two schools carry a different district code in the state files' latest row than in NCES's directory (Charter School of Wilmington: NCES lists district code 70, the state files' latest row 32; Delaware Military Academy: NCES lists district code 79, the state files' latest row 32) and join on the school code, which did not change. The map was checked a second way, against the state files' own names, which it was not built from: the school name agrees with DOMO's on 199 of 199 crosswalked schools and the district name on 193 of 196. An independent value check from a file the map was not built from either: the state's chronic-absence rate against DOMO's on the same school-years, 1,257 pairs (227 at Leader in Me schools), a correlation of 1.0 with 100.0% within a tenth of a point (the largest gap 0.09 points, DOMO's value never above the state's, so DOMO carries the state's own figure cut to a tenth rather than rounded).
  • Every Delaware file labels a school year by the year it ended. The state's own column notes say so (2019 is the year that ended in June 2019), and the files bear it out: the attendance file's 2020 rows carry a median of 112.3 days enrolled against about 157.4 in every other year, the March 2020 closure, and chronic absence is redacted on every 2020 row. Each trimmed file carries several rows per school-year across race, gender, grade and special-population columns; only the all-students row is read (every one of those columns reading 'All Students' or 'All Educators'), and in the discipline and mobility files one row per action or per staff group and retention type is read deliberately, named below.
  • Attendance: the state's own chronic absence fills the year DOMO lacks; the attendance rate is a measure of its own. The Student Attendance dataset (2014-15 to 2024-25) carries, per school, the students enrolled as of June, the number and share chronically absent (10% or more of enrolled days), and the average days enrolled and absent per student. The published share rebuilds from the counts on 2186 of 2186 reported rows. On the 1,257 school-years where both publish one, DOMO's chronic absence is the state's figure to a tenth of a point, so they are read as the same measure and the state's value fills the 400 school-years DOMO has no value for (2014-15: 180, 2015-16: 39, 2016-17: 41, 2017-18: 42, 2018-19: 12, 2021-22: 20, 2022-23: 21, 2023-24: 21, 2024-25: 24; 5 counted Leader in Me schools), never a school-year DOMO carries; DOMO carries no 2014-15 chronic absence for Delaware, which is where nearly all of the fill lands. Rows the state redacts stay missing (23 outside 2019-20, early-childhood centers and special programs for the most part; a redacted row's zero is a shell, not a rate), and 8 reported rows whose chronic absence is exactly zero with no absences recorded are read as empty shells (2014-15 Central Middle School (1060 students); 2014-15 First State Military Academy (15 students); 2014-15 Delaware Design-Lab High School (20 students); 2015-16 East Side Charter School (546 students); 2015-16 Delaware STEM Academy (142 students); 2018-19 Brandywine SITE (26 students); 2022-23 Newark Charter School (2995 students); 2024-25 Seaford Middle School (874 students)). The attendance rate is rebuilt as one hundred minus average days absent over average days enrolled, on reported rows with at least one absence recorded: a whole-school rate carried on each band the school reports, as the core carries chronic absence, and a different cut of the same file (chronic absence counts students past a threshold; the attendance rate counts days).
  • Discipline: suspension rates are measures, published where five or more students were suspended. The Student Discipline dataset (2014-15 to 2024-25) carries, per school and disciplinary action, the number of students given the action at least once, the enrollment, the share, the incidents and the average length in days. The row for 'Out-of-School Suspension' is the total (the file splits it again by whether the student was placed in the state's alternative program, CDAP, under two spellings across the years, and drops the split in 2024-25); that row and the 'In-School Suspension' row are read, and 'Expulsion' goes to the research pack, where it is published for a handful of schools a year. The published share rebuilds from the counts on 4471 of 4471 reported rows. The state redacts a row where fewer than five students had the action (the smallest published count is 5), leaving the enrollment in place; in 2024-25 the out-of-school rate is published for 178 of 209 schools in the file, redacted for 29, and 2 schools have no out-of-school row at all (no published count; read as missing, never as zero). So the rates rest on the schools that suspend at least five students in a year, which is most schools and nearly every secondary school, and a school that gets under five drops out of the panel rather than showing a low rate; the redaction flags are in the research pack beside the rates. Both rates are whole-school rates carried on each band the school reports, and they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test). The state counts students with at least one action, so a school's rate cannot separate one student suspended five times from five students suspended once; the incidents per 100 students are in the research pack.
  • Teacher retention: the year is the year retained into, and 2023-24 is an empty shell. The Educator Mobility dataset (2014-15 to 2024-25) carries, per school, staff group and retention type, the share of staff on the November roll who were still at the same school, at another school in the district, at a school in another district, or out of Delaware public schools altogether one to five Novembers later, with the counts behind each. The classroom-teacher roll-up (Job Classification ALL, all experience levels) is read, and the one-year same-school rate is the measure. The School Year is the year the teachers were retained into: the base totals of the two-, three-, four- and five-year rows labelled a year agree with the one-year row labelled one, two, three and four years earlier on 2027 of 2143, 2005 of 2143, 1519 of 1899, 1272 of 1423 pairs (under the other reading, the row counting the staff of its own year, on 467 of 2346, 292 of 2314, 234 of 2272, 202 of 2240), so the 2024-25 row counts the teachers of November 2023 still there in November 2024, and it is shown on 2024-25. The four rates sum to one hundred on 2392 of 2636 one-year rows and the published rate rebuilds from the counts on 2393 of 2393. The 2023-24 year is an empty shell: every one of its 2786 rows carries a zero numerator and every one-year row a zero staff count, so it is excluded by name and the line jumps from 2022-23 to 2024-25. 17 rates of exactly zero on five or more teachers are read as unreported rather than as a school that kept no teacher (a closed or renumbered school whose teachers moved within the district: 2022-23 The Wallace Wallin School (5 teachers, turnover 0.0%); 2018-19 Dunbar (Paul Laurence) Elementary School (27 teachers, turnover 7.4%); 2018-19 Red Clay District Administration (8 teachers, turnover 0.0%); 2019-20 Appoquinimink PreSchool Center (15 teachers, turnover 6.7%); 2019-20 Elbert-Palmer Elementary School (21 teachers, turnover 4.8%); 2019-20 Pulaski (Casimir) Elementary School (26 teachers, turnover 3.8%)). Schools with fewer than ten teachers in the base year (76 school-years, administrative offices and small programs) stay off the rows and the card; those with five to nine keep their rate in the research pack with the count; under five carry no rate anywhere. The state publishes every count in full, so nothing is suppressed. Turnover (left Delaware public schools) is a chip on the card; the transfer rates, the three- and five-year rates, the early-career and experienced splits and whether the principal was retained are in the research pack. On the card the Leader in Me line runs from 2015 (4 schools) to 2025 (21 schools).
  • Growth is described on the card, never run through the growth test. The Student Growth dataset carries the measure the Delaware Student Success Framework has used since 2018: each student in grades 4 to 8 has a growth target in reading and in math, and a school's figure is the average share of the target its students reached, all students and for the school's lowest- and highest-achieving quarters, with English learners' progress toward proficiency beside it. It is published for 2017-18, 2018-19, 2022-23, 2023-24 only (736 school-years for 200 schools; the state row equals the student-weighted mean of the school rows to a tenth in every year, checked). A score of how far students moved is already a change, so it sits on the card as levels by group and in the research pack, with the district and state rows in the pack's published section, and never joins the rows: a growth test on a growth score would test acceleration. 11 school values run above 100 (students who beat their targets on average) and are kept as published.
  • 2025-26 reading and math come from Delaware's own Smarter Balanced results and extend the reading-and-math figure to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Delaware. The 2025-26 rows (950 school-grade-subject rows for 154 schools) were read from the Student Assessment Performance dataset on the Delaware Open Data Portal (updated September 29, 2026) and joined to the DOMO id through the NCES directory's Delaware school code. Proficiency is the state's percent proficient (Smarter Balanced levels 3 and 4), as in every prior year. Checked against the same dataset's 2024-25 rows, all 940 of DOMO's 940 Delaware grade 3-8 reading and math rows are in the state's file, and the percent proficient matches DOMO's on 940 of them. The state's file has no achievement levels, so 2025-26 shows proficiency only, as every year since 2018-19 does; the levels wait for the state's by-level release. Rows the state redacts for small numbers carry no value. 2025-26 is the same Smarter Balanced test on the same scale as 2024-25.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 76 surveys across 26 Delaware schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (26 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 2 of the 15 schools counted as Leader in Me in Delaware meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.

Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.

56 measures were screened, grouped into 16 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 3 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Delaware publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • This page is descriptive: only 15 Leader in Me schools have results at both ends of the straight-numbers window, so a significance verdict would rest on too few schools to trust.
  • Delaware redacts a school's suspension count under five students, so the suspension lines rest on schools that suspended at least five students in the year; an elementary school that gets under five drops out of the panel rather than showing a low rate.
  • Delaware's discipline file counts students with at least one action, so a school's rate cannot separate one student suspended five times from five students suspended once.
  • The Educator Mobility file's 2023-24 year is an empty shell in the state's own publication, so teacher retention and turnover jump from 2022-23 to 2024-25; the rate compares two November rolls, the state's own definition, so a teacher who left mid-year and was back the next November counts as retained and one who left over the summer counts as not retained.
  • The growth measure exists for four school years (2017-18, 2018-19, 2022-23 and 2023-24) and is a score against each student's own target, so it is levels, described, never tested, and the state's targets themselves changed across the pandemic gap.
  • Delaware's trimmed files carry no district or state rows for attendance, discipline or mobility, so the pack's published section carries the state's and each district's growth figures only.
  • The state's own file for the newest year does not yet carry every test DOMO carried the year before (2025-26 10th Grade Science (35 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade English Language Arts (41 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade Mathematics (38 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade Science (1 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade Social Studies (41 rows the year before); 2025-26 4th Grade Social Studies (105 rows the year before); 2025-26 5th Grade Science (92 rows the year before); 2025-26 5th Grade Social Studies (100 rows the year before); 2025-26 6th Grade Social Studies (57 rows the year before); 2025-26 7th Grade Social Studies (59 rows the year before); 2025-26 8th Grade Science (45 rows the year before); 2025-26 8th Grade Social Studies (58 rows the year before); and 1 more). Those results usually come out later in the fall. So a school is never compared on one set of tests against a different set, a school that took one of those tests the year before has its newest-year results in that grade band and subject left out (59 rows at 11 schools) until the missing results are released and folded in; the rest of its results, and every earlier year, are unchanged.
