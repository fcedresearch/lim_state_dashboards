Methods for the National Page

This page is built from the 51 state pages, not from the schools directly. Every number on it is an effect computed inside one state (a Leader in Me school against the comparison schools in its own state, on its own test, in its own years) and then combined across states. No school is ever compared with a school in another state, and no level (a "national percent proficient") is ever pooled, because a percentage point of proficiency in one state is not a percentage point in another.

Built 2026-10-06 from pages built 2026-10-06 to 2026-10-06, all on one rule set ({"builder": "build_payload_core.py", "builder_sha": "80c29b2972e0", "rules_files": ["lim_rules.py", "analysis.py"], "rules_fingerprint": "14c8e8e4e5ca", "skill_version": "2026-10-06"}). 93,039 schools carry values, 3,839 of them counted as Leader in Me in 2024-25.

State inclusion rule

Proposed rule: every state with three or more Leader in Me schools and three or more comparison schools in the view contributes to the pooled result, which is the same floor the state pages use for any group. The sidebar can narrow this to the 31 states whose own page runs a full statistical test (twenty or more Leader in Me schools on the headline), or to any set of states you pick. A state's row in the forest table is tested only when its own page is a full page; the 16 descriptive-page states (Connecticut, District of Columbia, Delaware, Hawaii, Idaho, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Oregon, South Dakota, Vermont, West Virginia, Wyoming) are shown as described, not tested, exactly as their pages word them, and the 4 territory pages (Alaska, Maine, North Dakota, Rhode Island) carry no Leader in Me group to pool.

We checked whether the Leader in Me schools in small states differ from the rest (record: Build/national/analysis/results.json, note national-state-inclusion-2026-09-09.md). Adding them moves the pooled effect by less than 0.01 of a standard deviation, they carry about six percent of the weight, and their pooled effect is not distinguishable from the larger states'. Inside their states they are the Title I schools and start lower; the within-state comparison already accounts for that, which is one more reason levels are never pooled here.

State counts in pooled results

All 51 state pages were pulled from the same national export and built the same way, and every one of them feeds this page. When a sentence here says "across 46 states" or "across 32 states", the states left out are the ones with no Leader in Me schools, or fewer than three with results at both ends of that window on that measure (or fewer than three comparison schools), so there is nothing in them to compare. The note under the headline names those states every time, and the state table carries a row for each of them saying so. It is never a missing pull. Graduation rate, for example, is carried by fewer states because only high schools have one and several states have fewer than three Leader in Me high schools.

Event-study design across all years

The card on the first tab is an event study: every Leader in Me school is lined up on its own start year and its change from its last pre-start year is compared, inside its own state, with the change over the same calendar years in the schools of that state that never joined. A cell is one state, one start cohort and one number of years since the start; cells need three or more starting schools and ten or more never-joined schools; the effect at each year since the start is the average of the cells weighted by the number of starting schools, and the standard errors are cluster-robust by school (a never-joined school appears in many cells), which also gives the joint test of whether our schools were already on a different path in the years before they started. Reading and math proficiency is the declared primary outcome; the seven outcomes' post-start averages are adjusted together. The hollow series is the same analysis on the schools still in the process at year six or seven, a check that the later years and the early years are read on the same schools; it was added after seeing the first run and is reported as a check, not a result. The textbook two-way fixed-effects regression (school and state-by-year effects, one Leader in Me indicator) is on the technical line, with the known caution that it mixes early and late cohorts. Schools that leave the process drop out of the later cells, so the later years describe schools that stayed. 2019-20 and 2020-21 are left out; the 2022 cohort's base year is 2018-19, so a sensitivity without that cohort is on the technical line too.

This build: post-start average on reading and math proficiency -0.09 points (-0.34 to +0.17), p = 0.513, adjusted 0.60 across 7 outcomes; pre-start path test p = 0.05; 43 states, 1122 cells.

Stage one: within-state effect estimation

  • One school, one observation. A school's reading and math rows are averaged into one value per year before anything is counted; a combined-grade school is one school here (the state pages let you split it by band; this page does not yet).
  • A school counts as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before (a launch from April 1 through September 15 makes that coming school year its Year 1; a launch from September 16 through March 31 makes that school year its Year 0, shown on the state pages and counted in neither group here, with Year 1 the year after), and exits are dated from the contract expiration, exactly as the state pages count them; this page reads those flags from the state pages and never re-decides them.
  • Balanced panels only: a school contributes to a window only with a value at both ends. The group is the school's status at the end of the window, as on the state pages.
  • The effect is Hedges' g on the change scores (the Leader in Me schools' mean change minus the comparison schools' mean change, over the pooled standard deviation of changes, with the small-sample correction), oriented so that positive always favors Leader in Me. The plain-points difference is kept for wording only.
  • A state contributes only with three or more schools on each side and some spread in the changes.

Stage two: random-effects pooling across states

  • Random effects (states genuinely differ), between-state variance by DerSimonian-Laird, inverse-variance weights.
  • The test and the interval use the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman correction on a t distribution with k − 1 degrees of freedom, in the truncated form so the interval can never be narrower than the plain random-effects one. The ordinary z result is on the technical line as a sensitivity.
  • Heterogeneity is reported as I². Above 75 percent the page says the states differ too much to quote one number and points at the forest table.
  • The measures strip declares reading and math proficiency the primary outcome. Its p-value, chronic absence's and graduation's are adjusted together by Benjamini-Hochberg, so scanning several measures and quoting the best one is not possible by accident; reading alone and math alone are components of the primary outcome and carry no adjustment. A measure that clears .05 on its own but not after adjustment is worded as a strong lead, not a proven finding.
  • The verdict is one tier: p at or below .05. Fewer than two contributing states: described, not pooled.

Standing relative to the state average, by year

The position view scores every Leader in Me school against its own state's schools in the same year (a z-score on that state-year's mean and spread across all schools), then averages the schools in view. Zero is the state's average school. Because each year is scored against that year's state, this view can run across the COVID gap; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are left out entirely, never interpolated.

The findings scan and adjustment for multiple comparisons

The findings tab lists results that favor Leader in Me from a scan written before it was run (Build/national/findings_scan.py). Every measure the state pages carry (the core measures and every state measure three or more states share as a concept), every window, every sub-population (Title I, high poverty, high minority, elementary, middle, high) and every design (all comparison schools, matched pairs, the share of schools improving, schools weighted by enrollment, the within-state-year relative scale, years in the process) is run once and every result is kept in analysis/findings_scan.csv. Each family of tests carries its own Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment. A finding is a favorable result whose adjusted p is at or below .05 with I² at or below 75; a lead clears .05 on its own but not after adjustment and is listed separately so it can be tested on the next year's data rather than quoted. The tab shows the family and its size beside every line. Results that run the other way are recorded in the scan file and on the Field insights tab, not on the findings tab. The MRA is not part of the scan.

This build: 332 tests in 8 families; 20 clear .05 on their own (15 in Leader in Me schools' favor), against about 16.6 that chance alone would produce; 0 hold after adjustment and 15 are leads.

One caution on the relative scale: a z-score against the state's spread moves when the spread moves, and the spread of chronic absence roughly doubled in 2021-22, so a school that sat below its state's average rose toward it mechanically. The relative windows are reported for completeness and are not read as effects on their own.

MRA analyses

The MRA is one instrument everywhere (IRT scale points built so the average school lands at 75), so Leader in Me schools can be averaged across states directly. One survey per school and year (spring first), the current instrument only (2021-22 on), exact 100.0 values already removed as sentinels by the state builds. The same-schools change is each school's own first-to-latest move, tested with a paired t test on ten or more schools.

Analyses not yet included

  • Matched pairs: the state pages' matched comparison is built per state; pooling the matched views is the right sensitivity analysis and is queued.
  • The full-history relative window (each school against its state each year, 2014-15 on) is shown as the position view but not pooled as an effect; the pooling contract leaves that to a design decision.
  • Grade-band as the unit, look-alike mode, the school list, the research pack and the district cards stay on the state pages.
  • The states' own measures (report-card grades, discipline, climate) are not pooled; they are on the state pages that carry them.
