Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results count as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before. A launch from April 1 through September 15 starts that coming school year, which is Year 1. A launch from September 16 through March 31 comes after the year was underway: that year is Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and the next year is Year 1. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day. An August 2023 launch counts from 2023-24; an October 2024 launch has 2024-25 as its Year 0 and counts from 2025-26.
  • A school's baseline is its last spring test before launch. A Leader in Me school's years before its launch count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time; its Year 0 never does.
  • 36 school-years on this page are a Year 0.
  • 0 Oklahoma schools on the roster carry no start date. Their launch cannot be placed, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 06, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 29 of the 106 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 7 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Oklahoma's own files join by the state's county-district-site code through the NCES directory (no code is claimed by two federal ids; a renumbered school joins under every code it held), audited against the state's own school names and against DOMO's chronic absence; the State Regents' reports join by high-school name within county. A school the directory does not carry keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Oklahoma measures.
  • The report card's overall score and academic growth indicator are whole-school scores carried on each band a school reports; the growth value table is fixed across years. The letter grade is the share of schools graded A or B on the card. No school was graded in 2019-20 or 2020-21, and those years stay off every card and row.
  • The report card's chronic absenteeism, the same number as DOMO's, fills the core's chronic-absence column only where DOMO has none; the report card's graduation rate is a class behind and fills the core's graduation column on the class's own year only where DOMO has none (2016-17 excepted, DOMO's own source differing that year). No DOMO value is overwritten.
  • Teacher retention (OEIP) is a whole-school rate on each band; the Regents' direct college-going rate sits on the secondary band on the class's own year; the closure classes of 2020 and 2021 stay off. Freshman GPA and developmental-course shares are card and pack values, never rows; the 2024 developmental report (a redesign) is excluded by name.

Judgement calls we had to make

The places the rules needed a threshold. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

Launch year (Year 0 and Year 1)
September 15 (decided October 6, 2026). A school that launched from April 1 through September 15 counts that coming school year as its Year 1; its baseline is the spring test before launch. A school that launched from September 16 through March 31 has that school year as its Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and counts from the next school year. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day (most of the Middle Atlantic and, by law, most of Minnesota). The line replaced July 1, which put June and August launches for the same school year a full year apart.
School year boundary for exits
July 1. An expiration in June belongs to the year that just ended; one in July belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 24 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 0 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 38% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 13 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 11 pairs (0 unmatched).

1 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Emerson Alternative Education (Hs).

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 50,850 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 100.0% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 1.1% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.1% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.1% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 84.7% of rows; the years and subjects that do not add up (2022-23 Social Studies, 2023-24 Social Studies, 2024-25 Social Studies) carry no level values.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Oklahoma's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 84.7% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values: 2022-23 Social Studies, 2023-24 Social Studies, 2024-25 Social Studies), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard (Below Basic, Basic, Proficient, Advanced in Oklahoma's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Oklahoma that is 1,216 Title I schools, 78 charter schools, 0 magnet schools and 1 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Oklahoma's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Social Studies: Geography, Social Studies, U.S. History; ELA: English Language Arts, English Language Arts I, English Language Arts II, English Language Arts III, Reading, Reading Language Arts; Math: Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Math, Mathematics; Science: Biology I, Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Oklahoma publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 106 Leader in Me schools and 22 of 1,738 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 1 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 32.1% of Leader in Me schools and 40.0% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 99 of the 106 roster schools carry a date, and 29 were past it by more than 30 days on October 06, 2026. Exits by year: 2021: 4, 2022: 11, 2023: 2, 2024: 5, 2025: 4, 2026: 3. 7 roster schools have no expiration date (7 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 7 schools the headline reads Leader in Me -2.78 against comparison -2.27 (p = 0.527, 56 vs 745 schools), and with them -2.79 against -2.27 (p = 0.499, 60 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2015-16 to 2016-17, 72.9 to 41.5 (-31.3); ELA 2022-23 to 2023-24, 34.4 to 44.5 (+10.1); ELA 2023-24 to 2024-25, 44.5 to 28.1 (-16.4); Math 2015-16 to 2016-17, 66.3 to 39.0 (-27.3). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.

And the report card's chronic absenteeism indicator, read back to the share chronically absent and joined through the codes, is DOMO's chronic absence cut to a tenth (DOMO writes 29.97 as 29.9) on 100.0% of 821 school-years in 2021-22, 100.0% of 848 school-years in 2022-23, 100.0% of 976 school-years in 2023-24, 100.0% of 1,359 school-years in 2024-25 (correlation 1.0 or better), which no name join could counterfeit. 243 report-card rows belong to 70 state schools with no DOMO record and stay off the page; 11 state reporting pseudo-sites (OTHER PLACEMENT ES; OTHER PLACEMENT HS; OTHER PLACEMENT MS; State Reporting By Grade Level ES...) are set aside by name. The State Regents' reports carry high-school names under counties, no code, and join by name within county to the names the report card and the directory spell: 5,060 of 5,107 college-going rows (exact 5,018, ambiguous 34, name contained, high-school site 28, generic words removed 14, unmatched 13), 2,706 of 2,723 GPA rows and 3,158 of 3,178 developmental-education rows; the rest are counted, never guessed (DIMENSIONS ACADEMY (Cleveland County); DOVE SCIENCE ACADEMY - TULSA (Tulsa County); HARDING CHARTER PREPARATORY HS (Oklahoma County); HARDING CHARTER PREPARATORY HS (Oklahoma County); NORTHEAST ACAD FOR HEALTH SCI AND ENG (Oklahoma County); OKLAHOMA SCHOOL FOR THE DEAF (Murray County); OKLAHOMA VIRTUAL CHARTER ACADE (Oklahoma County); SANTA FE SOUTH HS (Oklahoma County) and more). As a check of that join, the OEIP dashboard's copy of the Regents' Oklahoma-college rate, keyed on the state code, equals the name-joined figure within half a point on 93.7% of 447 high schools for the class of 2022, 100.0% of 451 high schools for the class of 2023 (the class of 2022 was revised between the Regents' report and the office's copy for 28 high schools, by a few students each, at most 8.33 points).
  • The report card's overall score is a measure; the letter grade is a share. Oklahoma's School Report Card (2017-18, 2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25) scores each school on its indicators and publishes an overall score, 0 to 100, the share of the possible points earned, and an A-F grade. The letter is the state's binning of that score at cut points that differ a little between years and between elementary and high schools (2017-18 elementary and middle B from 56.7, high school B from 60.6; 2018-19 elementary and middle B from 56.9, high school B from 61.1; 2021-22 elementary and middle B from 57.4, high school B from 63.0; 2022-23 elementary and middle B from 61.3, high school B from 63.2; 2023-24 elementary and middle B from 61.4, high school B from 63.1; 2024-25 elementary and middle B from 61.1, high school B from 63.1), so the score is the state's own continuous measure and runs through the same machinery as every other measure on the page (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), carried on each band a school reports as the core carries chronic absence, on 12,365 school-year-bands. Its components differ by school type (achievement, growth, chronic absenteeism and English learner progress for elementary and middle schools; achievement, graduation, postsecondary opportunities, chronic absenteeism and English learner progress for high schools), so a school's score is comparable with its own across years and with schools of its kind within a year, which is how the state uses it. The letter sits on the card as the share of schools graded A or B and in the research pack. The 2019-20 and 2020-21 files carry one state row and no school: no school was graded in the closure years, and nothing from them is on the page.
  • Growth is on a fixed value table, so the growth score is a measure. The report card's academic growth indicator scores each tested student's move between the eight OSTP performance levels in English language arts and mathematics from one spring to the next on a value table, averages the points, and scales the school's average to 0-100. The A-F growth archives carry, per school and subject, the value-table score and the 8-by-8 matrix of students by prior and current level, and the table rebuilds from those matrices by least squares in every year: on the diagonal 1->1 -1.6 to 1.3, 2->2 89.4 to 90.1, 3->3 93.3 to 96.1, 4->4 93.3 to 97, 5->5 99.2 to 101.1, 6->6 103.4 to 105.8, 7->7 113.4 to 116 and one level up 1->2 119.2 to 121.5, 2->3 128.8 to 131.4, 3->4 129.3 to 131.2, 4->5 127.6 to 132.1, 5->6 129.3 to 131.5, 6->7 131.5 to 137.2 points across 2017-18 to 2024-25, and one table fitted on all six years' matrices together reproduces every year's published scores within a point on 96.7% or more of subject rows, a median error of 0.204 points or less. The composite's mapping from the value-table score held as well: a value-table score of about 60 to 61 to 62 reads 0 and about 111 to 112 to 113 to 114 reads 100 in every year. The archive's composite equals the report card's growth indicator to a hundredth on 100.0% of 7,247 school-years, so the report card is the source and the archive the audit; the ELA and mathematics value-table scores sit in the research pack. The indicator exists only for schools with tested grades 4 to 8 (a student needs a prior-year score), and it runs through the growth test on 9,393 school-year-bands, whole-school on each band the school reports.
  • Chronic absenteeism is DOMO's number, and fills the years DOMO lacks. The report card publishes chronic absenteeism as the share of students not chronically absent (absent 10% or more of enrolled days); read back to the share chronically absent it is DOMO's chronic absence to the tenth DOMO keeps, on every school-year both carry (2021-22: 100.0% of 821 within a tenth, statewide 15.39% against DOMO's 15.35%; 2022-23: 100.0% of 848 within a tenth, statewide 14.99% against DOMO's 14.95%; 2023-24: 100.0% of 976 within a tenth, statewide 13.94% against DOMO's 13.9%; 2024-25: 100.0% of 1,359 within a tenth, statewide 15.42% against DOMO's 15.38%), so it is the same number from the same publisher, DOMO's copy cut to a tenth. DOMO carries no Oklahoma chronic absence before 2021-22, and the state's figure (to the hundredth) fills the core's column only where DOMO has none: 5,961 school-years in all (2017-18: 1,661, 2018-19: 1,666, 2021-22: 822, 2022-23: 788, 2023-24: 708, 2024-25: 316), never a school-year DOMO carries (4,004 kept; 29 school-year-bands the state's measures add to the page, with no test rows of their own, take DOMO's own export value first). The statewide rate was 13.5% in 2017-18, 14.0% in 2018-19, 19.5% in 2021-22, 20.0% in 2022-23, 19.0% in 2023-24, 19.1% in 2024-25; the same schools moved together across the closure gap (correlation 0.569 on 1,601 schools from 2018-19 to 2021-22), the pandemic rise, not a change in counting. The card's chronic absenteeism line is the state's own series, 2017-18 on.
  • The graduation rate is a class behind, and the core's column is filled from it. The report card's four-year graduation rate is lagged: the file for a school year carries the class that graduated the spring before. DOMO's own graduation rate shows it (2021-22: 83.5% of 345 equal the file labelled 2022-23 to a hundredth, 1.2% the same-labelled file; 2022-23: 66.8% of 431 equal the file labelled 2023-24 to a hundredth, 1.6% the same-labelled file; 2023-24: 100.0% of 427 equal the file labelled 2024-25 to a hundredth, 2.6% the same-labelled file), so each rate sits on the class's own year, DOMO's convention. Where DOMO's values match the file for fewer schools (2016-17), DOMO's column is another source that year, and DOMO's value always stands. The core's graduation column is filled only where DOMO has no value: class of 2018 from the 2018-19 file (444 school-years DOMO lacks; DOMO's own value kept on 0); class of 2022 from the 2022-23 file (101 school-years DOMO lacks; DOMO's own value kept on 345, and laid first on 1 added rows); class of 2023 from the 2023-24 file (26 school-years DOMO lacks; DOMO's own value kept on 431, and laid first on 2 added rows); class of 2024 from the 2024-25 file (32 school-years DOMO lacks; DOMO's own value kept on 427, and laid first on 3 added rows). 2016-17 is not filled at all, because DOMO's own values that year equal the file labelled a year later for only 27.6% of schools, a different source that would sit beside the report card's figure in one year; the class of 2021 falls in the closure years and is off the page. The five- and six-year rates are in the research pack on the class year. No DOMO value is overwritten.
  • Teacher retention and attendance come from the OEQA's dashboard, two years only. The Office of Educational Quality and Accountability publishes an OEIP dashboard workbook per school year, and only two exist (2022-23, 2023-24); no school-level teacher retention or attendance file exists for the earlier years. Per school the workbooks carry the share of teachers who stayed, moved within the district, moved elsewhere in the state or left Oklahoma public schools (the four shares sum to 100 on every row), their experience, the average daily attendance rate by grade, the grade 7-12 dropout rate, and the office's copies of the Regents' college-going, GPA and remediation figures and the ACT composite. They key on the same state code, and reach 1,769 of 1,770 schools (102 Leader in Me). The office withholds schools under six teacher FTE; 5 values of exactly zero are kept as published (a school whose whole staff turned over). Teacher retention is a per-school rate, so it runs through the growth test on 3,893 school-year-bands, two years wide; the attendance rate sits on the card and in the research pack.
  • College-going is the Regents' count, Oklahoma colleges only, joined by name within county. The State Regents' High School Indicators reports list, for every public high school by county, the graduates of a class and how many entered an Oklahoma public or private college the following fall (direct) or any time in the following academic year, from the Regents' own enrollment records; 11 classes, 2014 to 2024, and the published share rebuilds from the counts on every row. A graduate who enrolled outside Oklahoma is not counted, so the rate understates college-going for schools near the state line; the OEIP's 2022 and 2023 classes add an out-of-state share from a name and birth-date match, kept in the research pack. The 2018-19 workbook's school sheet still carries the 2017 column header from the year before; its title and its district sheet say the class of 2018 and its counts are not the 2017 file's (8.3% identical, chance), so it is read as the class of 2018. The reports carry no code, so they join by name within county (exact 5,018, ambiguous 34, name contained, high-school site 28, generic words removed 14, unmatched 13), and the OEIP's coded copy of the same rate agrees within half a point on 93.7% of 447, 100.0% of 451 high schools. The direct rate is a per-school rate on the class's own year, so it runs through the growth test on the secondary band (3,645 school-years, 6 Leader in Me high schools ever while counted); the classes of 2020 and 2021 are closure classes and stay off every card and row. The 2022-23 report card's own postsecondary enrollment archive, the one year of that archive with content, is in the research pack on the class year.
  • Freshman GPA and developmental courses describe the graduates who went to college. The Regents also report, per high school, the freshman-year GPA of the class's graduates in Oklahoma public colleges and the share enrolled in a developmental course (classes of 2018 to 2023 for GPA, 2018 to 2023 for developmental education). Both describe the graduates who enrolled, not the class, so they sit on the card and in the research pack and never on the rows. Schools with five or fewer freshmen are withheld (561 school-classes in the GPA reports, 537 in the developmental reports), read as missing. The class of 2024 GPA workbook (2024-HSIP-GPA.xlsx) is published by district only and is not read. The class of 2024 developmental report counts developmental and co-requisite enrollment apart under a changed first-time-student rule (the Regents' own note), so its 455 rows are a different measure, excluded by name.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 110 surveys across 60 Oklahoma schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 26 answered by more than one group (blended), 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (60 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 2 of the 75 schools counted as Leader in Me in Oklahoma meet the criteria, and a further 1 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2024-25, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 7 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Oklahoma publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • The report card's overall score is a composite whose components differ between elementary and high schools, so a primary-band and a secondary-band score are the state's two recipes, comparable with their own kind.
  • The academic growth indicator exists only for schools with tested grades 4 to 8, so high schools and K-2 schools carry no growth score.
  • Oklahoma publishes school-level teacher retention and attendance for two years only (2022-23 and 2023-24), so those measures are levels and a one-step change, not a trend.
  • The Regents' college-going rate counts enrollment in Oklahoma colleges only; a graduate who left the state is not counted, and the statewide rate fell by a quarter across the classes of 2014 to 2022, so a school's own direction is read against that tide.
  • The freshman GPA and developmental-course shares describe the graduates who enrolled in an Oklahoma public college, a self-selected group, and are withheld for schools with five or fewer freshmen.
