Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results count as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before. A launch from April 1 through September 15 starts that coming school year, which is Year 1. A launch from September 16 through March 31 comes after the year was underway: that year is Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and the next year is Year 1. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day. An August 2023 launch counts from 2023-24; an October 2024 launch has 2024-25 as its Year 0 and counts from 2025-26.
  • A school's baseline is its last spring test before launch. A Leader in Me school's years before its launch count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time; its Year 0 never does.
  • 17 school-years on this page are a Year 0.
  • 9 New Jersey schools on the roster carry no start date. Their launch cannot be placed, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 06, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 55 of the 103 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 12 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • New Jersey's own measures join through the county-district-school code NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against the state files' own school names and against DOMO's chronic absence and graduation rate (the state's own values); a school with no state code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no New Jersey measures.
  • The state's chronic absenteeism rate and four-year graduation rate fill chronic absence and graduation only where DOMO has no value, having been checked as the same measure on the school-years both carry; no DOMO value is overwritten. Where the state restated a figure in a later database, the report of the year's own class or school year stands.
  • The two growth medians and the two suspension rates are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports; the dropout rate sits on the secondary band. State suppression marks are read as missing, never zero; a suspension share above 100 and a faculty attendance of exactly zero are set aside.
  • The 2021-22 Relative School Improvement Measure is a different measure from the growth percentile and stays out of the growth series; the 2016-17 SAT participation and dual enrollment columns and the recut days-absent bands from 2023-24 are excluded by name; 2019-20 and 2020-21 stay off every New Jersey card, row and pack value, and the 2020-21 database is not read.
  • Postsecondary enrollment from the class of 2023 is the lower bound of the state's published range, the share of graduates matched to an enrollment, the same definition as the earlier exact shares.

Judgement calls we had to make

The places the rules needed a threshold. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

Launch year (Year 0 and Year 1)
September 15 (decided October 6, 2026). A school that launched from April 1 through September 15 counts that coming school year as its Year 1; its baseline is the spring test before launch. A school that launched from September 16 through March 31 has that school year as its Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and counts from the next school year. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day (most of the Middle Atlantic and, by law, most of Minnesota). The line replaced July 1, which put June and August launches for the same school year a full year apart.
School year boundary for exits
July 1. An expiration in June belongs to the year that just ended; one in July belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 43 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 2 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 49% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 28 pairs (2 unmatched); Secondary: 15 pairs (0 unmatched).

4 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Middlesex County Vocational School East Bruns. School of Career Develop, Newbury Early Learning Center, Somerset County Vocational Technical High School, Warren County Vocational Technical School.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 159,842 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 8.7% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 2.7% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 1.9% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.8% of rows. Read as no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 0.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. New Jersey's file reports achievement levels that cannot stand as measures here: no combination of the reported levels reproduces PercentMetStandard on more than 9% of rows (a five-level state whose export carries four columns, usually) (no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate; levels add up on 0.0% of rows). The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In New Jersey that is 1,629 Title I schools, 102 charter schools, 0 magnet schools and 9 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in New Jersey's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English Language Arts; Math: Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Mathematics; Science: Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; New Jersey publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 103 Leader in Me schools and 16 of 2,199 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 1 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 39.8% of Leader in Me schools and 17.2% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 91 of the 103 roster schools carry a date, and 55 were past it by more than 30 days on October 06, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 1, 2020: 2, 2022: 3, 2023: 10, 2024: 9, 2025: 10, 2026: 20. 12 roster schools have no expiration date (9 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 9 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 6.54 against comparison 6.23 (p = 0.797, 37 vs 1992 schools), and with them 7.51 against 6.23 (p = 0.263, 45 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.

The 2019-20 database carries no chronic absenteeism and 2020-21 is not read; the state's own rate is in the research pack for every year as chronic_nj.
  • Student growth is a measure, not only a level, and 2021-22 is a different one. New Jersey's median student growth percentile ranks each grade 4-8 student against students statewide with the same prior score and takes the school's median; the reports carry it for 2014-15 to 2024-25 except 2019-20 and 2020-21 (no state test) and 2021-22, when the state published a Relative School Improvement Measure instead, a comparison of the school's scale-score change against a group of similar schools, which is a different measure: it sits in the research pack under its own name (rsim_ela, rsim_math) and never in the growth line. The Department's own growth workbooks cross-tabulate the same medians by student group; their all-students school rows equal the report databases' values on 100.0% of 13,811 school-years (2018-19, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25), so the database value stands and the workbooks are the check. Each year's medians are restated in the two following databases; the restatements agree within 0.15 on 10,368 of 10,368 school-years. Both medians are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence, and run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test); reading growth reaches 64 Leader in Me schools while counted. The state withholds a school's median where fewer than ten students have a growth score.
  • Suspension is a measure; the incidents card is a count per 100 students. The reports carry the share of a school's students given at least one suspension of any kind from 2014-15 and, from 2016-17, in-school and out-of-school suspension separately; from 2017-18 they add removals to another placement, expulsions and arrests. Any suspension and out-of-school suspension are per-school rates and join the rows on each band the school reports; out-of-school suspension reaches 81 Leader in Me schools while counted and names the behavior measure for look-alike mode. 20 suspension shares above 100 (pct_oss 9, pct_susp 11; a count printed where a rate belongs) are set aside. The state withholds small counts with an asterisk, read as missing, never zero. The incidents card is different: New Jersey publishes the count of violence, vandalism, weapons, substance and harassment, intimidation and bullying incidents each school reported to the state, with its own per-100 rate, which rebuilds from the count and the school's enrollment within 0.1 on 100.0% of 14,836 school-years; the card pools each group's incidents over its enrollment, 2016-17 to 2024-25 with the closure years out. Incidents are self-reported and a school's own direction is firmer ground than the gap between groups.
  • What each database carries, and what is set aside by name. Eleven School Performance Reports databases were pulled, 2014-15 to 2024-25; ten are read. The 2020-21 workbook is not: every value it carries describes a closure year or restates an earlier file. The 2019-20 workbook is read only for the values that describe other years (the class of 2019's 16-month college enrollment and restated growth and graduation). Across the files, 34,074 all-students cells carry one of the state's marks instead of a number (N 15,819, * 11,716, blank 1,815, S 1,572, n/a 773), each read as missing: an asterisk or S is the state's small-count suppression, N a measure the school does not have. 111 faculty attendance values of exactly zero are read as unreported. Excluded by name: SAT participation for 2016-17 (the 2016-17 SAT participation column runs thirty points above the years either side of it (a different base)); dual enrollment for 2016-17 (the 2016-17 dual enrollment column is published for a third of the high schools at three times the level of the years either side (a different base)); the 2014-15 and 2015-16 postsecondary tables, whose window the layout does not state; the days-absent bands from 2023-24, which the state recut as shares of days enrolled rather than days absent (carried in the pack as absent_20pct_plus, never on the more-than-15-days line); and the district-level teacher retention table, which the state publishes for districts, not schools. From the class of 2023 the state publishes postsecondary enrollment as a range whose lower bound is the share of graduates the National Student Clearinghouse matched, the same definition as the exact shares of the earlier classes: the lower bound is carried (9,913 range cells). The trend workbooks restate the earlier classes as ranges too, and their lower bounds sit within a point of the databases' exact shares on 72.1% and within two points on 85.9% of 2,040 school-classes (a median 0.4 points apart: the state re-ran the Clearinghouse match), so the database of the class's own year stands and the trend workbooks only add the classes it lacks. The accountability summative score is published for 2016-17, 2017-18 and 2021-22 on (none for 2018-19 to 2020-21); the 2023-24 workbook marks a modified score with a dagger, which is stripped. New Jersey's Leader in Me schools are almost all elementary and middle schools, so the high-school measures (dropout, college enrollment, SAT, ACT, AP and IB, dual enrollment) reach only a few of them.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 82 surveys across 42 New Jersey schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (42 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 7 of the 49 schools counted as Leader in Me in New Jersey meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2024-25, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 12 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; New Jersey publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • New Jersey's growth percentile covers students in grades 4-8 with a prior-year score, so a K-3 school or a high school carries no growth median, and a school's median rests on the students who tested in both years.
  • The state withholds a school's suspension count where it is small, so the smallest schools are missing on the suspension lines rather than zero, and a share above 100 in a few school-years is a count printed where a rate belongs.
  • Reported incidents are what schools reported to the state's system; reporting practice differs between schools, so a school's own direction over time is firmer ground than the gap between groups.
  • The accountability summative score is a percentile-based index against schools of the same configuration, not a rate; it is a level, described, never tested, and was not published for 2018-19 to 2020-21.
  • New Jersey's Leader in Me schools are almost all elementary and middle schools, so the high-school measures (dropout, college enrollment, SAT, ACT, AP, IB and dual enrollment) describe a handful of them and sit mostly in the research pack.
