Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 2 Arkansas schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 31 of the 74 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 6 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • 1 roster schools also appear in the comparison export in years after their roster rows end, which is DOMO's own record that they left; they count as Leader in Me through their last roster year and in neither group after.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Arkansas's own measures join through the state LEA code NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, followed across renumberings outside NCES's window by name within district and audited against a name-within-district join and DOMO's own graduation rate; a school with no state LEA code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Arkansas measures.
  • Attendance is the year's average daily attendance over its average daily membership; a rate at or above 99.9% is read as unreported. Suspensions are actions per 100 students on the state's own average daily membership for the same year (DOMO's enrollment where the attendance file has no row); a school-year left blank is unreported, never zero, and a rate on fewer than 20 students is not drawn.
  • The Workforce Stability Index is the state's 2017-2021 index; the 2022-23 file's percentile-rank index is carried under its own name. An attrition share above 100% is read as unreported with the index built on it.
  • The content growth score is the growth line and row measure; the 2016-17 value-added column is excluded by name. The card's letter-grade line is the ESSA-era grades only; the 2013-14, 2014-15 and 2024-25 grades sit in the research pack under their own schemes.
  • The state's four-year graduation rate is filed on its class's year and fills only the years DOMO lacks, on the secondary band; it never overwrites a DOMO value. 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Arkansas card and row.
  • 2025-26 results for English language arts and reading in grades 3-10, math and science in grades 3-8, Algebra I in grades 7-10, Geometry in grades 8-11 and Biology in grades 9-12 come from Arkansas DESE's ATLAS workbook; proficiency is ATLAS levels 3 and 4, as in prior years. A masked share is used only where the state's own published shares and counts fix it exactly; shares published only as a range, and masked cells nothing published fixes, stay blank.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 29 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 1 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 48% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 23 pairs (1 unmatched); Secondary: 6 pairs (0 unmatched).

3 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Arkansas School For The Blind Elementary, Arkansas School For The Blind High School, Belle Point Alternative Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 125,346 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 95.3% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.7% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.5% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.3% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 90.3% of rows; the years and subjects that do not add up (2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math (end-of-course), 2014-15 Math) carry no level values.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Arkansas's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 90.3% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values: 2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math (end-of-course), 2014-15 Math), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard. The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Arkansas that is 840 Title I schools, 129 charter schools, 33 magnet schools and 5 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Arkansas's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English, English Language Arts, Reading; Math: Algebra, Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Math, Mathematics; Science: Biology, Science; Writing: Writing.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Arkansas publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 21 of 74 Leader in Me schools and 237 of 1,066 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 5 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 51.4% of Leader in Me schools and 41.4% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 68 of the 74 roster schools carry a date, and 31 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2021: 7, 2022: 7, 2023: 3, 2024: 4, 2025: 5, 2026: 5. 6 roster schools have no expiration date (5 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 5 schools the headline reads Leader in Me -4.47 against comparison -4.76 (p = 0.869, 36 vs 806 schools), and with them -4.64 against -4.76 (p = 0.946, 40 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2014-15 to 2015-16, 32.0 to 52.5 (+20.5); ELA 2022-23 to 2023-24, 49.8 to 32.8 (-17.0); Math 2014-15 to 2015-16, 23.5 to 43.4 (+19.9). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Joining Arkansas's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Arkansas's files key on the state's seven-digit LEA code instead (a four-digit district and a three-digit school number: DeWitt Elementary is 0101001), which shares no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 1,140 id pairs for 1,115 of the 1,140 schools on this page, including 74 of 74 Leader in Me schools and 1,065 of 1,066 comparison schools. NCES's directory in this build runs 2016-17 to 2023-24; a school renumbered outside that window (a district-conversion charter takes a 7xx school number and gives it back when the charter ends) is followed by name within its four-digit district, 31 of them, among them one Leader in Me school (Fountain Lake Middle School: 2602007 in 2011 to 2014, 2602008 in 2025 to 2026 beside NCES's 2602702). 24 schools NCES never listed joined by an exact name within district. The map was audited a second way: a name-within-district join from DOMO's school name and the page's district label to the state files' own names speaks for 1,086 of the NCES-joined schools and agrees on 1,086. The state files' own name agrees with DOMO's on 1,181 of 1,195 crosswalked ids. An independent value check from a file the map was not built from: the state's own four-year graduation rate against DOMO's on the same school-years agrees within a point on 98.8% of 170 school-years (r = 0.989).
  • Attendance is the year's own averages, and a perfect rate is unreported. The ADE Data Center reports each school's average daily attendance and membership by quarter with the year-to-date average beside them; the rate here is the year's ADA over the year's ADM (2010-11 to 2025-26, 16,923 school-years, no district rows, no duplicate ids). In 170 school-years a school reported attendance exactly equal to membership in every quarter, and a further 72 sat within a tenth of a point of it; a rate at or above 99.9% is read as unreported (242 school-years). Arkansas publishes no chronic-absence rate in these files and DOMO carries none for the state, so attendance stands as the page's attendance measure and nothing is filled. It is a whole-school rate carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence, and it runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test).
  • Discipline is actions per 100 students on the state's own membership. My School Info reports each school's disciplinary actions and infractions as counts (2013-14 to 2025-26; the same 37 columns in every year; every action answers an infraction, and a school's two totals run within a few percent of each other in every year). Out-of-school suspension is the injury and non-injury columns together, over the state's own average daily membership for the same school and year, so a school whose enrollment moved a third in a decade is not measured against one snapshot; every rated school-year found its membership there, and 8 on fewer than 20 students carry no rate. These are actions, not distinct students: a student suspended three times counts three times, so a rate can pass 100 at a school that suspends often. A school that left the year blank (1,080 school-years, 6 to 11 in a hundred each year) is unreported, never zero. Detention, bus suspension, parent conference, Saturday school and warning are reported only from 2018-19 and are not part of any suspension figure. The closure years stay off every Leader in Me comparison; the research pack keeps the counts and rates for every year.
  • Two Workforce Stability Indexes, not one. My School Info's Workforce Stability Index files carry two different measures under one report name, which the export's group row labels 'Workforce Stability Index (2017-2021)' and 'Workforce Stability Index (2023)'. The 2016-17 to 2020-21 files carry the first: 100 minus the average of a school's shares of inexperienced, provisionally licensed, out-of-field, departing and ineffective teachers, which rebuilds from its components on 5,283 of 5,283 rows. The 2022-23 file carries the second, a percentile rank on experience, attainment, licensure and retention, with the component shares beside it; it sits on the card as teachers retained and in the research pack under its own name, never on the 2017-2021 line. 16 attrition shares above 100% (a small school's departures counted against a smaller staff) are read as unreported along with the index built on them. In 2020-21 the inexperienced share reads about twice the earlier years' and the zero-attrition count collapses, a definition shift in a closure year that is off every comparison anyway. No file covers 2021-22, 2023-24 or later.
  • Three grading schemes, one growth line, and a lagged graduation rate. The Office for Education Policy reposts the state's school-level accountability files in a different layout each year. Letter grades exist under three schemes: the points-based A-F of 2013-14 and 2014-15, the ESSA-index grades of 2016-17 to 2022-23 (the same cut scores by school level in every year), and the LEARNS A-F of 2024-25; no grades were issued for 2015-16, the closure years or 2023-24. The card's grade line is drawn on the ESSA-era grades only, and the pack carries each scheme under its own name. The ESSA School Index runs 2016-17, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23. Growth comes in two scores on the same scale: the value-added score that folds in English learners' language growth and the content growth score for reading and math alone; where a file carries both they correlate at 0.943 or better but differ by more than a point at 8.4% to 14.7% of schools. The content score is the line and the row measure (2017-18, 2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25); the 2016-17 file carries only the value-added score, a different measure, so it is excluded from that line by name and sits in the pack, as does the 2015-16 school growth score from the recognition file (identical in the two files that carry it, 1037 of 1037 rows). Each file's four-year graduation rate is the prior class's, as the state's index uses it: DOMO's own 2018 rate matches the 2018-19 file's on 98.8% of 170 schools within a point, so every rate is filed on its class's year. DOMO carries no graduation rate for Arkansas before 2018; the state's rate fills 2014-15, 2015-16, 2016-17 on the secondary band (295 schools) and never overwrites a DOMO value. Ids with a leading zero are stored as numbers in these workbooks and are padded back to seven digits.
  • Arkansas's own measures are measures, not only levels. Attendance, the two suspension rates, the Workforce Stability Index, the ESSA index and the content growth score are per-school rates, so they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), each carried on every band a school reports. The card shows the same measures as statewide levels by group, with bullying infractions, corporal punishment, teacher attrition, teachers retained and the A-or-B share beside them; group values are school means with a three-school minimum, described, never significance-tested, and the roster growing every year means composition as much as practice.
  • 2025-26 results come from Arkansas's own ATLAS results, for every test DOMO carries for Arkansas, and extend each subject to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Arkansas. The 2025-26 rows (7,556 school-grade-test rows for 956 schools) were read from the Arkansas DESE ATLAS 2026 school-by-grade workbook and joined to the DOMO id through the NCES directory's Arkansas LEA code. They cover English language arts and reading in grades 3-10, math and science in grades 3-8, Algebra I in grades 7-10, Geometry in grades 8-11 and Biology in grades 9-12, each under the name DOMO uses for that test in earlier years. Proficiency is levels 3 and 4 (Proficient and above), as in every prior year, and 2026 is the third year of ATLAS on the same scale. Where the state masked a school's proficient share to protect a small group of students, the share was filled in only where the state's own published shares and counts in the same row fix it exactly (2,755 rows), at the value the state's rounding gives. Shares the state reported only as a range (below 5% or above 95%), masked shares the published figures do not fix exactly, and grades with fewer than ten students tested carry no value.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 66 surveys across 41 Arkansas schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (41 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 0 of the 45 schools counted as Leader in Me in Arkansas meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

67 measures were screened, grouped into 19 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 6 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Arkansas publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Arkansas's suspension figures are counts of actions, not of students, so a school's rate cannot separate one student suspended five times from five students suspended once, and schools differ in what they record; a school's own direction over time is firmer ground than a gap between schools.
  • The Workforce Stability Index changed construction between 2020-21 and 2022-23 and no file covers 2021-22, so the staffing lines run 2016-17 to 2018-19 on one index and 2022-23 on another, and the two are never joined.
  • Arkansas has graded schools under three schemes since 2013-14 and issued none in 2015-16 or 2023-24, so the grade line is short and the growth score, the one measure the state has published on one scale since 2017-18, is the firmer academic read.
  • The attendance and discipline files already cover 2025-26, a year ahead of the test-score exports, so those lines run one year further than everything else on the page, on each school's status read by the core's own start and exit rules; the state marks 2025-26 as the most recent completed year, which may still be revised.
  • The state's own file for the newest year does not yet carry every test DOMO carried the year before (2025-26 11th Grade Algebra (1 rows the year before); 2025-26 12th Grade Geometry (3 rows the year before); 2025-26 8th Grade Biology (2 rows the year before)). Those results usually come out later in the fall. So a school is never compared on one set of tests against a different set, a school that took one of those tests the year before has its newest-year results in that grade band and subject left out (16 rows at 5 schools) until the missing results are released and folded in; the rest of its results, and every earlier year, are unchanged.
  • DOMO carries no chronic absence for any Arkansas school in these exports, so that measure is not offered on this page.
