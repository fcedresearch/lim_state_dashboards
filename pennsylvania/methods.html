Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results count as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before. A launch from April 1 through September 15 starts that coming school year, which is Year 1. A launch from September 16 through March 31 comes after the year was underway: that year is Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and the next year is Year 1. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day. An August 2023 launch counts from 2023-24; an October 2024 launch has 2024-25 as its Year 0 and counts from 2025-26.
  • A school's baseline is its last spring test before launch. A Leader in Me school's years before its launch count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time; its Year 0 never does.
  • 16 school-years on this page are a Year 0.
  • 5 Pennsylvania schools on the roster carry no start date. Their launch cannot be placed, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 06, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 41 of the 134 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 1 inside the grace window stay active; 26 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Pennsylvania's own measures join through the AUN and school number NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against the state files' own school names and districts and against DOMO's graduation rate (the state's own value to the decimal); a school with no state id keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Pennsylvania measures.
  • Every Pennsylvania value is keyed on the school year it describes: the index prints attendance and graduation one year in arrears and the postsecondary shares two, which is undone before anything is joined.
  • Chronic absence is 100 minus the state's regular attendance rate for every year, because the DOMO records carry none for Pennsylvania; the graduation rate is DOMO's own where DOMO has it and the state's index fills the school-years DOMO lacks. No DOMO value is overwritten.
  • The 2019-20 file's assessment, growth, career and course columns repeat 2018-19 exactly and are excluded by name; only that file's attendance, graduation and postsecondary values (new data, describing 2018-19) are read. 2019-20 and 2020-21 stay off every Pennsylvania card, row and pack value.
  • The 2024-25 columns re-based on 12th graders (rigorous courses, industry-based learning, AP/IB, dual enrollment) are different measures, excluded by name and kept in the research pack under their own keys; the 2024-25 science columns are under the state's waiver and its chronic-absenteeism columns are empty shells. State suppression marks are read as missing, never zero.
  • The PVAAS growth scores and the career standards benchmark are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports; the postsecondary transition share and rigorous courses of study sit on the secondary band. The current CSI, ATSI and TSI lists are shown against 2024-25.

Judgement calls we had to make

The places the rules needed a threshold. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

Launch year (Year 0 and Year 1)
September 15 (decided October 6, 2026). A school that launched from April 1 through September 15 counts that coming school year as its Year 1; its baseline is the spring test before launch. A school that launched from September 16 through March 31 has that school year as its Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and counts from the next school year. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day (most of the Middle Atlantic and, by law, most of Minnesota). The line replaced July 1, which put June and August launches for the same school year a full year apart.
School year boundary for exits
July 1. An expiration in June belongs to the year that just ended; one in July belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 64 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 18 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 47% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 44 pairs (13 unmatched); Secondary: 20 pairs (5 unmatched).

9 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Butler County Area Vocational Technical School, Carbon Career & Technical Institute, Columbia-Montour Area Vocational-Technical School, Connellsville Area Career & Technical Center, Dobbins Area Vocational-Technical High School, Jefferson County-DuBois Area Vocational-Technical School, Mastbaum Jules E Area Vocational-Technical School, Middle Years Alternative, Randolph A. Philip Area Vocational-Technical High School.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 220,847 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 2.0% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 2.1% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 100.0% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 0.5% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the top; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 100.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Pennsylvania's file reports four achievement levels, numbered from the top (Pennsylvania's level 1 is the highest). They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 1 and 2 together on 100.0% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values), so each level can stand as a measure: level 4 below the standard; level 3 approaching the standard; level 2 meeting the standard; level 1 exceeding the standard (Below Basic, Basic, Proficient, Advanced in Pennsylvania's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 1 and 2 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 3 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Pennsylvania that is 1,632 Title I schools, 194 charter schools, 51 magnet schools and 0 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Pennsylvania's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Math: Algebra I, Math; ELA: English Language Arts, Literature, Reading; Science: Biology, Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Pennsylvania publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 13 of 134 Leader in Me schools and 646 of 2,777 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 462 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 33.6% of Leader in Me schools and 42.4% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 108 of the 134 roster schools carry a date, and 41 were past it by more than 30 days on October 06, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 1, 2020: 4, 2021: 2, 2022: 10, 2023: 10, 2024: 3, 2025: 6, 2026: 5. 26 roster schools have no expiration date (21 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 21 schools the headline reads Leader in Me -2.02 against comparison -1.45 (p = 0.57, 76 vs 2412 schools), and with them -1.11 against -1.45 (p = 0.692, 94 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Joining Pennsylvania's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Pennsylvania's files key on the district's nine-digit AUN and the school number instead, which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both for every public school (its state school id is PA, the AUN and the school number, one to one in every year from 2016-17 to 2023-24), so that is the anchor: 2,879 id pairs for 2,879 of the 2,911 schools on this page, including 134 of 134 Leader in Me schools and 2,745 of 2,777 comparison schools; 2,795 of them appear in at least one Future Ready PA file (the state writes the school number without leading zeros, which NCES pads to four). The map was checked a second way, against the state files' own school names and districts, which it was not built from: the state's name agrees with DOMO's on 2,787 of 2,795 crosswalked schools and its district on 2,719 of 2,752 with a district in both (8 names read one by one: abbreviations, renamings and schools the state lists under a program name). An independent value check from a column the map was not built from either: the state's four-year cohort graduation rate is DOMO's within 0.15 of a point on 100.0% of 3,265 school-years once the index's one-year lag is undone. 247 schools in the 2024-25 file have no DOMO record on this page (15 of them opened after the last CCD directory in this build), so they can be neither Leader in Me nor comparison schools here.
  • Every Leader in Me school is reached. All 134 Leader in Me schools on this page carry a Pennsylvania school number in NCES's directory, and every one appears in at least one Future Ready PA file.
  • The index reports attendance and graduation a year late, and where graduates are two years late. The Future Ready PA Index file for a school year prints the previous year's regular attendance and four-year cohort graduation rate, and the postsecondary status of the class that graduated two years before. The graduation lag is proven on DOMO's own figures: read as the file labels them, the state's graduation rates match DOMO's within 0.15 on 4.3% of 3,916 school-years (r = 0.863); shifted one year earlier they match on 100.0% of 3,265. The attendance lag is proven on the schools themselves: of the 77 schools NCES first lists in one of five years, 0 carry an attendance value in that year's file and 74 in the next year's, so a school's first year of attendance appears in the file for the year after. Every Pennsylvania value on this page is therefore keyed on the school year it describes, not on the file that printed it, which is why attendance and graduation end at 2023-24 and the postsecondary shares at 2022-23, and why the 2019-20 file still contributes its (real) 2018-19 attendance and graduation values.
  • Chronic absence comes from the state, as the complement of regular attendance; graduation only fills gaps. The DOMO records carry no chronic absence value for any Pennsylvania row (0 values across every row), so chronic absence on this page is 100 minus the state's regular attendance rate (the share of students present 90% or more of their enrolled days, the same 10% line as the federal definition): 16,068 school-years for 2,789 schools on this page, 2016-17 to 2023-24, 59,733 test rows filled plus 727 of the school-year-bands added below for Pennsylvania's own measures. The state's 2024-25 file adds two chronic-absenteeism columns of its own, but they are empty shells (headers with no values in any school), so the complement is the only source. DOMO's graduation rate is the state's cohort rate to the decimal, so the state's file supplies only the school-years DOMO lacks (2,634 secondary rows, nearly all 2016-17, the cohort the 2017-18 file reports) and no DOMO value is overwritten. On regular attendance the Leader in Me line runs from 2016-17 (52 schools) to 2023-24 (83 schools), against 2,558 comparison schools in 2023-24.
  • The 2019-20 file repeats 2018-19, and is read only for what is new in it. Spring 2020 testing was cancelled, and the state's 2019-20 file carries the 2018-19 value, identical school for school, in every assessment-based column, 20 in all: the proficient and advanced shares by subject, the PVAAS growth scores, test participation, grade 3 reading and grade 7 mathematics, the career standards benchmark, industry-based learning, rigorous courses of study, AP/IB and dual enrollment (100.0% identical on 2,753 schools for ELA proficiency, against 1.0% between 2017-18 and 2018-19). Those 40,876 all-students values are excluded by name rather than read twice. The same file's attendance, graduation and postsecondary columns are new values (1.6% identical for attendance) and describe 2018-19, so they are kept. The 2020-21 file carries no growth score at all. Both closure years (52,270 all-students values describing 2019-20 or 2020-21) stay off every card, row and pack value.
  • What the 2024-25 file changes, and what the state suppresses. The 2024-25 file re-bases rigorous courses of study, industry-based learning, AP/IB participation and dual enrollment on 12th graders instead of graduates (the headers say so: Percent12thGraders where every earlier file says PercentGraduates), so those columns are different measures: excluded by name from every series and kept in the research pack under their own keys. Every science column in that file reads Suppress:Waiver, so the science growth and proficiency series end at 2023-24, and its 12 chronic-absenteeism columns are empty shells. Across the eight files, 281,936 all-students cells carry one of the state's marks rather than a number (Suppress:Data Does Not Apply 188,045, Not Applicable 30,138, NA 27,200, IS 22,165, Suppress:Waiver 8,763, Insufficient Sample 4,098), each read as missing, never as zero: IS and Insufficient Sample are the state's small-group suppression, Data Does Not Apply a measure the school does not have (no grade 3, no graduates), See Home District a school whose students are reported at their home district. The career standards benchmark sits at exactly 100 for 27.2% of school-years on this page, and the PVAAS scores pile up at both ends of their 50-to-100 scale (11.3% at 50 and 12.2% at 100 in ELA), which the growth test at the top of the page should be read with. An exact zero on the career standards benchmark is read as unreported rather than as a school where no student met it: it is a submission-based measure, 179 of the 229 schools at zero in some year read 50 or more in their other years, and the largest such cluster is 17 Bethlehem Area SD schools at zero in 2018-19 that average 97.2 in their other years (234 all-students school-years).
  • Growth, career standards, where graduates are and rigorous courses are measures, not only levels. Each is a per-school rate or score, so it runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test): the PVAAS growth scores and the career standards benchmark are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence; the postsecondary transition share and rigorous courses of study sit on the secondary band. the ELA growth score reaches 126 Leader in Me schools, the math growth score reaches 126 Leader in Me schools, the career standards benchmark reaches 120 Leader in Me schools, the postsecondary transition share reaches 11 Leader in Me schools, rigorous courses of study reaches 16 Leader in Me schools. Regular attendance is not offered twice: it reaches the rows as chronic absence, its complement, and sits on the card in the state's own framing. Pennsylvania's files carry no discipline or climate measure, so look-alike mode names no behavior measure for this state. The designation lists are a share of schools on the card and a flag in the pack; the state posts only its current lists, with no prior-year lists on the page, so they cannot be drawn as a series.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 193 surveys across 77 Pennsylvania schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 13 answered by students, 12 answered by staff, 11 answered by more than one group (blended), 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (77 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 14 of the 94 schools counted as Leader in Me in Pennsylvania meet the criteria, and a further 1 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2024-25, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. A school in its third year is measured from its baseline year; a school already in the process is measured from the start of the window.
Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

65 measures were screened, grouped into 18 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 26 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Pennsylvania publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • The Future Ready PA Index reports attendance and graduation one year late and postsecondary status two years late, so the state's newest file adds 2023-24 attendance and graduation and the class of 2023, and 2024-25 waits for the next file.
  • The state suppresses any group with too few students (IS, Insufficient Sample), so the smallest schools and groups are missing rather than zero, and roughly one school in four carries an English learner progress value.
  • The career standards benchmark sits at exactly 100 for a third of schools, and the PVAAS growth score is bounded at 50 and 100 with a share of schools at each end, so both compress growth at the extremes; the graduation rate is not capped.
  • Where graduates are a year on rests on the state's match to college, military and wage records, and misses graduates working out of state or unmatched, so the transition share is a floor.
  • Pennsylvania publishes no summative grade or index score and no discipline or climate measure in the files in this build; the support lists are the state's current ones with no prior-year lists posted, so they are a snapshot, not a series.
