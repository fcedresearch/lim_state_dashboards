Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 0 District of Columbia schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 4 of the 6 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 0 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • The District of Columbia's own measures join through the OSSE LEA and school codes NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against the OSSE files' own school names and against DOMO's graduation rate and enrollment; a school with no OSSE code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no District of Columbia measures.
  • Chronic absence comes from OSSE for every year, because the DOMO records carry none for the District of Columbia; the graduation rate is DOMO's own where DOMO has it (OSSE's cohort rate to the decimal) and OSSE's table fills the school-years DOMO lacks. No DOMO value is overwritten.
  • In-seat attendance and teacher retention are whole-school rates carried on each band a school reports. OSSE's top-coded, bottom-coded and suppressed cells stay missing, never zero; a retention rate on fewer than five teachers, an exact zero at a closed or misattributed school and a dash at a school new that year are set aside.
  • Out-of-school suspension is carried on the card as the share of schools at 5% or more, because OSSE publishes the rate only above a size-dependent floor; the published rates sit in the research pack.
  • The DC SAYS survey (2024-25) is one year with no trend: a topic is drawn on the card only where three counted Leader in Me schools carry a published result; every topic for every respondent group is in the research pack.
  • The 2019-20 and 2020-21 report-card workbooks are read only for the college-enrollment classes of 2018 and 2019 they carry; the 2021-22 attendance-growth column and 2021-22 school score are excluded by name; the 2025-26 workforce file is held for the next build.
  • 2025-26 school results from SchoolDigger's bulk file (the source of DOMO's subject file), read exactly as DOMO reads earlier years, extend each test to the newest year.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 1 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 1 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start -0.3 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median -0.3), and 0% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 0 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 1 pairs (1 unmatched).

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 13,981 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 14.5% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 1.3% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.2% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.1% of rows. Read as no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 0.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. District of Columbia's file reports achievement levels that cannot stand as measures here: no combination of the reported levels reproduces PercentMetStandard on more than 15% of rows (a five-level state whose export carries four columns, usually) (no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate; levels add up on 0.0% of rows). The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In District of Columbia that is 196 Title I schools, 121 charter schools, 6 magnet schools and 3 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in District of Columbia's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Math: Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Integrated Math II, Math, Mathematics; ELA: English I, English II, English Language Arts, Reading; Science: Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; District of Columbia publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 6 Leader in Me schools and 60 of 228 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 57 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 33.3% of Leader in Me schools and 43.9% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 6 of the 6 roster schools carry a date, and 4 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2024: 1, 2025: 3. 0 roster schools have no expiration date (0 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 0 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 7.2 against comparison 2.66 (p = 0.048, 5 vs 178 schools), and with them 7.2 against 2.66 (p = 0.048, 5 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2013-14 to 2014-15, 47.7 to 22.5 (-25.2); Math 2013-14 to 2014-15, 51.1 to 19.9 (-31.2). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Joining OSSE's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. OSSE's files key on the LEA and school codes OSSE assigns instead (the District of Columbia Public Schools is LEA 001, Burrville Elementary its school 221), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 224 id pairs for 223 of the 234 schools on this page (1 school carries more than one OSSE code across the years and joins under each), including 6 of 6 Leader in Me schools and 217 of 228 comparison schools; the 11 it cannot reach are charter schools that closed before NCES's 2016-17 directory and appear in no OSSE file. The map was checked against the OSSE files' own school names, which it was not built from: they agree with DOMO's name on 222 of 223 crosswalked schools (1 read one by one). An independent value check from files the map was not built from either: DOMO's own graduation rate is OSSE's four-year rate within a point on 101 of 101 secondary school-years both carry (to the decimal on 83), and DOMO's enrollment against OSSE's on 214 schools correlates at r = 0.954, half of them within 7.3% on size.
  • Every Leader in Me school is reached. All 6 Leader in Me schools on this page carry an OSSE code in NCES's directory and appear in OSSE's files, so none is named as unreached. They are counted on each card only in the years the core's own rules count them (2021-22: 3, 2022-23: 3, 2023-24: 5, 2024-25: 5, 2025-26: 2); a card line needs three counted schools, so no Leader in Me point is drawn before 2021-22, and a point that would rest on fewer is not drawn.
  • Chronic absence comes from OSSE; graduation is DOMO's own, filled from OSSE where DOMO has none. The DOMO records carry no chronic absence value for any District of Columbia row (0 values across every row), so chronic absence comes from the DC School Report Card's chronic absenteeism metric, the share of students absent 10% or more of their enrolled days: 828 school-years for 208 schools on this page, 2021-22 to 2024-25; OSSE published no chronic absenteeism for 2017-18 or 2018-19 (its 2017-18 "90% Attendance" column is the share of students attending 90% or more of days, a different measure, kept in the research pack under its own name), and 2019-20 and 2020-21 are closure years. The threshold changed once: from 2022-23 a student must be enrolled 20 instructional days to be counted, against 10 in 2021-22. The DOMO graduation rate is OSSE's four-year adjusted cohort rate to the decimal on the school-years both carry, so it stands wherever DOMO has it and OSSE's rate fills the 76 secondary school-years DOMO lacks; no DOMO value is overwritten. One difference between the two is worth knowing: where OSSE top-codes a rate as ">99%", DOMO shows 100 (4 of 5 such school-years), so DOMO's column reaches the ceiling a little more often than OSSE's; OSSE's top- and bottom-coded cells stay missing here.
  • In-seat attendance and teacher retention are measures, not only levels. Both are per-school rates, so they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the growth test), carried on each band a school reports as the core carries chronic absence. In-seat attendance, the share of enrolled days students were in their seats, was published for 2017-18 and 2021-22 and then retired in favor of chronic absenteeism and attendance growth, so it is two years apart with the closures between them; it is on the rows and in the research pack, and it is not on the card because a two-point line with one Leader in Me point would say less than the number itself. Teacher retention is the share of a school's teachers who were teaching at the same school the year before, from OSSE's educator workforce data files (teachers retained into 2021-22 to 2025-26); the published percent rebuilds from the file's own counts on every row, and the 2021-22 report card's own retention column agrees with the workforce file on nearly every school. 12 rates at schools with fewer than five teachers, 7 values of exactly zero (7 at schools OSSE itself flags as closed or misattributed) and 7 dashes at schools new that year are set aside. The 2025-26 workforce file describes teachers retained into a school year after this page's last and is held for the next build.
  • Discipline is a share of schools on the card, and the rates are in the pack. From 2021-22 OSSE publishes a school's out-of-school suspension rate, the share of students suspended at least once, only above a floor that depends on the school's size ("<1%" for most schools, "<5%" for small ones, "<0.1%" for the largest), which hides the rate for about four schools in ten (2021-22: a rate for 122 of 249 schools; 2022-23: a rate for 148 of 253 schools; 2023-24: a rate for 157 of 251 schools; 2024-25: a rate for 157 of 251 schools). A rate known mostly for the high-suspending schools cannot feed a growth test honestly, so the published rates go to the research pack and the card carries what the publication rule leaves knowable: whether 5% or more of a school's students were suspended (a bottom-coded cell is under its ceiling, so under 5; "<=10%" is unknown), which is settled for 222 of 222 schools in 2017-18, 249 of 249 schools in 2021-22, 252 of 253 schools in 2022-23, 251 of 251 schools in 2023-24, 251 of 251 schools in 2024-25. 2017-18 is read from the report card's whole-number shares, the same measure truncated to the integer and published for every school. Between the two eras sits the District's Student Fair Access to School Amendment Act of 2018, which from 2019-20 bars most out-of-school suspensions for students through grade 8 and caps their length, so the 2017-18 share of schools at 5% or more belongs to a different policy world from 2021-22 on and the line is read with that break in mind. The 2021-22 and 2022-23 files also carry suspension counts, which are incidents, not students, and are not used.
  • The DC SAYS survey: one year, no trend. OSSE gave the DC SAYS school climate survey across the District for the first time in spring 2025 and publishes each school's results as the share of respondents answering favorably on a topic, separately for students in grades 3-5 and 6-12, for families and for staff, with response rates. A school's result is published only where at least 10 answered and participation reached the survey's floor (50% of students or staff, 20% of families), so the family lines rest on the minority of schools that reached 20%. It is one year, so every DC SAYS line on the card is a single point with no trend, described. The topics drawn on the card are belonging, self-efficacy, perseverance, school safety for students in grades 3-5 (3 Leader in Me schools against 134 comparison schools); family engagement, school fit for families (3 Leader in Me schools against 59 comparison schools). The grades 6-12 student topics and the staff topics are in the research pack only: in 2024-25 only 2 Leader in Me middle schools were counted (Hart Middle had exited), under the three a line needs, and only 2 Leader in Me schools reached the 50% staff participation floor. Every topic for every respondent group sits in the research pack.
  • Growth percentiles, the accountability score and college enrollment. OSSE's median growth percentile (the median of each tested student's growth rank against students who started at the same place) is on the card for ELA and math, published for 2017-18 and 2022-23 to 2024-25; the school's STAR score (2017-18, 2018-19) and accountability score (2022-23 on) are in the research pack, because the framework changed across the window and a composite is not one series; the 2021-22 score, computed under a modified framework with no ratings issued and sitting at half the level of the years around it, is excluded by name; the separate 2022-23 summative-score workbook is an earlier posting of the same scores, identical for 192 of 199 schools and revised in the later aggregate file for 7 high schools, so the aggregate file is the one read. College enrollment (within 6 and 12 months of graduation) is keyed to the graduating class, 2018 to 2024 less the closure classes of 2020 and 2021; OSSE re-publishes a class's figure in later years and the values differ (155 of 188 figures published twice), so the latest publication is kept and named as such. The District's Leader in Me schools are elementary and middle schools, so no high-school measure has a Leader in Me line; they are in the research pack for the comparison schools.
  • 2025-26 results come from SchoolDigger's bulk file, the same source as DOMO's subject file for every earlier year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for District of Columbia. DOMO's subject file is SchoolDigger's test-score table, so the 2025-26 rows (1,552 school-grade-test rows for 215 schools) were read from SchoolDigger's newest bulk file for FranklinCovey in the same layout: the percent meeting the state's standard and the first four achievement levels, with SchoolDigger's grade and test labels. The same file's 2024-25 rows reproduce DOMO's: 100.0% of DOMO's 1,779 District of Columbia school rows for that year are in it, and the proficiency figure is identical on 100.0% of them. 456 newest-year rows carry only some of the four levels (DOMO's year before carried none), so their levels are left blank and their percent meeting the standard is kept.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 2 surveys across 2 District of Columbia schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 31 answered by more than one group (blended), 6 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (2 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 0 of the 2 schools counted as Leader in Me in District of Columbia meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

43 measures were screened, grouped into 19 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 0 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; District of Columbia publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • This page is descriptive: only 5 Leader in Me schools have results at both ends of the straight-numbers window, so a significance verdict would rest on too few schools to trust.
  • This page is descriptive: the headline rests on too few Leader in Me schools to test, and at most five on any OSSE card line, so every District of Columbia figure is a level described on the schools counted that year, never tested, and a share-of-schools line moves twenty points per school.
  • OSSE publishes a school's suspension rate only above a floor that depends on its size, so the rate is known for about six schools in ten and the page carries the share of schools at 5% or more instead.
  • The DC SAYS survey exists for 2024-25 only, its family results only for the minority of schools whose families reached 20% participation, and it cannot show a trend until OSSE publishes a second year.
  • Chronic absenteeism's enrollment threshold changed from 10 to 20 instructional days in 2022-23, and in-seat attendance was retired after 2021-22, so neither attendance series is fully one series across the window.
  • The District's Leader in Me schools are elementary and middle schools, so the graduation, college-enrollment and advanced-coursework measures describe comparison schools only.
  • SchoolDigger's newest bulk file does not yet carry every test DOMO carried the year before (2025-26 5th Grade Science (83 rows the year before); 2025-26 8th Grade Science (21 rows the year before); 2025-26 End of course Science (15 rows the year before)). Those results usually come out later in the fall. So a school is never compared on one set of tests against a different set, a school that took one of those tests the year before has its newest-year results in that grade band and subject left out (0 rows at 0 schools) until the missing results are released and folded in; the rest of its results, and every earlier year, are unchanged.
