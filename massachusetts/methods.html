Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 0 Massachusetts schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 12 of the 15 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 1 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Massachusetts's own measures join through the DESE organization code NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited by name within district against the state files' own names and against DOMO's graduation rate and enrollment; a school with no code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Massachusetts measures.
  • The attendance and discipline rates are the portal's stored values (the bulk export rounds every rate to ten points); the retention rate and the share of students disciplined are rebuilt from the files' own counts.
  • Chronic absence comes from the state for every year, because the DOMO records carry none for Massachusetts; the graduation rate is DOMO's own where DOMO has it (the state's cohort rate to the decimal) and the state's DART file fills the school-years DOMO lacks. No DOMO value is overwritten.
  • Students disciplined and teacher retention are whole-school rates carried on each band a school reports; the growth percentiles are built by band from the published grade figures. A retention rate on fewer than five teachers, an exact zero and a school flagged as not reporting are set aside; a suspension rate the state leaves blank stays missing, never zero.
  • The 2016-17 growth figure is a median and is excluded by name from the mean series; the legacy medians, the pre-2018 school percentile and PPI and the cumulative target percentage are carried under their own names and never joined to the later series.
  • The 2019-20 and 2020-21 school years are out of every card, row and pack record; the attendance file's March rows are not read; the 2020 and 2021 accountability workbooks repeat 2019 and are not read.
  • 2025-26 school results from SchoolDigger's bulk file (the source of DOMO's subject file), read exactly as DOMO reads earlier years, extend each test to the newest year.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 6 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 0 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 33% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 4 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 2 pairs (0 unmatched).

26 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Assabet Valley Vocational High School, Bay Path Regional Vocational Technical High School, Blue Hills Regional Vocational Technical, Bristol-Plymouth Vocational Technical, Cape Cod Region Vocational Technical, Charles McCann Vocational Technical, Diman Regional Vocational Technical High, Gr Lawrence Regional Vocational Technical, Gr Lowell Regional Vocational Technical, Gr New Bedford Vocational Technical, Lynn Vocational Technical Institute, Madison Park Technical Vocational High School, Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical, Northeast Metro Regional Vocational.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 138,870 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 76.7% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.6% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 17.1% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.4% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 75.4% of rows; the years and subjects that do not add up (2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math, 2014-15 Science (end-of-course), 2014-15 Science, 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science (end-of-course), 2015-16 Science, 2016-17 Science (end-of-course), 2016-17 Science, 2017-18 Science (end-of-course), 2017-18 Science, 2018-19 Science (end-of-course), 2018-19 Science, 2021-22 Science (end-of-course)) carry no level values.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Massachusetts's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 75.4% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values: 2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math, 2014-15 Science (end-of-course), 2014-15 Science, 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science (end-of-course), 2015-16 Science, 2016-17 Science (end-of-course), 2016-17 Science, 2017-18 Science (end-of-course), 2017-18 Science, 2018-19 Science (end-of-course), 2018-19 Science, 2021-22 Science (end-of-course)), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard (Not Meeting Expectations, Partially Meeting Expectations, Meeting Expectations, Exceeding Expectations in Massachusetts's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Massachusetts that is 1,043 Title I schools, 86 charter schools, 0 magnet schools and 17 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Massachusetts's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English Language Arts, English Language Arts/Literacy; Math: Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Integrated Math I, Integrated Math II, Integrated Math III, Mathematics; Science: Biology, Chemistry, Introductory Physics, Physics, Science, Science and Technology/Engineering, Technology/Engineering; Social Studies: Civics.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry no free-and-reduced-lunch share for Massachusetts (15 of 15 Leader in Me schools and 1,674 of 1,674 comparison schools are blank), so every school sits in the Unknown poverty band, the poverty filter has nothing to select, and matched pairs cannot use the poverty caliper. A state file with its own poverty measure fills this in when it lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 14 of the 15 roster schools carry a date, and 12 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2021: 1, 2022: 1, 2024: 1, 2025: 8, 2026: 1. 1 roster schools have no expiration date (1 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 1 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 4.53 against comparison -1.45 (p = 0.333, 3 vs 1457 schools), and with them 4.64 against -1.45 (p = 0.166, 4 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2015-16 to 2016-17, 66.7 to 57.3 (-9.3). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Joining the state's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. The state's files key on DESE's eight-digit organization code instead (Waltham's Douglas MacArthur Elementary is 03080032; the first four digits are the district), which shares no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 1,674 id pairs for 1,673 of the 1,689 schools on this page (1 school carries more than one code across the years and joins under each), including 15 of 15 Leader in Me schools and 1,658 of 1,674 comparison schools; the 16 it cannot reach closed before NCES's 2016-17 directory and carry 2014-15 rows only. All four are per-school rates, so they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the school list). Students disciplined is the share of a school's students who received at least one disciplinary action the state reports, all offenses, rebuilt from the discipline file's counts for every school (16,545 school-years, 2014-15 to 2024-25); the state publishes the separate in-school and out-of-school suspension rates only where six or more students were disciplined (10,951 of 23,970 school-years), a rate known mostly for the higher-suspending schools, so those sit in the research pack under their own names. Teacher retention is the share of a school's teachers from the year before still teaching at the same school in the year shown, rebuilt from the staff retention file's counts; 294 rates at schools with fewer than five teachers, 14 exact zeros (Center (2024-25: 30 teachers, none retained); Southbridge Academy (2024-25: 8 teachers, none retained); New Braintree Grade (2021-22: 10 teachers, none retained); West Roxbury Academy (2018-19: 41 teachers, none retained); Brooke Charter School (2016-17: 39 teachers, none retained); Center For Technical Education Innovation (2015-16: 58 teachers, none retained)) and 2 school-years the state flags as failing to meet its reporting requirements are set aside. Both are whole-school rates carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence. The growth percentiles are the mean student growth percentile on the state test in English language arts and in mathematics: on the card the whole-school grades 3-8 figure as the state publishes it; on the rows a school whose grades all sit in one band inside 3-8 carries that same published figure, and a combined-grade school's primary band is built from its grade 4 and 5 results and its secondary band from grades 6, 7, 8 and 10, student-weighted from the published grade figures (a high school's secondary band is its grade 10 figure). On the 1,990 combined-grade school-years where every grade is published, the two bands weighted together rebuild the whole-school figure within a point on 1,990. The state publishes a growth figure only where at least 20 students had one.

The MCAS file labels every year's growth column an average, but for 2016-17 the whole-school figure is not the student-weighted mean of the grade figures (within a point on 57.1% of school-years, against 100.0% or more in every later year) and its spread is half again as wide: it is the median growth percentile the state reported before its 2018 accountability system moved to means. So the mean series runs 2017-18 to 2024-25 less the closures, and the 2,618 2016-17 values sit in the research pack under their own name. The legacy MCAS file carries median growth percentiles for 2014-15 and 2015-16 (all grades, for the schools whose districts stayed on the legacy test rather than PARCC) and for grade 10 through 2017-18; they are medians on a different test and never join the mean series. Where the two files carry the same school-year (2016-17 and 2017-18), the next-generation file is the authority for grades 3-8 and the legacy file carries only grade 10, so no school-year is read from both for the same grades. Neither file's achievement columns are read: DOMO's subject file carries them.
  • The accountability percentile and the target percentage, and what changed between systems. Massachusetts replaced its accountability system in 2018. The accountability percentile (1-99, among schools serving the same grades) and the criterion-referenced target percentage (the share of a school's own improvement targets met) are read from the profiles workbooks for 2017-18, 2018-19 and 2021-22 and from the classification file for 2022-23 to 2024-25 (the two agree on every percentile and every classification in the years both carry). The 2019-20 and 2020-21 workbooks repeat the 2019 determinations for every school (checked) and are not read; 2021-22 carries a percentile published for information only and no classification. The target percentage on the card is the current-year figure (2017-18, 2022-23 to 2024-25); the cumulative figure the 2018-19, 2023-24 and 2024-25 reports also publish blends the year before (0.4 on the year before, 0.6 on the current year; it rebuilds from the weights on 3,102 of 3,102 rows) and sits in the pack under its own name. The state's 2025 accountability data workbook cross-checks the classification file: the percentile agrees on 1517 of 1517 schools and the annual target percentage within a point on 1572 of 1572. One thing the cross-check shows: the 2025 report restates the year before, and for 49 of 1549 schools its prior-year target percentage differs by more than a point from what the 2024 report published, so the current-year series carries each year as its own report published it and the 2025 cumulative figure blends the restated value. The 2014-15 to 2016-17 workbooks describe the earlier system (a school percentile and a cumulative progress and performance index), carried in the pack under their own names and never joined to the later series; the classification itself is a category, carried as a flag (requiring assistance or intervention, or not) in the pack.
  • College enrollment, MassCore, pathways and the DART high-school indicators are in the research pack. The graduates-attending-higher-education file counts each high school's graduates enrolled in college within 16 months of graduation (classes of 2015 to 2024; the class of 2026 is not yet published on that window) and by the March after graduation (classes of 2015 to 2025), keyed on the graduating class, with the in-state and campus-type shares; the DART file carries the four- and five-year graduation rates, dropout, promotion, college enrollment the fall after graduation, persistence, FAFSA completion, advanced coursework and the SAT averages for 449 high schools (it repeats 167 rows exactly, dropped, and carries 224 school-year figures twice with differing values, nearly all of them the class of 2024's college enrollment in two vintages with no marker of which is later, which are set aside rather than averaged); MassCore completion is the share of graduates finishing the state's recommended program of study (at exactly 100% for 46.8% of school-years, a ceiling); pathways enrollment (2021-22 to 2024-25) is the share of students in Chapter 74 career/technical, Innovation Career Pathway and Early College programs; the cohort progression file follows each graduating class through college enrollment, persistence and a degree, rebuilt from its counts. Massachusetts's Leader in Me schools are elementary and K-8 schools, with one high school counted for one year, so no high-school measure has a Leader in Me line; they are in the research pack for the comparison schools.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 4 surveys across 3 Massachusetts schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 37 answered by more than one group (blended). Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (3 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 2 of the 4 schools counted as Leader in Me in Massachusetts meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.

Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 1 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Massachusetts publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • This page is descriptive: only 4 Leader in Me schools have results at both ends of the straight-numbers window, so a significance verdict would rest on too few schools to trust.
  • This page is descriptive: the headline rests on too few Leader in Me schools to test, and at most a dozen on any state card line, so every Massachusetts figure is a level described on the schools counted that year, never tested.
  • The DOMO records carry no free-and-reduced-lunch share for Massachusetts, so the state cards cannot say how the two groups compare on poverty; a level gap is as likely to be composition as practice.
  • The state publishes a school's suspension rates only where six or more students were disciplined, so those rates are known for about half the schools and the page carries the share of students disciplined instead.
  • The growth series begins in 2017-18 (the 2016-17 figure is a median), the accountability series in 2017-18 under a new system, and chronic absence in 2017-18, so none of the state's measures reaches back to the page's first years.
  • Massachusetts's Leader in Me schools are elementary and K-8 schools, with one high school counted for one year, so the graduation, college enrollment, MassCore, pathways and DART measures describe comparison schools only.
