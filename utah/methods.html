Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 9 Utah schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 43 of the 99 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 24 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • 2 roster schools also appear in the comparison export in years after their roster rows end, which is DOMO's own record that they left; they count as Leader in Me through their last roster year and in neither group after.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Utah's own measures join through the state LEA and school numbers NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against a name-within-LEA join and DOMO's own chronic absence; the growth files carry names only and join by school name within LEA, and a name two schools share is refused. A school with no state id keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Utah measures.
  • Attendance is the year's average days attended over its average days enrolled, K-12; a rate at or above 99.9% or of exactly zero is read as unreported. Utah's files carry no chronic-absence rate, so DOMO's chronic absence is neither filled nor overwritten.
  • The growth measure is the mean of a school's English language arts and mathematics median growth percentiles; a masked subject (under ten students) leaves the school with no combined figure that year. The ACT composite is the grade 11 mean; a benchmark share the state publishes as a range is carried as text and averaged nowhere. 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Utah card and row.
  • 2025-26 RISE mathematics (grades 3-8) and science (grades 4-8) and Utah Aspire Plus English language arts, mathematics and science (grades 9-10), from the Utah State Board of Education's 2026 proficiency rates and levels workbooks, extend each subject to the newest year; proficiency is Proficient plus Highly Proficient, as in prior years, and a masked cell is filled only where the state's published numbers fix it. RISE English language arts grades 3-8 is held for 2025-26 because it moved to a new scale in spring 2025 and the page has no 2024-25 value on that scale.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 89 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 4 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 39% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 52 pairs (1 unmatched); Secondary: 37 pairs (3 unmatched).

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 70,808 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 100.0% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.0% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 3.3% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.9% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 57.2% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Utah's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 57.2% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard (Below Proficient, Approaching Proficient, Proficient, Highly Proficient in Utah's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Utah that is 302 Title I schools, 133 charter schools, 21 magnet schools and 7 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Utah's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Math: Math, Mathematics, Secondary Math I, Secondary Math II, Secondary Math III; ELA: English Language Arts, Language Arts; Science: Biology, Chemistry, Earth Science, Physics, Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Utah publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 99 Leader in Me schools and 13 of 842 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 1 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 2.0% of Leader in Me schools and 3.1% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 75 of the 99 roster schools carry a date, and 43 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 4, 2019: 4, 2021: 5, 2022: 1, 2023: 4, 2024: 5, 2025: 10, 2026: 10. 24 roster schools have no expiration date (21 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 21 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 2.59 against comparison 1.14 (p = 0.347, 34 vs 704 schools), and with them 1.87 against 1.14 (p = 0.56, 50 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Joining Utah's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Utah's attendance and ACT files key on the state's LEA number and three-digit school number instead (Alpine District is 01 and American Fork High is 704; a charter carries a letter, 8K), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 942 id pairs for 941 of the 941 schools on this page, including 99 of 99 Leader in Me schools and 842 of 842 comparison schools (2016-17 to 2023-24; one school renumbered inside that window joins under each number). The growth files carry names only, so each (LEA, school) name resolves to the same pair by school name within LEA, through the attendance and ACT files' own names for the same year first, then any year's, then NCES's names: 7,823 of 7,846 growth school-years find a state id and 7,083 of those a school on this page (740 are schools DOMO's exports do not carry; 21 find no id at all, a name no other file spells the same way, such as Bryant Middle (Salt Lake District); Dixie Sun School (Washington District); Eschool@Provo School District (Provo District)); one name two schools share within one LEA (Minersville School (Beaver District)) is refused rather than guessed. The map was audited a second way: a name-within-LEA join from DOMO's school name and the page's district label to the state files' own names speaks for 930 of the NCES-joined schools and agrees on 930. The state files' own name agrees with DOMO's on 928 of 932 crosswalked ids. An independent value check from a value the map was not built from: DOMO's own chronic absence against the state's attendance rate on the same school-years runs r = -0.961 on 4,143 school-years (Spearman -0.976), which is what one attendance measure against another should do when the ids line up.
  • Attendance is days attended over days enrolled, and a perfect rate is unreported. USBE's attendance and membership report gives each school's average days of attendance and of membership and their ratio, K-12 and by grade and group (2017-18 to 2024-25, 8,095 school-years, no duplicate ids, no LEA rows on the school sheet; the published rate rebuilds from the two averages on 8,095 of them). Schools under ten students with attendance are not listed and a group under ten is blank. In 33 school-years a school reported attendance within a tenth of a point of its membership and in 9 it reported none at all; a rate at or above 99.9% or of exactly zero is read as unreported. USBE's own note says 2019-20 membership was prorated under the pandemic hold-harmless rule, so that year's rates run high; it is a closure year and off every comparison anyway. Utah's report carries no chronic-absence rate, so DOMO's chronic absence (which the core carries for 2017-18, 2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25) is never filled or overwritten from these files; the two were audited against each other instead, as the join check above. The attendance rate is a whole-school figure carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence, and it runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test). The 2024-25 companion file of average days absent gives per-grade averages with no counts to weight them (48 schools report zero absences in every grade, a reporting gap read as unreported, and 269 grade cells are masked), so it sits in the research pack only, as the mean of a school's grade averages, and on no card.
  • Growth is the state's own median growth percentile. USBE's student growth percentile files rank each student's score gain against students who started at the same score and publish the school's median rank by subject (2016-17, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25; there is no 2019-20 file because there was no spring 2020 test). The statewide median is 50 in every subject and year by construction, so a school's figure reads directly as above or below typical growth. 1,643 school-subject cells under ten students are masked and left missing. The 2016-17 and 2017-18 medians come from the SAGE tests and 2018-19 on from RISE (grades 3-8) and Utah Aspire Plus (grades 9-10); the construction is the same and the state publishes them as one series, so they are drawn as one here, and science medians begin in 2018-19 (none for 2017-18); in 2022-23 the by-test sheet also carries grade 3, which the overall median folds in. The 2020-21 medians measure growth across the two-year gap from spring 2019 and sit in the pack only, as a closure year. The measure at the top of the page is the mean of a school's English language arts and mathematics medians, on every band the school reports; a school with one subject masked carries that subject in the pack and no combined figure. The files carry no school numbers, so every row joins by name within LEA as described above; Minersville School (Beaver District) names two schools in one LEA and is left out.
  • The grade 11 ACT is a census test, and the benchmark share is often a range. Every Utah eleventh grader takes the ACT on a state test day, so a school's mean composite (2017-18 to 2024-25) is not a self-selected group. The composite is printed exactly for nearly every school (8 school-years masked under ten students); the share scoring 18 or higher is printed exactly for 1,100 school-years and as a range for 368 (100 more masked outright), smaller schools mostly, so the card's benchmark line averages the exact values only and the research pack carries each range as the state wrote it. The 2017-18 file heads the column '% Scoring 18 or Higher' and later files 'Percent With 18 or Higher Composite Score'; the definition is the same. The state tested 32,998 students in 2019-20 against about 41,227 in a normal year (the March 2020 test day was lost), a closure year that is off every comparison. The composite is a measure at the top of the page on the secondary band, high schools only.
  • Utah's own measures are measures, not only levels. Attendance, the combined growth median and the ACT composite are per-school rates, so they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), each carried on every band a school reports. The card shows the same measures as statewide levels by group, with attendance for economically disadvantaged students, the two subject medians and the ACT benchmark share beside them; group values are school means with a three-school minimum, described, never significance-tested, and the roster growing every year means composition as much as practice. Utah publishes no school-level discipline file in this build, so the page carries no behavior measure and look-alike mode names none.
  • 2025-26 results come from Utah's own RISE and Utah Aspire Plus files and extend mathematics (grades 3-10), science (grades 4-10) and English language arts (grades 9-10) to the newest year; English language arts in grades 3-8 stays at 2023-24. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Utah. The 2025-26 rows (5,329 school-grade-subject rows for 870 schools) were read from the Utah State Board of Education's 2026 RISE and Utah Aspire Plus proficiency rates and proficiency levels workbooks (all students, school rows) and joined to the DOMO id through the NCES directory's Utah state id, the same join as the state's other files on this page. They cover every test DOMO carried for Utah in 2024-25: RISE mathematics in grades 3-8 and science in grades 4-8, and Utah Aspire Plus English language arts, mathematics and science in grades 9 and 10. Proficiency is the state's percent proficient (Proficient plus Highly Proficient), the figure DOMO carries, and the four levels are Below Proficient, Approaching Proficient, Proficient and Highly Proficient; read the same way, all 5,041 of DOMO's 5,041 Utah rows for 2024-25 are one row of the state's own 2024-25 workbooks, and the proficiency figure equals DOMO's to the tenth on 62% of them and is within half a point on 93% (median difference 0.0 points); DOMO's values come from an earlier release of that year than the workbooks on file, and most differences are one or two students. Utah masks small groups: a group under ten students is not reported, a group under forty is shown as a ten-point range, and a share near 0 or 100 as a bound. A masked cell is filled only where the state's own published numbers fix it exactly (the four levels add to 100, and proficiency is the top two levels), and only when the filled value sits inside the range the state printed. 22 proficiency values and 360 level values were filled this way, a level the state leaves blank (no student reached it) is zero, and the 1,425 masked rows whose proficiency stays unknown carry no value, as in DOMO's earlier years; no midpoint of a range is ever used. RISE English language arts in grades 3-8 is not on the page for 2025-26. Utah gave a new RISE English language arts test on a new scale in spring 2025, and the state released those results in November 2025, after DOMO's 2024-25 pull, so DOMO's 2024-25 Utah rows carry no grades 3-8 English language arts and this page's last grades 3-8 English language arts year is 2023-24, on the old scale. A 2025-26 value would be compared across the scale change, so it is held. On the state's own files the new scale runs about 2 points below the old one at the same schools and grades, and 2024-25 to 2025-26 on the new scale is comparable (median change +1.9 points, 2,417 school-grade pairs). Until 2024-25 is added on the new scale, the combined reading-and-math figure of a school's grades 3-8 in 2024-25 and 2025-26 is its mathematics alone, while earlier years combine both subjects. The Secondary Math I test, which DOMO does not carry for Utah, is not read, and schools opened after the newest NCES directory (2023-24) have no federal id yet and carry no 2025-26 row (13 schools).

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 97 surveys across 37 Utah schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build 2 surveys were a second survey in a school-year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (37 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 9 of the 55 schools counted as Leader in Me in Utah meet the criteria, and a further 2 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. A school in its third year is measured from its baseline year; a school already in the process is measured from the start of the window.
Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

58 measures were screened, grouped into 13 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 24 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Utah publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Utah publishes no school-level discipline or climate file in this build, so the page carries no behavior measure from the state and look-alike mode has none to name.
  • A median growth percentile is a median: at a small school a few students move it, and the state masks any cell under ten students, so the smallest schools carry no growth figure at all.
  • The ACT benchmark share is printed as a range for smaller schools, so its card line stands on the schools with an exact value and leans toward larger high schools; the composite mean, printed for nearly every school, is the firmer read.
  • Utah's attendance rate is a whole-school figure carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence; a K-6 school's rate sits on its grade 6 secondary rows as well as its primary rows.
  • Grades 3-8 English language arts ends at 2023-24 on this page. Utah's RISE English language arts moved to a new scale in spring 2025, DOMO's 2024-25 Utah rows carry none of it, and the state's 2025-26 results are held until 2024-25 is added on the new scale; in 2024-25 and 2025-26 the combined reading-and-math figure of a school's grades 3-8 is its mathematics alone.
