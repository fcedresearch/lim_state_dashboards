Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 3 Alabama schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 68 of the 158 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 2 inside the grace window stay active; 75 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • 2 roster schools also appear in the comparison export in years after their roster rows end, which is DOMO's own record that they left; they count as Leader in Me through their last roster year and in neither group after.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Alabama's incident reports carry school names only, so they join by school name within system through the state's spelling in the NCES directory and through DOMO's spelling, which must agree; a school neither spelling reaches keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no incident count.
  • A school missing from a year's incident report is read as reporting no incidents where its system reported and the school is shown operating that year; exclusively virtual schools stay out of the rates.
  • Alabama's masked small cells arrive as -0.01 in the level columns (229 rows in 2014-15 and 2015-16) and are read as exported, a hundredth of a point on a level that is 0.
  • Alabama's accountability workbooks join by the state's system and school codes through the NCES directory, with the name join run beside them as a check that must agree; the report-card exports and the graduation files carry names only and join by school name within system through the state's spelling and DOMO's. A school no file reaches keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no state measures.
  • The state's graduation-rate and college-and-career-ready indicators are lagged a class; the page puts them on the class's own year. The core's graduation column is filled only where DOMO has no value, following DOMO's own convention year by year, from the complete accountability files; no DOMO value is overwritten.
  • The federal report-card supporting data's masked graduation and CCR rates are never used as a measure; the accountability indicator, published for every high school, carries both, and the supporting data's counts go to the research pack.
  • The accountability points are a whole-school score carried on each band a school reports; the letter grade is the share of schools graded A or B on the card. ID and AW grades carry no points.
  • The chronic absenteeism indicator for 2016-17 and 2017-18 (an earlier count) and the 2020-21 accountability file (a closure year) are excluded by name from every card and row and kept in the research pack, labelled.
  • The ACHE college-going list carries names under counties and joins by a name unique statewide, a shared name settled by county; one class only, so it is a card level and a pack value, never a trend.
  • 2025-26 grades 3-8 English language arts and math and grade 11 science (from the Alabama State Department of Education's participation-and-proficiency files) extend each subject to the newest year; proficiency is levels 3 and 4, as in every prior year.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 139 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 4 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start -0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 43% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 92 pairs (2 unmatched); Secondary: 47 pairs (2 unmatched).

1 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Continuous Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 85,453 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 100.0% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.1% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.3% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.2% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 100.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Alabama's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 100.0% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard. The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Alabama that is 926 Title I schools, 18 charter schools, 38 magnet schools and 5 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Alabama's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Math: Math, Mathematics; Science: Science; ELA: English, Reading.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Alabama publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 158 Leader in Me schools and 4 of 1,172 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 3 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 13.9% of Leader in Me schools and 24.3% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 83 of the 158 roster schools carry a date, and 68 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 1, 2020: 2, 2021: 9, 2022: 16, 2023: 12, 2024: 14, 2025: 6, 2026: 8. 75 roster schools have no expiration date (72 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 72 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 11.41 against comparison 11.18 (p = 0.907, 19 vs 761 schools), and with them 11.67 against 11.18 (p = 0.658, 77 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2024-25 to 2025-26, 51.2 to 60.5 (+9.3). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Two ids, one school. These comparison ids share a name and city with a Leader in Me school and no tested year, so they are folded into it: Gulf Shores Elementary School (Gulf Shores); Gulf Shores High School (Gulf Shores); Gulf Shores Middle School (Gulf Shores); Orange Beach Elementary School (Orange Beach); Orange Beach MiddleHigh School (Orange Beach).

The SIR web reports carry school names only, one sheet per school system, so each row is joined by school name within system two ways that must agree: through the state's own spelling (the NCES Common Core of Data directory lists every Alabama public school under the state's name and system name beside its federal id, 2016-17 to 2023-24) and through DOMO's spelling.   • A school absent from a year's report is read as zero. The SIR lists only schools with incidents (no row in any year carries a zero), so a school missing from its system's sheet in a year the system reported is read as reporting none: 134 of the 4,848 school-years on the card, against 4,714 listed. The reading is applied only where something shows the school open that year (a DOMO record for the year, the NCES directory listing it open that year or in the directory's last year, or a tested grade), so a school that had not opened or had closed is not handed a zero; 202 school-years were left out on that test and 10 because the system reported nothing that year. The report cannot separate a school with no incidents from one that did not file, and the page does not try.
  • Virtual schools stay out of the rates. 15 schools NCES flags as exclusively virtual (Baldwin County Virtual School (Baldwin County); Athens Renaissance School (Athens City); Alabama Destinations Career Academy (Chickasaw City); Baldwin County Elementary Virtual School (Baldwin County); BCS Virtual Academy of Learning (Birmingham City); Genesis Innovative School (Conecuh County); Dothan City Virtual School (Dothan City); EDGE Virtual School (Elmore County); Alabama Virtual Academy at Eufaula City Schools (Eufaula City); Houston County Virtual Academy (Houston County); Jefferson County Virtual School (Jefferson County); Alabama Connections Academy (Limestone County); Madison County Virtual Academy (Madison County); Saints Virtual Academy (Selma City); SCC Virtual Preparatory Academy (St Clair County)) are left out of the incident rates and the research pack's incident values: a fully online school reports a handful of incidents over an enrollment that runs to thousands, which would pull its group's rate down without saying anything about a building. The August page carried them.
  • Incident counts are self-reported. Alabama publishes incident counts by offense rather than suspension rates, so the card shows reported incidents per 100 students on DOMO enrollment (one snapshot per school). Every school's total was proved against the sum of its offense rows and every system's total against the sum of its schools, with no exceptions across the four reports. Schools self-report and systems differ in how carefully they record, so a school's own direction over time is firmer ground than the gap between groups, and incident counts are not comparable across states. The Leader in Me line stands on 108 schools in 2021-22 and 97 in 2024-25, each year's own membership under the page's rules, so the card's first-to-last sentence is spoken only when the counted group stayed within half its size; a roster that changes as schools pass the full-year rule or exit is composition, not a trend.
  • Alabama changed tests twice in this span. Alabama used ACT Aspire through 2016-17, Scantron Performance Series in 2017-18 and 2018-19, and ACAP from 2020-21 on; spring 2020 testing was cancelled and spring 2021 was the first ACAP year. The core's state table carries no entry for Alabama, so this is the August page's record of the eras: the straight-numbers window (2021-22 on) sits inside the ACAP era, and the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year, which absorbs the level breaks. Grade 11 results are ACT-derived and end-of-course tests appear only in some years, so high-school lines mix instruments across eras.
  • Masked small cells arrive as -0.01. DOMO's subject file marks Alabama's masked small cells with a -0.01 sentinel in the level columns on 229 rows, all in 2014-15 and 2015-16, where the other levels already add to 100 (the masked level is a true zero or near it). The core reads the level columns as exported, so those rows carry -0.01 rather than a blank on that level, a hundredth of a point in years outside every straight-numbers window; the August page nulled them.
  • Joining Alabama's accountability files. The state's accountability workbooks (2018-19 and 2021-22 to 2024-25) carry Alabama's own system and school codes, which the NCES Common Core of Data directory lists beside the federal id ("AL-001-0055"), so they join by code, and the name join is run beside it as a check: code and name agree on 6,711 of 6,711 indicator rows and 6,708 of 6,708 letter-grade rows both reach, with no disagreement. The 2016-17, 2017-18 and 2020-21 report-card exports and the ten graduation files carry names only and join by school name within system through the state's spelling and DOMO's, which must agree, as the incident reports do. The 10 workbook rows whose code the directory does not carry yet (schools new in 2024-25) match no DOMO record by name either, so they stay counted, never guessed. Reach: indicator scores for 158 of 158 Leader in Me schools and 1,147 of 1,172 comparison schools; letter grades for 158 of 158; a graduation rate for 29 Leader in Me and 378 comparison high schools. State rows no school matched: 17 indicator rows, 12 grade rows, 7 graduation-file rows, all counted, never guessed. A value check the join was not built from: DOMO's chronic absence equals the state's chronic absenteeism indicator within half a point on 100.0% of 1,195 school-years in 2021-22, 100.0% of 1,187 school-years in 2022-23, 100.0% of 1,190 school-years in 2023-24, 100.0% of 1,169 school-years in 2024-25.
  • The graduation rate and the college-and-career-ready rate are a class behind. Alabama's accountability graduation-rate and CCR indicators are lagged: the report card for a year carries the class that graduated the spring before. The files show it. DOMO's own graduation rates for 2016-17 to 2018-19 equal the state's file labelled one year later (2016-17: 100.0% of 269 school-years within half a point of the file labelled 2017-18 (the indicator), 13.0% of the file labelled 2016-17; 2017-18: 100.0% of 261 school-years within half a point of the file labelled 2018-19 (the indicator), 11.9% of the file labelled 2017-18; 2018-19: 100.0% of 108 school-years within half a point of the file labelled 2019-20 (the supporting data), 12.0% of the file labelled 2018-19), while DOMO's chronic absence and the core's proficiency line up with the same-labelled file; from 2021-22 DOMO's graduation rate equals the same-labelled file (2021-22: 100.0% of 355; 2022-23: 100.0% of 360; 2023-24: 100.0% of 366; 2024-25: 100.0% of 360), so DOMO's column is the class's own year through 2018-19 and the report-card year, a class behind, from 2021-22 on: two conventions in one column, which the full-history window scores year by year and the straight-numbers window never mixes. This page puts the state's graduation rate and CCR share on the class's own year, as the Tennessee page does with Ready Graduate, so the classes of 2020 and 2021 fall in the closure years and stay off every comparison. The core's graduation column is filled only where DOMO has no value, following DOMO's own convention in each year and only from the complete accountability files: 2015-16 from the file labelled 2016-17 (353 school-years DOMO lacks); 2016-17 from the file labelled 2017-18 (87 school-years DOMO lacks); 2017-18 from the file labelled 2018-19 (99 school-years DOMO lacks); 2021-22 from the file labelled 2021-22 (6 school-years DOMO lacks); 2022-23 from the file labelled 2022-23 (4 school-years DOMO lacks); 2023-24 from the file labelled 2023-24 (6 school-years DOMO lacks); 2024-25 from the file labelled 2024-25 (7 school-years DOMO lacks); 2014-15 and 2018-19 are not filled because their only source is the masked supporting data. No DOMO value is overwritten.
  • The federal report-card supporting data masks most high schools. The ten graduation files (CCR_AL) carry the cohort, the graduates and the college-and-career-ready count with the two rates, but the state masks a count wherever fewer than eleven students sit on either side of it (the smallest published number of non-graduates is 11) and writes any rate of 95 or more as "~", so a graduation rate is published for about 38% of high schools a year, the largest and the lowest-graduating ones, with a ceiling of 94.98 (a rate the counts rebuild on every row that carries both). A rate known for the schools with more non-graduates cannot feed a growth test, so the rows and the card carry the accountability indicator instead, which the state publishes for every high school and which the supporting data reproduces exactly wherever it publishes a value (2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25; the 2016-17 and 2017-18 exports round to a decimal or a whole number). The counts and the published rates go to the research pack, on the class's year.
  • The letter grade's points are a measure; the letter itself is a share. Alabama's A-F grade is a binning of the state's accountability points, 0 to 100, published beside it: A is 90 and up, B 80 to 89, C 70 to 79, D 60 to 69, F under 60, and the bands hold on every graded school in every file. The points are therefore the state's own continuous score, not a mapping of letters, and they run through the same machinery as every other measure on the page (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), carried on each band a school reports as the core carries chronic absence. The formula weights achievement, growth and chronic absenteeism for every school and adds the lagged graduation and CCR indicators for high schools, so a school's points are comparable with its own across years and with other schools of its kind within a year, which is how the state uses them. The letter grade sits on the card as the share of schools graded A or B and in the research pack. Grades run 2018-19 and 2021-22 on (none were issued for 2019-20 and 2020-21); a school graded ID (insufficient data) or AW (approved waiver) carries no points and is counted, not graded (18 in 2018-19, 15 in 2021-22, 17 in 2022-23, 14 in 2023-24, 18 in 2024-25).
  • Chronic absenteeism changed count in 2018-19; 2016-17 and 2017-18 are excluded by name. The state's chronic absenteeism indicator fell from 17.93% to 10.97% at the mean between 2017-18 and 2018-19 across 1,323 schools, with 76.6% of schools falling by a quarter or more, the median school at 0.6 of its previous rate and the ranking of schools intact (correlation 0.802), which is what a change in counting looks like and what a change in attendance does not. The 2021-22 to 2024-25 values are the numbers DOMO carries, and the series after the closures runs from the 2018-19 level through the pandemic rise and back down, so the card draws 2018-19 and 2021-22 on and leaves 2016-17 and 2017-18 off by name; every year is in the research pack, labelled. The core's chronic-absence column is not filled from this file.
  • Growth is capped at 100. The academic growth indicator tops out at 100, and the share of schools at the cap rose from 13% in 2016-17, 22% in 2017-18, 23% in 2018-19, 39% in 2021-22, 41% in 2022-23, 39% in 2023-24, 39% in 2024-25. A ceiling that two schools in five sit on flattens the mean at the top for both groups, so a gap on the growth line is mostly a gap in how many schools fall short of the cap.
  • College-going is one class, joined by name within county. The Alabama Commission on Higher Education publishes the college-going rate by high school for one class at a time and overwrites the file each summer, so the folder holds the class of 2025 only (374 high schools under 67 counties, 50,907 graduates of the file's 50,907). The rate is one minus the file's not-enrolled share, which rebuilds on every row; on 20 rows the two-year and four-year split leaves a few graduates unclassified and the not-enrolled count is used. The list carries names under counties, no system and no id, so a school joins by a name unique across the state's and DOMO's current spellings, exact then with the generic words removed; a shared name is settled by county through a system-to-county map the unique matches teach; a name contained in one high school's name inside the county (or in one whose system that map has not placed) is the last resort: unique name 286, unique name (loose) 60, shared name, settled by county 11, name contained, inside the county 8, unmatched 7, name contained, system's county unplaced 2. 26 of 26 Leader in Me high schools on the page are reached. Rows no school matched, counted and never guessed: Alabama Virtual Academy at ESC (Barbour County); THE EDGE (Elmore County); Athens Rennaisance School (Limestone County); Kate Duncan Smith Dar High School (Marshall County); Accel Day&Evening Academy (Mobile County); John L Leflore Preparatory Academy (Mobile County); University Charter School 6-12 (Sumter County). One class makes a level, not a trend, so the card shows the point and the pack carries the school values.
  • The 2020-21 accountability file is a closure year. The 2020-21 report-card export carries the chronic absenteeism indicator for 1,338 schools and nothing else (achievement, growth, graduation and CCR are blank on every row), at a statewide 8.27% that reflects a year of remote and quarantined attendance. It stays off every card and row and sits in the research pack, labelled.
  • 2025-26 results come from Alabama's own assessment results and extend grades 3-8 English language arts and math and grade 11 science to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Alabama. The 2025-26 rows (7,129 school-grade-test rows for 1,269 schools: 6,754 grades 3-8 English language arts and math, 375 grade 11 science) were read from the Alabama State Department of Education's participation-and-proficiency files (all students) and joined to the DOMO id through the NCES directory's Alabama system and school code. Proficiency is levels 3 and 4, the figure DOMO carries for Alabama in every earlier year, and the four achievement levels add to 100 on every row, so 2025-26 carries the levels as well. The same schools' 2024-25 and 2025-26 values rank schools alike (r .85 in English language arts and .87 in math) with a median change under half a point, the size of an ordinary year. Grades 4 and 8 science stay at 2024-25: grade 8 science rose a median 12.5 points in one year and grade 4 science 7.6, while the same schools' English language arts and math moved under 4 points, which points to a change in the science test or its cut scores rather than growth; they are added once the state's documentation shows the scale unchanged. Grade 11 English and math results for 2025-26 are not released yet.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 107 surveys across 58 Alabama schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (58 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 29 of the 93 schools counted as Leader in Me in Alabama meet the criteria, and a further 1 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 75 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Alabama publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • SIR incident counts are self-reported and reporting practices vary by system; treat level differences between systems with more caution than trends within one, and never compare incident counts across states.
  • The incident report cannot separate a school with no incidents from one that did not file; a school absent from a year's report is read as zero, which is the report's own convention.
  • Alabama's accountability graduation and CCR indicators are a class behind the report-card year; the page shows them on the class's own year, while DOMO's graduation column follows the class year through 2018-19 and the report-card year from 2021-22 on.
  • The federal report-card supporting data masks the graduation and CCR rate for three high schools in five, the ones with fewer than eleven students on one side, so its published rates are censored by outcome and are not used as a measure.
  • The academic growth indicator is capped at 100 and two schools in five reach the cap, which flattens the mean at the top for both groups.
  • The chronic absenteeism indicator changed count between 2017-18 and 2018-19; its earlier years are excluded by name.
  • The state's college-and-career-ready benchmark list widened over the window and the statewide rate rose from 64% to 88%, so a school's rise in the CCR share is read against that tide; the growth test compares the two groups against the same tide.
  • The ACHE college-going rate covers one class; a level on the card, never a trend, until earlier classes are archived.
  • The state's own file for the newest year does not yet carry every test DOMO carried the year before (2025-26 11th Grade English (374 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade Mathematics (375 rows the year before); 2025-26 4th Grade Science (672 rows the year before); 2025-26 8th Grade Science (432 rows the year before)). Those results usually come out later in the fall. So a school is never compared on one set of tests against a different set, a school that took one of those tests the year before has its newest-year results in that grade band and subject left out (978 rows at 160 schools) until the missing results are released and folded in; the rest of its results, and every earlier year, are unchanged.
