Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 20 Maryland schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on September 30, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 151 of the 220 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 1 inside the grace window stay active; 61 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Maryland's own measures join through the state system and school numbers NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against the state files' own school names and against DOMO's chronic absence; a school with no state number keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Maryland measures.
  • DOMO's chronic absence is the state's own share and stands; the school-years DOMO lacks in years it otherwise carries are filled from the state's file and no DOMO value is overwritten. DOMO's graduation rate is the following year's cohort rate and blank at 95 or above; it is left as it arrived and the state's own four-year cohort rate is carried as its own measure.
  • The attendance rate sits on the rows by band as the state publishes it; a rate the state writes as '95 or above' is read as 95 and counted. State-suppressed cells ('*', 'under 5', 'over 95', 'na') stay missing, never zero.
  • A Maryland School Survey score is the state's 1-to-10 topic score; the overall score is the mean of a school's topics, the state's own construction; students are scored by grade span, the elementary span on the primary band and the middle and high spans on the secondary band.
  • The class of 2016 SAT file and the 2021-22 growth measure are excluded by name; the ACT means and the MLDS outcomes sit in the research pack only; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Maryland card, row and pack record.
  • 2025-26 grades 3-8 reading and math (from the Maryland State Department of Education's 2026 MCAP workbooks) extend the reading-and-math figure; proficiency is levels 3 and 4 and the four levels are carried, with the state's withheld shares filled only where the published shares fix them, as DOMO fills them.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 78 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 1 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start -0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median -0.1), and 31% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 55 pairs (1 unmatched); Secondary: 23 pairs (0 unmatched).

3 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Carver Vocational-Technical High, Catonsville Center for Alternative Studies, Mergenthaler Vocational-Technical High.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 90,179 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 53.3% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.7% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 6.2% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.2% of rows. Read as no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 0.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. Maryland's file reports achievement levels that cannot stand as measures here: no combination of the reported levels reproduces PercentMetStandard on more than 53% of rows (a five-level state whose export carries four columns, usually) (no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate; levels add up on 0.0% of rows). The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Maryland that is 420 Title I schools, 55 charter schools, 92 magnet schools and 29 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Maryland's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English 2, English Language Arts; Math: Algebra, Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Mathematics; Science: Biology, Science; Social Studies: Social Studies.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Maryland publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 220 Leader in Me schools and 20 of 1,159 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 0 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 6.4% of Leader in Me schools and 26.5% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 159 of the 220 roster schools carry a date, and 151 were past it by more than 30 days on September 30, 2026. Exits by year: 2022: 3, 2023: 106, 2024: 19, 2025: 21, 2026: 2. 61 roster schools have no expiration date (44 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 44 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 5.98 against comparison 4.96 (p = 0.682, 7 vs 898 schools), and with them 4.88 against 4.96 (p = 0.923, 48 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Two ids, one school. These comparison ids share a name and city with a Leader in Me school and no tested year, so they are folded into it: Jones Lane Elementary (Gaithersburg); S. Christa McAuliffe Elementary (Germantown).
  • Joining Maryland's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Maryland's files key on the state's two-digit local school system number and four-digit school number instead (Allegany is 01, Flintstone Elementary its 0301), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 1,381 id pairs for 1,370 of the 1,379 schools in Maryland's DOMO records (11 schools carry more than one state number across the years, Garrett County having renumbered its schools in 2019-20, and join under each), including 220 of 220 Leader in Me schools and 1,150 of 1,159 comparison schools; the 9 comparison schools it cannot reach appear in none of NCES's directories from 2016-17 on. The MLDS Center's outcomes workbook carries names only; 261 of its 303 high-school names were joined to state numbers by name within local school system (3 by a prefix, 1 by a name unique in the state); the 42 it could not place are evening, alternative and special centers and schools that closed before the attendance files begin.
  • Chronic absence is the state's own figure; DOMO's graduation rate is a year off and capped. DOMO's chronic absence for Maryland is the state's chronic absentee share to the decimal on every one of the 6,964 school-years both carry, so the core's measure stands as it is; the 47 school-years DOMO lacks in years it otherwise carries (2017-18: 24, 2018-19: 20, 2021-22: 3; 0 of them Leader in Me) are filled from the state's file, and no DOMO value is overwritten. The state's own share by grade band (its Elementary, Middle and High rows) is in the research pack; the state first published it for 2017-18, and the three earlier attendance files carry no chronic-absence column. DOMO's graduation rate is another matter: on the same school-year it is within 0.15 of the state's four-year cohort rate on 4.2% of 640 pairs (median gap 2.2 points), but shifted one year it matches on 100.0% of 428: DOMO's value for a year is the following year's cohort rate (DOMO's 2017-18 is the state's 2018-19; DOMO's 2018-19 matches no year in the folder; DOMO's 2021-22 is the state's 2022-23; DOMO's 2022-23 is the state's 2023-24; DOMO's 2023-24 is the state's 2024-25; DOMO's 2024-25 matches no year in the folder). And DOMO carries a value for 1 of the 282 school-years the state puts at 95 or above (its highest value is 94.9; the state's is 100.0): the report card displays those as "95 or above" and the feed reads them as blank, which drops the best-graduating quarter of high schools from DOMO's measure. Nothing is filled or overwritten there; DOMO's measure is left as it arrived, and the state's own four-year cohort rate is carried as its own measure on the year the class graduated.
  • The attendance rate: by band, with the state's 95 ceiling. The state's attendance files (2014-15 to 2024-25) carry days attended and days of membership for every school, split into Elementary, Middle and High rows for a school that spans them and, from 2017-18, an All row beside them. The rows carry the rate by band (Elementary on the primary band; Middle and High on the secondary band, weighted by students enrolled ninety days or more), so a K-8 school is read on each band it reports, as the tests are. The card and the pack carry the whole-school rate: the state's All row where it publishes one, the single row of a one-band school, and a member-weighted mean of the bands for a combined school in the three earliest years. The state writes any rate of 95 or more as "95 or above" and withholds the days behind it, so the rate is read as 95 there: about 45% of school rows sat at the ceiling in the years through 2020-21 and 10% since, and the closure years are left out of every comparison. The ceiling holds the top down for both groups, so a school that improved into it shows no gain; chronic absence at the top of the page has no such cap. The shares absent more than 20 days and fewer than 5, which the state floors at 5, are in the research pack.
  • The Maryland School Survey: what educators and students say about their school. The state surveys students and educators each spring (students by the accountability span they sit in) and publishes, per school, a 1-to-10 score on each of 11 topics in five domains (Safety: bullying, emotional safety, physical safety, substance abuse; Community: respect for diversity, participation and engagement; Environment: behavioral and academic supports, physical environment; Relationships: staff-student, student-student; and for educators only, Instructional Support: instructional feedback), 10 the most favourable, with the number of respondents; a school appears only where ten or more responded. The files in the folder are the surveys of 2019, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025: the first survey was spring 2019, none was given in 2020 or 2021, and every year carries the same topics on the same scale, so each is drawn on its school year (the spring 2025 survey on 2024-25). The overall score on the card and in the rows is the mean of a school's topic scores, which is the state's own construction: the accountability detail's "School Survey" measure equals that mean within 0.06 on 99.7% of 14,219 school-spans, which is also why a higher score is the favourable direction on every topic, bullying and substance abuse included (the state awards more points for it). Students are scored by grade span; in the rows the elementary span sits on the primary band and the middle and high spans on the secondary band, and on the card and in the pack a school is the respondent-weighted mean of its spans. Four topics per respondent group are on the card, chosen as the ones a field person can read and act on; every topic is in the research pack. The student Bullying topic stays off the card: its statewide mean fell from 4.44 in 2022 to 3.68 in 2023 and the share of schools at the floor of 1 went from 8% to 20%, a step the files do not explain, so it is not drawn as one line. On the overall educator score the Leader in Me line runs from 2019 (4 schools) to 2025 (70 schools), against 1,081 comparison schools in 2025.
  • The accountability points are the number behind the star, and a few schools inherit a star. Maryland's report card rates every school one to five stars from the share of accountability points it earned; the share is on the rows and the card, and the star with it in the research pack. The framework's measures have shifted between years (the school survey entered in 2018-19; the 2021-22 file's growth measure is a continuous "Student Growth" figure where every other year carries the median student growth percentile, so that year's growth is excluded by name from the pack's growth columns), and no ratings were issued for the closure years, so the line jumps from 2018-19 to 2021-22. 64 school-years in the 2023-24 and 2024-25 files belong to schools with no tested grades that take the star of a paired school and earn no points of their own; they carry neither here. The 2023-24 and 2024-25 star files label the school-number and school-name columns the wrong way round; they are read by content. The 2017-18 and 2018-19 detail files store rates as fractions and the later files as percentages; both are read to a percent.
  • The postsecondary files: the class's own year, the SAT's first redesigned class out, the ACT in the pack. College enrollment is the state's National Student Clearinghouse match: the share of each high school's graduates enrolled anywhere within 12, 16 and 24 months, by graduating class (2015 to 2024; the newest classes have no 24-month figure yet), with the published share rebuilding from the file's own counts on every row; the 12-month share is on the rows and the card. The college readiness files carry the SAT and ACT means of each school's test-takers and the AP exams taken and passed. The class of 2016 SAT file is excluded by name: it is the redesigned SAT's first class, 59 of 209 high schools carry a mean, no school has more than 65 test-takers, and the statewide mean sits at 909.8 against 1036.1 the next year. The ACT means are in the research pack only: from the class of 2021 the state withholds most schools' means (class of 2021: 89 of 172 schools; class of 2022: 83 of 178 schools; class of 2023: 86 of 179 schools; class of 2024: 75 of 170 schools; class of 2025: 63 of 163 schools). The MLDS Center's outcomes workbook (the 2025 release) follows each graduating class through college and the Maryland workforce: the share enrolled full-time the fall after graduation, the share enrolled at any point since (reported once three years have passed), the share of enrollees with a degree by 25 and median quarterly wages at 25, for the classes of 2007-08 to 2023-24 where each has come of age. It is an aggregate suppressed table keyed on names, so it joins by name and sits in the research pack rather than on the card; its "immediate" share is a narrower measure than the Clearinghouse 12-month share and the two are labelled apart.
  • Suspensions are published as PDF only. Maryland publishes its suspension counts by school and offense category as PDF tables (in-school and out-of-school suspensions and expulsions, 2015-16 to 2024-25) and no data file, so no discipline measure is on this page; the PDFs were not staged. The Maryland School Survey's bullying and physical-safety topics and the student-student relationships topic are the nearest reading of behaviour the state publishes as data.
  • 2025-26 reading and math come from Maryland's own MCAP results and extend the reading-and-math figure to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Maryland. The 2025-26 rows (7,186 school-grade-subject rows for 1,109 schools) were read from the Maryland State Department of Education's 2026 MCAP English language arts and mathematics workbooks (the school-level sheet, all students, grades 3 to 8, created July 16, 2026) and joined to the DOMO id through the NCES directory's Maryland state id, the system and school numbers every state file carries. Proficiency is the state's own proficient share (levels 3 and 4), and the four achievement levels are the state's Level 1 to Level 4 shares, level 1 the lowest. This is what DOMO carries for Maryland in every earlier year: all 7,160 of DOMO's 2024-25 Maryland grade 3-8 reading and math rows are one row of the state's own 2024-25 workbook, and proficiency and all four levels agree to the tenth of a point on every row that carries them. The state writes a share under 5% as "5 or below", a share over 95% as "95 or above", and withholds a grade with fewer than ten testers. As DOMO does, a withheld share is filled only where the published shares fix it (the four levels add to 100, and proficiency is levels 3 and 4), a remainder of 0.3 of a point or less is read as 0, and a share the published ones do not fix is left blank; 6,822 rows carry all four levels. A grade whose proficiency stays unknown carries no 2025-26 row. Maryland kept the MCAP tests and their cut scores in 2025-26 and reports the year on the same scale as the earlier MCAP years, so 2025-26 extends the trend.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 173 surveys across 124 Maryland schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (124 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 6 of the 50 schools counted as Leader in Me in Maryland meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. A school in its third year is measured from its baseline year; a school already in the process is measured from the start of the window.
Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

70 measures were screened, grouped into 23 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 61 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Maryland publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • The state writes any attendance rate of 95 or more as '95 or above', so the attendance measure is capped at 95 for both groups and a school that improved into the ceiling shows no gain; chronic absence has no such cap.
  • The Maryland School Survey is published only for schools with ten or more respondents and was not given in 2020 or 2021, so the survey lines rest on spring 2019 and spring 2022 on; the student Bullying topic steps down in 2023 for reasons the files do not give and is not drawn.
  • The accountability points fold together measures whose weights the state has adjusted between years, so the points are a level to describe, never a growth claim of their own.
  • The SAT, ACT and AP figures are means and shares among the graduates who tested, which travel with who sits the test; the counts are in the pack.
  • Maryland publishes suspensions as PDF tables only, so no discipline measure is on this page.
