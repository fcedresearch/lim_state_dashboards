Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 31 Ohio schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 109 of the 266 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 1 inside the grace window stay active; 46 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Ohio's own measures join through the building IRN NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against the report card's own names within district and against DOMO's chronic absence, graduation rate and enrollment; a school with no IRN keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Ohio measures.
  • Chronic absence and the graduation rate are DOMO's own where DOMO has them (the state's figures to the decimal); the overview files fill only the years DOMO lacks, and no DOMO value is overwritten.
  • Attendance, the Performance Index (2021-22 scale), one-year value-added and the discipline rates are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports; the state's withheld cells stay missing, a withheld discipline reason counts as none, and an attendance rate of exactly zero is read as unreported.
  • The 2014-15 value-added score, the 2021-22 and 2022-23 readiness component and the 2019-20 Prepared for Success column are excluded by name; the two Performance Index scales, the two value-added poolings and the letter-grade and star schemes are kept apart under their own names; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Ohio card, row and pack record.
  • 2025-26 school results from SchoolDigger's bulk file (the source of DOMO's subject file), read exactly as DOMO reads earlier years, extend each test to the newest year.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 192 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 5 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 39% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 110 pairs (3 unmatched); Secondary: 82 pairs (2 unmatched).

51 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Akron Alternative Academy, Alternative Education Academy, Barber Community Learning Center, Berwick Alternative K-8 School, Betty Jane Community Learning Center, Buchtel Community Learning Center, Case Community Learning Center, Cassady Alternative Elementary School, Cedarwood Alternative Elementary School, Columbus Alternative High School, David Hill Community Learning Center, Devonshire Alternative Elementary School, Duxberry Park Alternative Elementary School, East Community Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 257,304 rows. Ohio's file carries no level percentages at all, so the definition cannot be checked against the levels and the percent-in-the-lowest-level measure has no data here.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. Ohio's file carries no level percentages. The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Ohio that is 2,169 Title I schools, 387 charter schools, 0 magnet schools and 0 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Ohio's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English I, English II, English Language Arts, English Language Arts I, English Language Arts II, Reading; Math: Algebra I, Geometry, Integrated Math I, Integrated Math II, Mathematics; Social Studies: American Government, American History, Social Studies; Science: Biology, Physical Science, Science; Writing: Writing.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Ohio publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 3 of 266 Leader in Me schools and 414 of 3,085 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 379 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 25.2% of Leader in Me schools and 26.2% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 220 of the 266 roster schools carry a date, and 109 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 2, 2021: 6, 2022: 9, 2023: 19, 2024: 32, 2025: 24, 2026: 17. 46 roster schools have no expiration date (32 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 32 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 4.37 against comparison 4.59 (p = 0.784, 104 vs 2570 schools), and with them 5.16 against 4.59 (p = 0.444, 130 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2014-15 to 2015-16, 74.0 to 57.0 (-17.0). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Two ids, one school. These comparison ids share a name and city with a Leader in Me school and no tested year, so they are folded into it: Stevenson Elementary School (Heath).
  • Joining Ohio's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Ohio's files key on the state's six-digit building IRN instead (Ada Elementary is 000059), which shares no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: one id pair for each of 3,331 of the 3,351 schools on this page, and 18 schools NCES never listed (closed before its first directory year in the folder, 2016-17) joined by an exact name within district, such as Lake Erie Academy (Lake Erie Academy); Osborn Intermediate School Central (Ashland City); Fairmount Elementary School (Canton City); Timken High School (Canton City), including 266 of 266 Leader in Me schools and 3,083 of 3,085 comparison schools. The map was checked a second way, against the report card's own building names, which it was not built from: they agree with DOMO's name on 3,342 of 3,349 crosswalked schools, and the district on 3,303 of 3,333 (7 read one by one: buildings renamed or repurposed since NCES listed them, none of them Leader in Me schools).   • Attendance, the Performance Index and one-year value-added growth are measures, not only levels. Each is a per-school rate, so it runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), carried on each band a school reports as the core carries chronic absence. The attendance rate is each overview file's own-year column through 2020-21 and the attendance details from 2021-22 (an overview file repeats the two earlier years' rates as well; from 2015-16 on those repeats match the earlier file to the decimal and are not read, and the 2014-15 file's repeats and the 2016-17 file's two-years-back column do not match and are not read either); 4 rates of exactly zero are read as unreported. The Performance Index is on the rows only on the scale the state adopted with the 2021-22 report card, because the redesign changed the weights per level and let a school pass 100 (103 building-years above 100 from 2021-22, none before; the same schools correlate at 0.93 across the redesign, 2018-19 to 2021-22, against 0.97 and 0.98 between adjacent years within an era): the 2014-15 to 2018-19 index is its own line on the card and its own column in the pack. Value-added on the rows is the one-year overall effect size in hundredths of a standard deviation (the published figure times 100, so that a change the page rounds to one decimal still reads), 2021-22 to 2024-25, from the one-year gains sheets: the report card's own composite is a two-year pool in 2022-23 and a three-year pool from 2023-24 (a wider pool shrinks the standard error and inflates the index), and the 2015-16 to 2018-19 composite behaves as a multi-year pool too (adjacent years correlate at 0.89, 0.94, 0.85 for the same schools, against 0.61, 0.64, 0.65 for the one-year composites), so both sit in the research pack under their own names. The 2014-15 value-added score is excluded by name: for the same schools it correlates at -0.26 with the 2015-16 composite, a different statistic from a different test.
  • Suspension occurrences are a floor, and the page says so. Ohio's building discipline files count occurrences by discipline type for each of sixteen reasons and withhold any cell under ten (buildings with every out-of-school suspension reason withheld: 1,602 of 3,418 in 2017-18; 1,656 of 3,384 in 2018-19; 1,787 of 3,408 in 2021-22; 1,700 of 3,372 in 2022-23; 1,702 of 3,342 in 2023-24). The rate here is the published occurrences summed over the reasons, per 100 students of the building's enrollment that year, with a withheld reason counting as none: every figure is a floor, exact for a school whose suspensions sit in a few well-populated reasons and an understatement for a small school whose few suspensions never reach ten in any one reason. The research pack carries the number of withheld reasons and the most they could add per 100 for each school-year. The denominator is the report card's own enrollment rather than DOMO's because the DOMO export carries one Student Count per school, repeated in every year (3,265 of 3,265 Ohio schools with two or more years; one high school reads 1 student in every year); that count is the state's 2024-25 enrollment, which is why the page treats it as a trait, and it sits beside the state's count in the pack. Out-of-school suspensions and all discipline occurrences (expulsions, both suspensions, in-school alternate discipline, emergency and hearing removals) are measures at the top of the page; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are left off (buildings closed from March 2020; the 2024-25 file is not yet posted), and 0 building-years with occurrences but no usable enrollment carry no rate.
  • The readiness component was re-specified in 2023-24, and two years are excluded by name. The College, Career, Workforce and Military Readiness percent is on the card from 2023-24 only. Its technical-assessment pathway (three or more technical assessments passed) averages 0.0% and 0.0% of the cohort in 2021-22 and 2022-23, zero for every school, and 25.47% in 2023-24; for the 784 high schools in both years the mean moves from 40.0 to 58.8 and 72% rise by more than ten points. The first two years sit in the research pack under their own name. The Prepared for Success component that preceded it (2015-16 to 2018-19) is a different measure and sits in the pack; its 2019-20 column reads zero for 3,353 buildings including every school without a graduating class, a file defect, and is excluded by name. College enrollment within two years is the same National Student Clearinghouse measure in every file, so it runs from the class of 2015 to the class of 2022 on each class's own year; the six-year completion share is in the pack.
  • Letter grades and stars are two schemes. Ohio graded components A-F through 2018-19 (an overall grade in 2017-18 and 2018-19 only), rated nothing in 2019-20 and 2020-21, rated components with stars from 2021-22 and gave an overall star rating from 2022-23. The overall stars are on the card from 2022-23; the letter grades are in the research pack as points (A=4 to F=0) under their own names and never join the star series.
  • 2025-26 results come from SchoolDigger's bulk file, the same source as DOMO's subject file for every earlier year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Ohio. DOMO's subject file is SchoolDigger's test-score table, so the 2025-26 rows (23,458 school-grade-test rows for 3,024 schools) were read from SchoolDigger's newest bulk file for FranklinCovey in the same layout: the percent meeting the state's standard and the first four achievement levels, with SchoolDigger's grade and test labels. The same file's 2024-25 rows reproduce DOMO's: 100.0% of DOMO's 23,833 Ohio school rows for that year are in it, and the proficiency figure is identical on 100.0% of them.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 352 surveys across 136 Ohio schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 13 answered by students, 12 answered by staff, 11 answered by more than one group (blended), 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (136 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 24 of the 139 schools counted as Leader in Me in Ohio meet the criteria, and a further 5 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

58 measures were screened, grouped into 21 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 46 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Ohio publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Ohio withholds any discipline cell under ten occurrences, reason by reason, so the suspension rates are floors: exact for a school whose suspensions sit in a few well-populated reasons, understated for a small school whose few suspensions never reach ten in any one reason. The pack carries the number of withheld reasons per school-year.
  • The Performance Index was rebuilt with the 2021-22 report card (new weights, a scale that passes 100), so the two eras are two series and the rows carry only the current one.
  • The report card's own value-added composite pools two years in 2022-23 and three from 2023-24, and its 2015-16 to 2018-19 composite behaves as a multi-year pool; the one-year effect size is the only value-added figure the rows carry.
  • The readiness component counts a pathway from 2023-24 that was zero for every school before, so its first two years stand apart; college enrollment arrives two years after each class graduates, so its latest class is 2022.
  • The attendance rate is compressed (most schools between 90 and 97), so small differences carry more weight than they would on a wider measure.
  • Ohio's test file carries no percent in the lowest level values, so that measure is not offered on this page.
