Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 9 Washington schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 9 of the 44 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 14 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • 1 roster schools also appear in the comparison export in years after their roster rows end, which is DOMO's own record that they left; they count as Leader in Me through their last roster year and in neither group after.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Washington's own measures join through the district and school codes NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited by name within district and against DOMO's graduation and proficiency; a school with no state code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Washington measures.
  • Chronic absence is 100 minus the state's regular attendance rate (students missing fewer than 10% of enrolled days), rebuilt from each year's numerator and denominator; DOMO carries none for Washington, so nothing is overwritten. The growth median (the mean of the English language arts and mathematics medians) is a whole-school measure carried on each band a school reports.
  • The exclusionary discipline rate is masked under three excluded students, so the rows never carry it; the card carries the share of schools at 3% or more, known from a published rate or a ceiling at or under 3%. State-masked cells stay missing, never zero.
  • The School Improvement Framework is keyed on the school year its data ends in, and its three-year pooled graduation and attendance rates fill nothing; the graduate outcomes' class of 2025 first-year enrollment (first fall only), the pooled earnings file and the by-sector companion are excluded by name; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Washington card and row.
  • 2025-26 Smarter Balanced English language arts and math (grades 3-8 and 10) and WCAS science (grades 5, 8 and 11), from the Office of Superintendent of Public Instruction's Report Card Assessment Data, extend each subject to the newest year; proficiency is the share of students tested at level 3 or 4, as in prior years, and a row the state leaves blank carries no value.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 30 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 1 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 53% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 24 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 6 pairs (1 unmatched).

5 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Okanogan Outreach Alternative School, Partnership for Excellence in Alternative Remote Learning, Renaissance Alternative High School, Stillaguamish Valley Learning Center, Woodland Alternative School.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 155,470 rows. Washington's file carries no level percentages at all, so the definition cannot be checked against the levels and the percent-in-the-lowest-level measure has no data here.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. Washington's file carries no level percentages. The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Washington that is 988 Title I schools, 19 charter schools, 47 magnet schools and 210 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Washington's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English Language Arts; Math: Math; Science: Biology, Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Washington publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 44 Leader in Me schools and 4 of 2,114 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 3 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 18.2% of Leader in Me schools and 12.8% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 30 of the 44 roster schools carry a date, and 9 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2021: 1, 2022: 5, 2024: 3. 14 roster schools have no expiration date (11 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 11 schools the headline reads Leader in Me -2.23 against comparison -0.02 (p = 0.364, 13 vs 1833 schools), and with them 0.39 against -0.02 (p = 0.808, 23 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2014-15 to 2015-16, 50.5 to 58.9 (+8.5). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Joining Washington's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Washington's files key on OSPI's five-digit district code and four-digit school code instead (Clover Park is 27400, Tillicum Elementary its school 2651), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school (its state school id is WA, the district code and the school code, one to one and unchanged in every directory year from 2016-17 to 2023-24), so that is the anchor: 2,153 id pairs for 2,153 of the 2,158 schools on this page, including 44 of 44 Leader in Me schools and 2,109 of 2,114 comparison schools; 2,153 of them appear in at least one Washington file (some files drop the district code's leading zero, which is put back). The map was checked a second way, by name within district, which it was not built from: the state files' own school name agrees with DOMO's on 2,143 of 2,153 crosswalked schools (10 read one by one: renamed schools, abbreviations and programs listed under another name), and a name-within-district join from DOMO's name and district to the state files' names lands on the same state id for 2,045 schools, on another id for 1, on two or more for 58 and on nothing for 49, never on a Leader in Me school.   • Chronic absence on this page is Washington's own, as 100 minus regular attendance; DOMO carries none.   • The growth median is a measure, not only a level. The state masks schools under ten students; the 2024-25 file prints student counts in its percent columns, so the low, typical and high growth shares are rebuilt from the counts in every year. The framework's own growth medians are a different, three-year figure and stay in the research pack under their own names.
  • Discipline is a share of schools, and the rate itself stays off the rows. The Report Card discipline files (2014-15 to 2024-25) carry the exclusionary discipline rate, the share of a school's students suspended or expelled at least once, all grades; suspension and expulsion are not split. The state masks the rate wherever fewer than three students were excluded and prints only a ceiling ('<0.73%' is fewer than three of 411 students), so the published rate exists for 14,991 of 25,898 school-years and a ceiling for 10,376; the rate is known for the higher-discipline schools and missing for the lowest, and a school that came down from five excluded students to two would vanish rather than register a fall, so the rate cannot feed a growth test honestly and sits in the research pack with its ceiling. The card carries what the masking leaves knowable: whether 3% or more of a school's students were excluded, known for 22,499 of 25,898 school-years (a published rate is read directly, a ceiling at or under 3% means under, and a ceiling above 3%, which only a school under a hundred students can have, is unknown). The bar sits above the typical elementary school and below the typical secondary school. The published rate rebuilds from the file's own numerator and denominator on every row checked (14,991 of 14,991). The 2020-21 file carries a rate for 290 schools, the remote year; it and 2019-20 stay off every card and row. On the card the Leader in Me line runs from 2015 (6 schools) to 2025 (22 schools).
  • The School Improvement Framework is a level, keyed on the year its data ends in; its own rates fill nothing. The Washington School Improvement Framework is the state's ESSA accountability rating: every school's deciles on proficiency, growth, graduation, English-learner progress, regular attendance, ninth graders on track and dual credit, combined into a score of 1 to 10 and a support tier. The folder carries the runs of 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025; there was no run for 2020 or 2021. Each run is shown on the school year its data ends in (the 2017 run on 2016-17, the 2025 annual run on 2024-25), which the files' own attendance denominators bear out: they sum to about three million students, three times the state's enrollment, so the framework's rates are three-year pooled figures ending in the run year. That is why the framework's four-year graduation rate agrees with DOMO's annual rate only loosely (median difference 1.8 points, 31.7% within a point) and its regular attendance with the annual file at r = 0.877 with a median gap of 2.86 points: the same schools, a different window. So the framework's rates fill nothing at the top of the page; DOMO's graduation rate stands untouched, and the framework's own figures sit in the research pack under their own names. The score is the weighted mean of the school's indicator deciles (a decimal in every run); the 2022 run's other-academic decile also weighed average achievement level beside growth, and the identification rules and tier names changed between runs, so the card's line is read as a level within a run and never as a trend across runs. In the 2025 run the state placed (in its own tier names) 1,326 schools in Foundational, 536 schools in Tier 1: Targeted 1-2, 179 schools in Tier 2: Targeted 3+, 125 schools in Tier 3: Comprehensive.
  • Ninth graders on track, dual credit, the Graduation Pathways and the Education Research and Data Center's graduate outcomes are high-school families. The School Quality or Student Success file also carries, per high school, the share of ninth graders who earned every credit attempted and the share of students in a dual-credit course (2014-15 to 2024-25); the Graduation Pathways file carries the share of each four-year cohort completing each pathway under the state's 2019 graduation pathways law, House Bill 1599 (classes of 2020 to 2025; 17,342 published rates, the rest masked as N<10 or top/bottom range, and the published rate rebuilds from the file's counts on 9,140 of 9,140 rows); and the Education Research and Data Center's graduate outcomes carry, per high school and graduating class, first-year college enrollment by level (classes of 2005 to 2025), persistence into a second year, degree completion six years on and remedial coursetaking, each keyed on the graduating class's year. Washington's Leader in Me schools are almost all elementary schools: fewer than three Leader in Me high schools carry any of these while counted, so they sit in the research pack and the school list rather than on the card or the rows. The graduate outcomes files publish a three-class pooled figure for small schools (17,977 rows), carried apart under its own name; the class of 2025 first-year enrollment counts the first fall only, a shorter window than every earlier class, and is excluded by name (1,554 rows); the earnings file is pooled across every graduating class and has no year to sit on, and the by-sector companion is a finer split of the same first-year figure, so neither is carried (2 files). Redacted cells (the state prints a band such as 0-5 in place of the value) stay missing.
  • 2025-26 results come from Washington's own Smarter Balanced and WCAS file and extend English language arts and math (grades 3-8 and 10) and science (grades 5, 8 and 11) to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Washington. The 2025-26 rows (18,077 school-grade-subject rows for 2,006 schools) were read from the Office of Superintendent of Public Instruction's Report Card Assessment Data for 2025-26 (data.wa.gov, all students, school rows) and joined to the DOMO id through the NCES directory's Washington state id, the same join as the state's other files on this page. They cover every test DOMO carried for Washington in 2024-25: Smarter Balanced English language arts and math in grades 3-8 and 10, and the Washington Comprehensive Assessment of Science (WCAS) in grades 5, 8 and 11, and each school's all-grades row, which the page reads only where a school has no grade row in a subject. Proficiency is the share of students tested who scored at level 3 or 4 (OSPI's "consistent grade-level knowledge and above"), the figure DOMO carries; the four levels are shares of students expected to test, as in DOMO's earlier Washington years, so they add to the participation rate rather than to 100, which is why this page offers no level measures for Washington in any year. Read this way, OSPI's September 2025 release of 2024-25 reproduces all 18,591 of DOMO's 18,591 Washington rows for that year, the proficiency figure equal to two decimals on 100% of them and the four levels likewise, so DOMO read that release. OSPI's September 2026 revision of 2024-25, in the same layout as the 2025-26 file and read by the same code, gives a value on 17,835 of DOMO's rows and equals DOMO's figure on 99.7% of them and within half a point on 99.9% (where they differ, mostly grade 11 science and grade 10, the revision counts the same students at level 3 or above over fewer students tested). WA-AIM, the alternate assessment, is not read, as DOMO does not carry it. OSPI suppresses small and extreme results. Of the 22,969 school rows for these tests in 2025-26, it prints nothing on 4,637 (fewer than ten students 2,235, no students 1,019, and results near the top or bottom of the range 1,383), and it prints the shares but not the counts on 2,264 more (so that the blank rows cannot be worked out from the others), which are read as printed. A blank row carries no value and no midpoint is used: the row itself prints no number, and the few that subtracting a school's other grades from its all-grades total would expose are left as the state left them. The top-or-bottom rule, as OSPI applies it in its September 2026 files, blanks mostly high-scoring results: in 2024-25 the 568 grade rows that OSPI's revision blanks this way averaged 73% proficient in DOMO's values, against 47% for the rest, and leaving them out lowers the 2024-25 statewide average of school results in grades 3-8 by 1.2 points in English language arts and 1.1 in math (0.5 and 0.8 for Leader in Me schools, which have fewer such rows). DOMO's 2024-25 values come from the first release, which printed those rows, so about a point of any statewide fall from 2024-25 to 2025-26 is this rule, and a school whose 2025-26 result is blanked drops out of that year's comparisons. The 2025-26 rows reach 1,968 schools against 1,948 in DOMO's 2024-25 rows at the same grades and tests, and 12,325 grade rows against 12,853 (96%); OSPI's revision of 2024-25 reaches 12,366 (96%), so the gap is the new suppression, not lost schools. Schools opened after the newest NCES directory (2023-24) have no federal id yet and carry no 2025-26 row (26 schools).

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 31 surveys across 12 Washington schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 13 answered by students, 12 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build 2 surveys were a second survey in a school-year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (12 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 3 of the 24 schools counted as Leader in Me in Washington meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

48 measures were screened, grouped into 13 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 14 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Washington publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Chronic absence on this page is the complement of Washington's regular attendance rate, not DOMO's measure, and it is missing for the small schools the state masks and for a school with no test row or growth median to carry it in a year.
  • Washington's exclusionary discipline rate is masked wherever fewer than three students were excluded, so the rate is known only for the higher-discipline schools; the page carries the share of schools at 3% or more instead, and suspension and expulsion are never split.
  • The growth median has no file for 2019-20 to 2021-22, so a growth-test window that spans those years compares medians three years apart.
  • The School Improvement Framework score pools most indicators over three years and its rules changed between runs; it is a level, described, never tested, and its own rates are not the annual ones.
  • Washington's Leader in Me schools are almost all elementary schools, so the high-school families (ninth graders on track, dual credit, the Graduation Pathways and the Education Research and Data Center's graduate outcomes) describe at most two Leader in Me high schools and sit in the research pack.
  • Washington's 2025-26 file leaves blank the results near the top or bottom of the range (a suppression rule as the state applies it in its September 2026 files), while DOMO's 2024-25 values, from the state's first release of that year, still carry them. Those rows are mostly high scorers, so 2025-26 statewide averages in grades 3-8 run about 1.2 points lower than they would with them (less for Leader in Me schools), and a school whose 2025-26 result is blanked drops out of that year's comparisons.
  • Washington's test file carries no percent in the lowest level values, so that measure is not offered on this page.
