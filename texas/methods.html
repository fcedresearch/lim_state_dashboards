Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results count as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before. A launch from April 1 through September 15 starts that coming school year, which is Year 1. A launch from September 16 through March 31 comes after the year was underway: that year is Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and the next year is Year 1. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day. An August 2023 launch counts from 2023-24; an October 2024 launch has 2024-25 as its Year 0 and counts from 2025-26.
  • A school's baseline is its last spring test before launch. A Leader in Me school's years before its launch count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time; its Year 0 never does.
  • 28 school-years on this page are a Year 0.
  • 19 Texas schools on the roster carry no start date. Their launch cannot be placed, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 06, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 267 of the 636 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 7 inside the grace window stay active; 97 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • 5 roster schools also appear in the comparison export in years after their roster rows end, which is DOMO's own record that they left; they count as Leader in Me through their last roster year and in neither group after.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Texas's own measures join through the TEA campus number NCES's directory carries beside the federal id (one to one in Texas), audited against the state's own campus and district names and against DOMO's dropout rate (TEA's own figure); a school with no campus number keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Texas measures.
  • TAPR columns are decoded by the reference workbooks for 2019 to 2023 and by the state's own prior-year chain elsewhere; a column the chain cannot confirm, or that is a different measure under the same code, is excluded by name. The 2024 file's college-enrollment column is coded a year off and the 2025 file's is read instead. TEA's sentinels are read as missing, never zero.
  • Chronic absence is TEA's own campus rate for every year, because the DOMO records carry none for Texas; nothing is overwritten. The attendance rate is a whole-school figure carried on each band a school reports; the grade 9-12 dropout rate, CCMR and Texas college enrollment sit on the secondary band, the last two keyed on the graduating class.
  • The accountability rating is a category, carried as the share of rated schools at A or B (2018-19 on, with 'Not Rated: SB 1365' read as evaluated and below B) and at Met Standard (2015-16 to 2017-18); the overall and academic growth scores are carried as two measures on either side of the 2023 refresh and never joined or put on the rows.
  • Discipline is read from TEA's campus summaries with a masked count (one to nine students) as missing and a missing heading as zero; the card carries shares of schools at a threshold the mask leaves knowable, and the rates, counts and mask flags sit in the research pack.

Judgement calls we had to make

The places the rules needed a threshold. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

Launch year (Year 0 and Year 1)
September 15 (decided October 6, 2026). A school that launched from April 1 through September 15 counts that coming school year as its Year 1; its baseline is the spring test before launch. A school that launched from September 16 through March 31 has that school year as its Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and counts from the next school year. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day (most of the Middle Atlantic and, by law, most of Minnesota). The line replaced July 1, which put June and August launches for the same school year a full year apart.
School year boundary for exits
July 1. An expiration in June belongs to the year that just ended; one in July belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 374 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 0 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 10% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 275 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 99 pairs (0 unmatched).

39 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Alice Carlson Applied Learning Center, Brazoria County Juvenile Detention, Carver Alter Learning Center, Cesar Chavez Learning Center, Charles Rice Learning Center, Cooke/Fannin/Grayson County Juvenile Post Ajud Facility, Dallas County Juvenile Justice, Edna Alternative, Eladio R Martinez Learning Center, Endeavor High School-Joe Frank Campbell Learning Center, Galena Park Independent School District (Isd) Career & Technical Echs, Galveston County Detention Center, Garza County Regional Juvenile Center, Harold T Branch Academy For Career & Technical Educational.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 521,670 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 91.1% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 10.6% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.4% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 2.2% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 91.0% of rows; the years and subjects that do not add up (2015-16 ELA (end-of-course), 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math (end-of-course), 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science (end-of-course), 2015-16 Social Studies (end-of-course)) carry no level values.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Texas's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 91.0% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values: 2015-16 ELA (end-of-course), 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math (end-of-course), 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science (end-of-course), 2015-16 Social Studies (end-of-course)), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard (Did Not Meet Grade Level, Approaches Grade Level, Meets Grade Level, Masters Grade Level in Texas's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Texas that is 5,946 Title I schools, 1,108 charter schools, 264 magnet schools and 569 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Texas's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English I Reading, English II Reading, Reading; Math: Algebra I, Mathematics; Science: Biology, Science; Social Studies: Social Studies, U.S. History; Writing: Writing.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Texas publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 7 of 636 Leader in Me schools and 148 of 8,175 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 34 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 46.4% of Leader in Me schools and 41.8% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 539 of the 636 roster schools carry a date, and 267 were past it by more than 30 days on October 06, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 1, 2020: 3, 2021: 14, 2022: 50, 2023: 47, 2024: 41, 2025: 49, 2026: 62. 97 roster schools have no expiration date (84 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 84 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 1.42 against comparison 2.57 (p = 0.037, 280 vs 6803 schools), and with them 1.8 against 2.57 (p = 0.128, 346 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2015-16 to 2016-17, 71.1 to 41.6 (-29.4); Math 2015-16 to 2016-17, 75.8 to 46.3 (-29.5). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Two ids, one school. These comparison ids share a name and city with a Leader in Me school and no tested year, so they are folded into it: Godley Middle (Godley); Joslin Elementary (Austin); Snyder J H (Snyder).
  • Same name, two schools. These pairs share a name and city but report in the same years, so they are kept apart: Parkview Elementary (Fort Worth); Smith Elementary (Houston); Smith Elementary (Houston); Thompson Elementary (Houston); Wilson Elementary (Houston).
  • Texas's own files join by campus number, through NCES. The DOMO id is the federal school id; every TEA file keys on the nine-digit campus number (the district's six digits and the campus's three), and no Texas file carries both. The NCES Common Core of Data directory lists the campus number beside the federal id for every Texas public school, and in Texas the two are paired one to one in every year (8,778 pairs for 8,775 of the 8,811 schools on this page; 636 of 636 Leader in Me schools). The join is checked a second way against the state's own names: TEA's campus name agrees with DOMO's on 8,740 of 8,769 crosswalked schools (the rest are renamed campuses, Bishop Arts STEAM Academy for John H. Reagan Elementary) and the district on 8,722, and independently against a DOMO column the map was not built from: DOMO's dropout rate is TEA's own annual dropout rate, identical to the hundredth on 56.1% of 11,396 high-school school-years and correlated 0.984 overall (DOMO carries the combined 7-12 rate for combined-grade schools). Three pairs were re-pointed to an id the core folded. 36 schools on the page have no campus number and keep their DOMO measures only.
  • The TAPR columns are decoded by workbook, and every other year by the state's own prior-year chain. The Texas Academic Performance Report downloads name their columns in code (CA0AT18R is the all-students attendance rate for 2017-18). The five reference workbooks in the folder decode the 2019 to 2023 files; the 2025 all-campus downloads carry their labels in a first header row; and the 2015 to 2018 and 2024 files are read only where the same column is confirmed by the chain of prior-year columns every TAPR carries: the same measure-year read from two consecutive files must agree campus by campus, which 128 measure-years do (the exception is a revision: CCMR for the class of 2022 identical on 76.7% and within a point on 98.1%, and the later file's figure is kept). Where the chain breaks the column is excluded by name: the 2015 to 2017 files' CA0GP column, which later years use for CCMR, averages in the seventies against fifty for the first real CCMR class and no later file repeats it, so it is a different measure; the pre-2018 TSI, dual-credit and SAT/ACT-criterion columns, whose definitions changed with the 2018 system; and the 2017 SAT and ACT averages, which sit in one file no later file confirms. The class of 2017 CCMR sits in one file too, but the 2018 accountability file computes the same rate independently and agrees within half a point on 97.3% of 1,734 high schools, so it is kept. The 2024 TAPR's college-enrollment column is coded as the class of 2021 but holds the class of 2022, campus for campus what the 2025 file prints under the right label, so that file's column is excluded and the 2025 file's read. 32 downloads were read; TEA's sentinels (35,524 masked cells, 2,197 not applicable, 2,013,321 blank) are missing, never zero. The STAAR School Progress sets were not read: their all-subjects growth rate is the accountability file's academic growth score to the decimal on every campus checked in 2018, 2019 and 2022.
  • Attendance, dropout, CCMR and Texas college enrollment are measures, not only levels. Each is a per-school rate, so it runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test). The attendance rate (2013-14 to 2023-24) is a whole-school figure carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence; the grade 9-12 annual dropout rate (2013-14 to 2023-24), CCMR (classes of 2017 to 2024) and Texas college enrollment (classes of 2013 to 2022, keyed on the graduating class; the state counts Texas public and independent institutions only, so an out-of-state enrollee is not in it) sit on the secondary band. attendance rate reaches 470 Leader in Me schools, dropout 912 reaches 60 Leader in Me schools, ccmr reaches 57 Leader in Me schools, college enroll tx reaches 38 Leader in Me schools. The TAPR reports attendance a year in arrears, so the newest attendance and chronic absence describe 2023-24. CCMR is the share of a class's graduates who met at least one of TEA's college, career or military readiness indicators; TEA has added indicators since the class of 2017 (OnRamps, then aligned CTE coursework and Level I and II certificates), so each class is read under the rule of its year, which is how the state reports it. 9,504 of 24,013 grade 9-12 dropout values are exact zeros (small schools with no dropouts) and stay as zeros; 235 attendance values of exactly 100 sit on tiny campuses and stay.
  • Chronic absence is the state's own rate, filling every year DOMO has none. The SchoolDigger records carry no chronic-absence value for any Texas school-year, so there is nothing to reconcile: TEA's campus chronic-absenteeism rate (students missing 10% or more of their enrolled days, the same definition DOMO uses elsewhere), published in the TAPR from 2018-19, fills the measure on 31,736 school-years (2018-19: 7,733, 2021-22: 7,977, 2022-23: 8,010, 2023-24: 8,016), never overwriting a DOMO value because there is none to overwrite. The 2019-20 and 2020-21 rates are in the file and stay off every comparison. The attendance rate and chronic absence are two cuts of the same attendance record and move together.
  • The accountability ratings are a share of schools, and the scores come in two eras. TEA rated campuses Met Standard or Improvement Required under the index system through 2016-17 and again in 2017-18, the first A-F year for districts, then A to F from 2018-19; no campus was rated in 2019-20 or 2020-21, and in 2021-22 the legislature barred D and F, which TEA printed as 'Not Rated: SB 1365' (561 campuses, counted here as rated below B because they were evaluated). A rating is a category, so the card carries the share of rated schools at A or B (2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26) and at Met Standard, and the research pack carries each school's flag. The overall scaled score and the academic growth score are numbers, but the 2023 refresh recalibrated both (the statewide mean overall score fell from 84.04 in 2021-22 to 78.81 in 2022-23 on a harder scale, and academic growth was redefined to blend annual growth with accelerated learning), so each is carried as two measures, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2021-22 under the 2018 system and 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26 under the refresh, never joined and never on the rows, where a growth test would read the refresh as a fall. The domain scaled scores and component scores are in the research pack. The accountability file's graduation component is the best of a campus's four-, five- and six-year rates, not the four-year rate DOMO carries, and the four-year rate itself lives in the TAPR completion set, which the pull did not include, so graduation stays DOMO's alone.
  • Discipline is a share of schools, because TEA masks every count under ten. The PEIMS campus discipline summaries (2015-16 to 2024-25; the 2019-20 summary is not in the folder) give each campus's cumulative enrollment and, for any action, in-school and out-of-school suspension, DAEP and JJAEP placement and expulsion, the number of students and of actions. TEA prints a count of one to nine as -999 (and a handful as '<10') and prints no zeros, so a heading a campus has no row for is zero; 458 campus-years (alternative and juvenile-justice programs that carry placements but no enrollment) are set aside. The total actions never fall below the sum of the five kinds on the 27,747 campus-years where all are published, which is the check that a missing row is a zero. The share of students with any action is published for 78.7% of campus-years and the out-of-school suspension share for 62.5%, and the masked ones are the schools with the fewest, so a rate on the rows would be biased upward and would drop a school the year it fell under ten. The card therefore carries shares of schools at a threshold that the mask leaves knowable (10% or more of students with any action, known for 99.2% of campus-years; 5% or more suspended out of school, known for 96.5%), and the rates for any action, in-school and out-of-school suspension and DAEP placement, with the actions per 100 students and the mask flags, sit in the research pack for every campus-year (expulsion and JJAEP placement are masked at nine campuses in ten and stay in the working files). Look-alike mode reads the share of students with any action from the pack, descriptively.
  • What the SAT, ACT, AP and dual-credit figures describe. The TAPR's SAT and ACT averages and its share of examinees above the criterion describe self-selected test takers, so they sit in the research pack (the pre-2016 SAT under its own name, the 600-2400 test); the share of graduates at or above the criterion is a share of all graduates and sits on the card. AP/IB participation is a share of grade 11-12 students and sits on the card; the share of examinees above the criterion is in the pack. Dual-credit graduates and TSI-met graduates are components of CCMR and are in the pack from the classes of 2017 and 2018. TEA's economically disadvantaged share for each accountability year is in the pack beside DOMO's lunch share, which is one snapshot repeated in every year and tracks TEA's latest share (rank correlation 0.961, 79.4% of schools within five points), so the poverty bands stand on DOMO's value; 33 DOMO records carry a lunch count above a student count of one to fifteen, shares the core already caps, which is a DOMO defect rather than a join error.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 885 surveys across 350 Texas schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 13 answered by students, 12 answered by staff, 11 answered by more than one group (blended), 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build 12 surveys were a second survey in a school-year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (350 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 41 of the 370 schools counted as Leader in Me in Texas meet the criteria, and a further 5 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 97 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Texas publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • The TAPR reports attendance, chronic absence and dropout a year in arrears and college enrollment two years in arrears, so the newest attendance and chronic absence describe 2023-24 and the newest college enrollment the class of 2022.
  • TEA masks every discipline count under ten, so the share of students suspended or disciplined is known for the larger and the zero campuses only; the card's shares of schools are the honest reading and the rates in the pack are not a statewide picture.
  • The overall accountability score and the academic growth score were recalibrated in the 2023 refresh, so the page carries each as two measures; a change read across 2021-22 and 2022-23 on either is the refresh, not the schools.
  • Texas college enrollment counts Texas public and independent institutions only, so a school whose graduates leave the state reads low; the SAT and ACT averages describe self-selected test takers and never join the rows or the card.
  • The four-year graduation rate is in the TAPR completion set, which the pull did not include; graduation on this page is DOMO's alone, and the accountability file's graduation component (the best of three rates) is in the pack under its own name.
