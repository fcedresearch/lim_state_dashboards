Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results count as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before. A launch from April 1 through September 15 starts that coming school year, which is Year 1. A launch from September 16 through March 31 comes after the year was underway: that year is Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and the next year is Year 1. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day. An August 2023 launch counts from 2023-24; an October 2024 launch has 2024-25 as its Year 0 and counts from 2025-26.
  • A school's baseline is its last spring test before launch. A Leader in Me school's years before its launch count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time; its Year 0 never does.
  • 17 school-years on this page are a Year 0.
  • 6 Illinois schools on the roster carry no start date. Their launch cannot be placed, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 06, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 52 of the 154 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 23 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Illinois's own measures join through the RCDTS code NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against the state files' own school names and against DOMO's chronic absence and graduation rate (the state's own values to the decimal); a school with no RCDTS code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Illinois measures.
  • Chronic absence and the graduation rate are DOMO's own where DOMO has them, and the report card fills the school-years DOMO lacks. No DOMO value is overwritten. Poverty stays DOMO's lunch share, which is the state's own low-income share for Illinois.
  • A 5Essentials score is drawn only across the years the survey was scored the same way (2015-16 to 2023-24); the re-benchmarked 2024-25 scores are excluded by name and kept in the research pack under their own keys; a zero in the 2018 and 2019 workbooks is read as not surveyed.
  • Teacher retention, mobility, Supportive Environment and Collaborative Teachers are whole-school rates carried on each band a school reports; state-suppressed cells stay missing, never zero; a retention value of exactly zero is read as unreported.
  • Out-of-school suspension is the share of students with at least one, from the report card's discipline counts, for 2022-23 and 2023-24 only; the 2024-25 counts under ten are redacted, so that year stays in the research pack. The 2019-20 summative designations (a copy of 2018-19) and 2020-21 (not calculated) are excluded by name; the CRDC columns are kept in the pack on their own collection year and never read twice; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Illinois card and row.

Judgement calls we had to make

The places the rules needed a threshold. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

Launch year (Year 0 and Year 1)
September 15 (decided October 6, 2026). A school that launched from April 1 through September 15 counts that coming school year as its Year 1; its baseline is the spring test before launch. A school that launched from September 16 through March 31 has that school year as its Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and counts from the next school year. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day (most of the Middle Atlantic and, by law, most of Minnesota). The line replaced July 1, which put June and August launches for the same school year a full year apart.
School year boundary for exits
July 1. An expiration in June belongs to the year that just ended; one in July belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 132 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 1 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 42% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 75 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 57 pairs (1 unmatched).

5 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Chicago Vocational Career Academy High School, Dunbar Vocational Career Academy High School, Glen Oak Community Learning Center, YCCS-Jane Addams Alternative High School, YCCS-Latino Youth Alternative High School.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 239,038 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 6.2% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.6% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.4% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.6% of rows. Read as no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 0.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. Illinois's file reports achievement levels that cannot stand as measures here: no combination of the reported levels reproduces PercentMetStandard on more than 6% of rows (a five-level state whose export carries four columns, usually) (no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate; levels add up on 0.0% of rows). The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Illinois that is 2,446 Title I schools, 157 charter schools, 147 magnet schools and 5 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Illinois's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Math: Mathematics; ELA: English Language Arts; Science: Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Illinois publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 154 Leader in Me schools and 74 of 3,460 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 5 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 33.1% of Leader in Me schools and 24.4% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 131 of the 154 roster schools carry a date, and 52 were past it by more than 30 days on October 06, 2026. Exits by year: 2021: 7, 2022: 8, 2023: 13, 2024: 6, 2025: 9, 2026: 9. 23 roster schools have no expiration date (17 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 17 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 19.12 against comparison 17.34 (p = 0.025, 89 vs 3209 schools), and with them 18.33 against 17.34 (p = 0.18, 105 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2023-24 to 2024-25, 36.1 to 51.0 (+14.9); Math 2023-24 to 2024-25, 25.6 to 36.0 (+10.4). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Joining Illinois's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Illinois's files key on the state's 15-character RCDTS code instead (region, county, district, district type and school), which shares no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 3,583 id pairs for 3,582 of the 3,614 schools on this page (1 school carries more than one code across the years and joins under each), including 154 of 154 Leader in Me schools and 3,428 of 3,460 comparison schools. Through 2022-23 NCES writes the code without its two-digit district type, which its district id carries, so the full code is rebuilt from the two. The map was checked a second way, against the state files' own school names, which it was not built from: they agree with DOMO's name on 3,568 of 3,574 crosswalked schools (6 read one by one: abbreviations and a renamed school). An independent value check from a column the map was not built from either: the report card's chronic absenteeism is DOMO's chronic absence within 0.15 of a point on 100.0% of 19,826 school-years, and the four-year graduation rate on 100.0% of 4,109.
  • Chronic absence and graduation: DOMO's values are the state's, so the state's table only fills gaps. On the 19,826 school-years both carry, DOMO's chronic absence for Illinois is the report card's chronic absenteeism to the decimal (100.0% within 0.15), and DOMO's graduation rate is the state's four-year cohort rate (100.0% within 0.15 on 4,109). The report card therefore supplies only the school-years DOMO lacks: 76 chronic-absence rows and 16 graduation rows among the test rows, plus chronic absence on 1,264 of the school-year-bands added below for Illinois's own measures (schools and years with no test rows, where DOMO's value is not on the page either), and no DOMO value is overwritten. 80 state values of exactly zero (2017-18 and 2018-19, nearly all where DOMO reads zero too) are kept as published. From 2023-24 the state redacts a rate whose count is under ten (an asterisk), which arrives as absence.
  • Poverty on this page stays DOMO's lunch share, which is the state's own low-income share. Illinois publishes a low-income share for every school every year, and DOMO's free-and-reduced-lunch share for Illinois tracks it: on the 3,515 schools with both, the two correlate at r = 0.997 (each school's latest published year), the medians are 52.5 and 52.1, 77.2% of schools sit within a point, and 33% of Leader in Me schools read as high-poverty on the lunch share against 32% on the state's measure (24% and 24% of comparison schools). Community eligibility has not pushed Illinois's lunch share to the ceiling (74 schools at 99% or more), so unlike Florida and North Carolina there is nothing to override: the lunch share sets the poverty bands, the filter, matching and look-alike mode, and the state's own share per year is in the research pack.
  • The 5Essentials survey: what teachers and students say about their school. Illinois surveys teachers and students in the upper grades each spring on the University of Chicago's 5Essentials, and the report card carries each school's score on the five essentials: Effective Leaders, Collaborative Teachers and Involved Families from teachers, Supportive Environment and Ambitious Instruction from students. Each is a 1-to-99 score on which 50 is the benchmark-year average, banded at 20, 40, 60 and 80. The lines run from the 2015-16 and 2016-17 detail files through the 2017-18 to 2023-24 report cards (Effective Leaders on 3,843 schools, Collaborative Teachers on 3,844 schools, Involved Families on 3,826 schools, Supportive Environment on 3,519 schools, Ambitious Instruction on 3,519 schools), the years the survey was scored the same way: a school-year appears only where enough teachers or students responded (the 2018 and 2019 workbooks write an unsurveyed school as zero, 4,507 cells read as missing; through 2018 districts had to run the 5Essentials only every other year, so 2015-16 and 2017-18 carry about half the schools). The 2019-20 report card carries no survey, 2020-21 is a closure year, and the 2024-25 survey was re-benchmarked: every essential's statewide mean lands at 50 that year (Effective Leaders 51.1, Collaborative Teachers 50.5, Involved Families 50.5, Supportive Environment 50.9, Ambitious Instruction 48.9) and the spread of school scores narrows by a fifth to a third (Supportive Environment's standard deviation 15.5 to 12.0), so 2024-25 is excluded by name and sits in the research pack under its own keys. Supportive Environment and Collaborative Teachers are also measures at the top of the page. On Supportive Environment the Leader in Me line runs from 2016 (14 schools) to 2024 (83 schools), against 3,170 comparison schools in 2024.
  • Teacher retention, mobility, two essentials and suspension are measures, not only levels. Each is a per-school rate, so it runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), carried on each band a school reports as the core carries chronic absence. Teacher retention is ISBE's own rate, which the state averages over three years; 145 values of exactly zero, nearly all Chicago charter schools, are read as unreported rather than as a school that kept no teacher. Mobility is the share of students who moved in or out during the year. Out-of-school suspension is the share of students with at least one, from the discipline counts the state began publishing in 2022-23: 2022-23: counts for 2,831 schools (1,543 of them under ten), 708 blanks read as none, 170 'Not Provided', 125 blanks left unreported (charters and districts that reported for no school); 2023-24: counts for 2,879 schools (1,591 of them under ten), 685 blanks read as none, 110 'Not Provided', 161 blanks left unreported (charters and districts that reported for no school). The 2024-25 sheet redacts every count under ten (1,872 cells), leaving a count for 1,269 schools, all of them at ten or more suspended students, so that year is a different publication and its rate stays in the research pack where the state published it.
  • Designations, growth and the CRDC columns. The summative designation is the state's accountability tier (Exemplary, Commendable, Targeted, Comprehensive and, from 2022-23, Intensive); the card carries the share of schools in the top two tiers. The 2019-20 designations are the 2018-19 ones carried over (identical for every school) and 2020-21 was not calculated, so both are excluded by name. The growth percentile is the school's mean student growth percentile on the state test, grades 4-8; the state's own growth workbooks carry the same cohort figure (2018-19: 92.7% of 2,828 schools within 0.6; 2021-22: 89.8% of 2,785 schools within 0.6; 2022-23: 91.5% of 2,819 schools within 0.6; 2023-24: 100.0% of 2,812 schools within 0.6; their 'baseline' series measures growth against 2019 and is a different measure, not used). The report cards also republish the federal Civil Rights Data Collection's suspension and expulsion shares, but a biennial collection copied into several report cards is not a per-year measure: the 2019 workbook carries the 2015-16 collection, the 2020, 2021 and 2023 workbooks the 2017-18 one, and the 2024 and 2025 workbooks the 2020-21 one. They are in the research pack keyed on their own school year, and never on a card or a row. The 2022 additional teacher data set (novice and provisional-license shares) and the 2016 and 2017 remediation reports (the classes of 2014 and 2015) are in the pack; the fixed-width 2014-15 to 2016-17 report card files are not read, because the 5Essentials detail files already carry those years' survey and no other column of theirs is on the page.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 142 surveys across 62 Illinois schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (62 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 15 of the 98 schools counted as Leader in Me in Illinois meet the criteria, and a further 2 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2024-25, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. A school in its third year is measured from its baseline year; a school already in the process is measured from the start of the window.
Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

58 measures were screened, grouped into 18 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 23 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Illinois publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • The 5Essentials is published only where enough of a school's teachers or students responded, so a school can be present one year and absent the next, and through 2018 districts had to run it only every other year, which halves the 2015-16 and 2017-18 coverage.
  • Illinois's teacher retention rate is a three-year average, so a school's one-year change on it understates a real change in staffing.
  • The state suppresses any count under ten (blank through 2022-23, an asterisk from 2023-24), so the smallest schools are missing on mobility, truancy and the discipline counts rather than zero; the graduation rate is not capped.
  • The out-of-school suspension share rests on two whole years of counts (2022-23 and 2023-24); the 2024-25 sheet redacts counts under ten and is a different publication.
  • The summative designation share and the growth percentiles are levels, described, never tested; the growth percentile is the school's mean student growth percentile, not a change in proficiency.
