Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results count as Leader in Me from its Year 1, the first school year it launched before. A launch from April 1 through September 15 starts that coming school year, which is Year 1. A launch from September 16 through March 31 comes after the year was underway: that year is Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and the next year is Year 1. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day. An August 2023 launch counts from 2023-24; an October 2024 launch has 2024-25 as its Year 0 and counts from 2025-26.
  • A school's baseline is its last spring test before launch. A Leader in Me school's years before its launch count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time; its Year 0 never does.
  • 5 school-years on this page are a Year 0.
  • 4 Kentucky schools on the roster carry no start date. Their launch cannot be placed, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 06, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 81 of the 272 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 39 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • 2 roster schools also appear in the comparison export in years after their roster rows end, which is DOMO's own record that they left; they count as Leader in Me through their last roster year and in neither group after.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Kentucky's own measures join through the state's six-digit school code, paired with the federal id by NCES's directory and confirmed by the state files' own NCES columns where they are sound (the 2024-25 files' column is defective and excluded by name), audited against the state files' own school names and against DOMO's chronic absence; a school with no state code keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Kentucky measures.
  • A Quality of School Climate and Safety Survey item is drawn only across years and levels where it is worded the same; a school's value is the share of its students agreeing or strongly agreeing, from the state's own column; the state's suppressed cells stay missing; the 2020-21 survey is not drawn.
  • The survey indexes and the overall accountability score sit on the band of the level the state scores (elementary on primary, middle and high on secondary); the attendance rate and suspension events per 100 are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports; graduation, readiness and college-going sit on the secondary band. State-suppressed cells stay missing, never zero; an attendance rate of exactly 0 or 100 is read as unreported.
  • Suspension is events per 100 of the school's enrollment as DOMO records it; from 2019-20 a count under ten is suppressed and stays missing; a school DOMO records under 50 students carries no rate; the card's group lines are events over enrollment; the civil-rights suspension files re-printed on the 2023-24 and 2024-25 report cards are excluded by name.
  • The 2021-22 overall accountability rate (status alone), the 2014-15 to 2016-17 college and career readiness rate (another definition) and the two earlier accountability scores (their own scales) are excluded from the series by name; DOMO's graduation rate is neither filled nor overwritten; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Kentucky card and row.

Judgement calls we had to make

The places the rules needed a threshold. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

Launch year (Year 0 and Year 1)
September 15 (decided October 6, 2026). A school that launched from April 1 through September 15 counts that coming school year as its Year 1; its baseline is the spring test before launch. A school that launched from September 16 through March 31 has that school year as its Year 0, shown on the page and counted in neither group, and counts from the next school year. September 15 leaves room for schools that open after Labor Day (most of the Middle Atlantic and, by law, most of Minnesota). The line replaced July 1, which put June and August launches for the same school year a full year apart.
School year boundary for exits
July 1. An expiration in June belongs to the year that just ended; one in July belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 171 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 5 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 31% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 109 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 62 pairs (5 unmatched).

6 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Bowling Green Learning Center, Buckner Alternative High School, Calloway County Alternative Program, Fayette County Learning Center, McDaniel Learning Center, Renaissance Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 101,531 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 100.0% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.1% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 3.0% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.4% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 94.7% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Kentucky's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 94.7% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard (Novice, Apprentice, Proficient, Distinguished in Kentucky's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Kentucky that is 905 Title I schools, 0 charter schools, 71 magnet schools and 126 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Kentucky's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. Writing: Writing; ELA: English, English II, Reading; Math: Algebra II, Mathematics; Social Studies: Social Studies, U.S. History; Science: Biology, Science. Left out because they are not achievement tests: Editing and Mechanics, Language Mechanics.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Kentucky publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 272 Leader in Me schools and 6 of 1,039 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 4 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 16.5% of Leader in Me schools and 25.9% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 233 of the 272 roster schools carry a date, and 81 were past it by more than 30 days on October 06, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 2, 2020: 7, 2021: 11, 2022: 9, 2023: 14, 2024: 9, 2025: 17, 2026: 12. 39 roster schools have no expiration date (37 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 37 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 4.85 against comparison 4.78 (p = 0.922, 129 vs 885 schools), and with them 4.87 against 4.78 (p = 0.889, 162 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Joining Kentucky's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Kentucky's files key on the state's six-digit school code (the three-digit district number and the three-digit school number: Adair County High School is 001010) and most carry the federal id beside it. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries the same code inside its state school id for every public school, so that is the anchor: 1,311 id pairs for 1,311 of the 1,311 schools on this page (1,299 confirmed by the state files' own NCES columns, 0 from NCES alone, 12 from the state files alone), including 272 of 272 Leader in Me schools and 1,039 of 1,039 comparison schools. The 2024-25 report card files carry a defective NCES column (every school's federal id is its district's id padded with zeros: 200 distinct ids for 1123 schools in the index file), so that column is excluded by name and 2024-25 joins on the school code like every other year. The map was checked against the state files' own school names, which it was not built from: they agree with DOMO's name on 1,310 of 1,311 crosswalked schools (1 read one by one: renamed schools and abbreviations). An independent value check from files the map was not built from either: the state's attendance rate against DOMO's chronic absence on the same school-years ranks them at Spearman -0.981 across 3,288 school-years (mean DOMO chronic absence by attendance quarter lowest attendance quarter: 46.8%, second: 30.4%, third: 21.6%, highest attendance quarter: 13.2%). Four codes (185015, 365065, 523050, 541043) carry a different federal id in NCES and in the state's files, a renumbered school; both ids are kept with the years each held the code, and a year's row joins to the id that held it then.
  • The Quality of School Climate and Safety Survey: what students say about their school. The state surveys students in the accountability grades each spring and publishes every school's results by level (elementary, middle, high) for all students; the folder holds the item files for 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25 (trimmed to the school-level, all-students rows; the 2020-21 elementary file is a zero-byte file on KDE's server and is not among them) and the climate and safety index for 2021-22 to 2024-25. The index is the state's own: each item scores strongly disagree 0, disagree 33, agree 67 and strongly agree 100, the climate index is the mean over the 14 climate items and the safety index over the 13 safety items; rebuilt from the item file, the climate index lands within a third of a point on every elementary school in 2021-22, and the question index rebuilds from the four shares within a point on every row. The 2020-21 survey is an NTI-era instrument (items about device access and video lessons beside the climate items) in a closure year, and is not drawn. The item wording changes between years and levels for a third of the items (an elementary 'I feel like am part of my school' beside a middle-school 'I feel like I am part of my school'; 'My school is an encouraging place' becoming 'a place where students feel encouraged'), so an item is drawn only across years and levels where it is worded the same, punctuation aside: the four on the card are "My school is a caring place." (2021-22 to 2024-25, ES/HS/MS); "Adults from my school care about me." (2021-22 to 2024-25, ES/HS/MS); "I feel safe in my classes." (2021-22 to 2024-25, ES/HS/MS); "There is at least one adult from my school who listens to me when I have something to say." (2021-22 to 2024-25, ES/HS/MS), and the research pack carries 16 items worded the same in every year and level from 2021-22 on. A school's value is the share agreeing or strongly agreeing, from the file's own column; the state suppresses a cell under its reporting floor (2020-21: 4,998, 2021-22: 4, 2022-23: 3,492, 2023-24: 4,004, 2024-25: 4,268 item cells), which stays missing. The indexes are never suppressed for all students. On the climate index the Leader in Me line runs from 2021-22 (149 schools) to 2024-25 (162 schools), against 890 comparison schools in 2024-25.
  • The survey indexes, attendance, suspension and the accountability figures are measures, not only levels. The climate and safety indexes, the attendance rate, suspension events per 100 students, the overall accountability score, the graduation rate indicator, postsecondary readiness and the in-state college-going rate are per-school rates, so they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test). The state scores the survey and the accountability profile by level, so an elementary level sits on the primary band and a middle or high level on the secondary band (a school scored at both middle and high carries their mean on the secondary band); the attendance rate and the suspension rate are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence; the graduation, readiness and college-going rates sit on the secondary band. The attendance rate comes from three file layouts (2014-15 to 2016-17 the federal attendance file, 2017-18 and 2018-19 the SAAR file, 2022-23 on the report card's attendance rate), the same days-attended-over-days-enrolled rate in each; the state published no school-level rate for the three years between. 239 rates of exactly 0 or 100 are read as unreported: every one belongs to a homebound, hospital, detention or alternative program.
  • Suspension is events per 100 students, and the state's suppression from 2019-20 is named. Kentucky's Safe Schools files carry, for every school, the count of out-of-school suspension events and of students suspended in the year, in a layout that changes four times across the window (a long report with headers and lines to 2016-17; a 2017-18 file that omits a zero line, so a school listed with no suspension line has zero; a 2018-19 file whose district and state totals carry a school number of their own; and a wide table with an All Students row for students and a Total Events row for events from 2019-20). The 2017-18 school rows sum to the state total exactly (65,902 events). The measure is events per 100 of the school's enrollment as DOMO records it (one current count per school), so the line moves with the events, not the roll; against the state's own adjusted average daily attendance, where it publishes one, the two denominators rank the schools at Spearman 0.995 across 5,296 school-years. A per-100 rate over fewer than 50 students moves two points per event, so 543 school-years at programs DOMO records under 50 students carry no rate, and each group's card line is the group's events over the group's enrollment rather than a mean of school rates, so the alternative programs that record several hundred events per 100 students (day-treatment centers, alternative schools, juvenile facilities) cannot swing it. From 2019-20 the state suppresses a count under ten (2019-20: 134 of 1,404; 2020-21: 262 of 1,406; 2021-22: 162 of 1,406; 2022-23: 132 of 1,408; 2023-24: 120 of 1,488; 2024-25: 103 of 1,491), so a school with one to nine events is missing rather than zero in those years and the statewide level steps up a little at 2021-22 for that reason alone; within any window from 2021-22 on the rule is the same at both ends. The KYRC24 and KYRC25 'Student Suspensions', 'Student Expulsions' and 'Arrests and Referrals' files are a federal civil-rights collection for 2020-21 re-printed on both report cards (their 'Collected School Year' column says so, and the two files are identical), and are excluded by name.
  • Kentucky's accountability system changed three times, so the score is one scale at a time. The profile files carry Unbridled Learning's overall score for 2014-15 and 2015-16 (the 2016-17 file carries the column empty; the state suspended the score), no overall in 2017-18, the five-star system's score and stars in 2018-19, graduation only in the two closure years, a status-only overall indicator rate in 2021-22, and from 2022-23 the overall combined indicator rate (status and change together) with a colour rating from 1 (red) to 5 (blue). Only the last is a series on one scale, so the page's overall accountability score is 2022-23 to 2024-25 (1,265, 1,264 and 1,263 school-levels); the 2021-22 overall rate is excluded by name, and the two earlier scores sit in the research pack under their own names. The OAA copy of the 2024-25 profile agrees with the report card file on 100.0% of 1,263 school-levels. The graduation rate indicator is the state's combined four- and five-year cohort rate in every file that carries it (2017-18 on), and DOMO's graduation rate is not that figure: on 448 shared school-years the two agree within 0.15 points on 12.1% and rank at Spearman 0.871, so DOMO's rate is left as DOMO has it and never filled, and the state's indicator is carried as its own measure. No Kentucky file in the folder carries chronic absence, so DOMO's value stands there too and nothing is filled.
  • Readiness and college-going are the class's own year. Transition readiness (2017-18, 2018-19) and postsecondary readiness (2021-22 on) are the same construction under two names: graduates who met an academic or a career benchmark, non-duplicated, over graduates, with a bonus for both that the page leaves off (the with-bonus rate passes 100 at some schools and is in the research pack). The 2014-15 to 2016-17 college and career readiness rate is Unbridled Learning's definition, with different benchmarks, and is excluded from the series by name; it is in the pack. The state suppresses a school's readiness under its reporting floor (2017-18: 1 of 228, 2018-19: 1 of 228, 2021-22: 0 of 228, 2022-23: 0 of 228, 2023-24: 0 of 228, 2024-25: 0 of 228 all-students rows). College-going is KYSTATS's, not KDE's: the High School Feedback Report's High School Summary sheet (read from the 49 MB public-access workbook) carries each high school's in-state college-going rate by graduating class, 2011 to 2024, and the July 2026 class-of-2024 file extends it one class; the report's 'any college' and 'in-state' rates are the same number in every row, so the page calls it in-state. The report card's own transition to in-state postsecondary education is a related figure for the prior class that the state suppresses for about half of its high schools, and sits in the pack. Each class sits on its own year, so the class of 2024 shows on 2023-24.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 213 surveys across 98 Kentucky schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (98 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 15 of the 155 schools counted as Leader in Me in Kentucky meet the criteria, and a further 1 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2024-25, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

75 measures were screened, grouped into 21 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 39 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Kentucky publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • The Quality of School Climate and Safety Survey is scored by level, so a K-8 school carries an elementary index on the primary band and a middle index on the secondary band; the card averages a school's levels, and a school whose item cell is suppressed is absent from that item's line.
  • Kentucky's survey items change wording between years and levels for a third of the items; the four on this page are drawn only across years and levels where the item is worded the same, and the 2020-21 survey is not drawn.
  • Suspension events are divided by DOMO's one enrollment count per school, so a school whose roll changed across the window carries that change in its rate; from 2019-20 the state suppresses counts under ten, so the low-suspending schools are missing in those years rather than zero; alternative programs record several hundred events per 100 students and sit in the comparison group's rows, where the school-type filter can set them apart.
  • Kentucky published no school-level attendance rate for 2019-20, 2020-21 or 2021-22, so the attendance line jumps from 2018-19 to 2022-23.
  • The overall accountability score exists on one scale only from 2022-23, three years; the earlier systems' scores are in the research pack but not in a series.
  • DOMO's graduation rate for Kentucky is neither the state's graduation indicator nor the four-year cohort rate and is left as DOMO has it; the state's own figures are carried under their own names.
