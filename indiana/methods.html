Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 22 Indiana schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 43 of the 104 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 21 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Indiana's own measures join through the state school id NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, a state id under two federal ids settled by the year each held it, a school NCES never listed joined by an exact name within corporation, and the map audited against a name-within-corporation join and DOMO's own graduation rate; a school with no state id keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Indiana measures.
  • Attendance is the state's published rate; a rate of exactly 100% is read as unreported, exclusively virtual schools stay out, and 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Indiana card and row.
  • The state grade line and points are the 2015-16 to 2017-18 domain model only; the 2014-15 and 2018-19 hold-harmless grades and the 2011-2014 model sit in the research pack under their own scheme. No Grade and Appeal Pending are counted, not graded.
  • The federal rating line is the four-band rating from 2018-19; the 2017-18 federal A-F grade sits in the pack. The comprehensive-support share follows the bottom-5% and graduation-under-67% rules only; ATSI flips and at-risk notes stay in the pack.
  • The core's chronic-absence and graduation columns are not filled from Indiana's files: the only file that carries either is the 2021-22 federal report card, a year DOMO has, whose graduation rate is a different rate and whose chronic-absenteeism count is a different count.
  • 2025-26 school results from SchoolDigger's bulk file (the source of DOMO's subject file), read exactly as DOMO reads earlier years, extend each test to the newest year.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 66 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 1 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 46% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 37 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 29 pairs (1 unmatched).

9 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): COMPASS Alternative School, Community Youth Services, Elkhart Alternative Education, IN School for the Blind & Vis Imprd, Indiana Digital Alternative School, Indiana School For The Deaf, Logansport Juvenile Correctional, Pendleton Juvenile Correctional, RISE Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 146,699 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 100.0% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.5% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.5% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 1.2% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 60.7% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Indiana's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 60.7% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard (Below Proficiency, Approaching Proficiency, At Proficiency, Above Proficiency in Indiana's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Indiana that is 998 Title I schools, 155 charter schools, 27 magnet schools and 10 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Indiana's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English 10, English Language Arts, English/Language Arts, Evidence-based Reading and Writing; Math: Algebra I, Math, Mathematics; Science: Biology, Biology I, Science; Social Studies: Social Studies, US Government.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Indiana publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 104 Leader in Me schools and 12 of 1,824 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 0 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 1.9% of Leader in Me schools and 9.4% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 83 of the 104 roster schools carry a date, and 43 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2021: 4, 2022: 4, 2023: 2, 2024: 4, 2025: 11, 2026: 18. 21 roster schools have no expiration date (6 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 6 schools the headline reads Leader in Me -0.71 against comparison 0.7 (p = 0.255, 48 vs 1547 schools), and with them -0.05 against 0.7 (p = 0.532, 53 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2017-18 to 2018-19, 60.7 to 47.8 (-12.9); Math 2014-15 to 2015-16, 62.8 to 52.6 (-10.2). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.

  • Attendance is the state's own rate, and a perfect rate is unreported. The Indiana Department of Education publishes each school's attendance rate, the share of enrolled student-days attended, in three files: one column per year from 2005-06 to 2019-20 (2,222 public-school rows; a school id that appears under two corporations, a takeover operator or a merged corporation, 15 of them, never carries the same year twice and folds to one), a 2020-21 file with the year's rate and the share of weeks each school's primary mode was in person, hybrid or virtual (1,971 rates, 218 marked Data Unavailable), and one sheet per year for 2022-23 to 2024-25. No file covers 2021-22, so that year is a real gap on the line. Non-public schools' rows (ids beginning with a letter) are set aside. In 139 public-school-years inside the page's window a school reported attendance of exactly 100%: juvenile centers, preschools and virtual academies reporting attendance equal to enrollment, read as unreported (101 of them on schools this page carries); 28 schools NCES flags as exclusively virtual are kept out of the attendance rows and card. It is a whole-school rate carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence, and it runs through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test). The 2019-20 rate (the year's schools closed in March) and the 2020-21 rate sit in the research pack, labelled, and off every comparison.
  • Indiana graded schools under three schemes, and two years were held harmless. The state's A-F grades in the folder run 2012-13 to 2018-19. The 2011 to 2014 grades come from a 4-point model (the points are in the 2013 and 2014 files); 2014-15 was held harmless for the new ISTEP+ (a school's grade could not fall below its 2013-14 grade: of 2,074 schools graded both years, 0 fell, 1,952 stood still and 122 rose); 2015-16 to 2017-18 used the domain model (performance and growth in grades 3-8, performance, growth and multiple measures in high school, weighted into overall points on which A is 90 and up, B 80 to 89.9, C 70 to 79.9, D 60 to 69.9 and F under 60, bands that hold on every graded school in both files; the overall points rebuild from the domain points and weights on 2,019 of 2,076 schools in 2016-17 and 2,098 of 2,106 in 2017-18); and 2018-19 was held harmless again for ILEARN (of 2,012 schools graded both years, 0 fell, 1,892 stood still and 120 rose). No state grade was issued after 2018-19. The card's grade line is therefore the domain model's three straight years, and the points and the growth domain are the two years the files publish them (2016-17 and 2017-18); the hold-harmless years and the 4-point model sit in the research pack under their own scheme. Each file restates the year before, and the restated grade agrees with that year's own file on 1,792 of 1,795 (2014 file restating 2013), 1,770 of 1,773 (2015 file restating 2013), 2,074 of 2,074 (2015 file restating 2014), 1,788 of 1,788 (2017 file restating 2016), 2,092 of 2,093 (2018 file restating 2017). Grades of No Grade and Appeal Pending (49 in 2017-18) are counted, not graded; adult high schools are graded on a separate model and stay out.
  • The federal rating is four bands from 2018-19; 2017-18 was a letter. Under Indiana's ESSA plan the 2017-18 federal accountability result was an A-F letter with overall points 0 to 100 built from weighted indicator points (achievement, progress, graduation, college and career readiness, English-learner progress, chronic absenteeism; A is 90 and up, and the bands hold), where the academic progress indicator sat at its cap of 100 for 653 of 1,526 schools and the college-and-career-readiness indicator for 353 of 379. From 2018-19 the state replaced the letter with four bands (Exceeds, Meets, Approaches, Does Not Meet Expectations) on the overall rating and on each indicator, and published no points, so the 2017-18 letter and points sit in the research pack and the card draws the share of schools at Meets or better for 2018-19 and 2021-22 to 2024-25 (none were issued for the closure years). The 2021-22 summary sheet's count column is shuffled between rows in the state's file, and the school-level sheet is read.   • The 2021-22 federal report card is one year, in the pack. The state's interactive federal report card workbook for 2021-22 carries, in its raw-data sheets, each school's out-of-school and in-school suspensions, expulsions (44 cells masked with ***), a federal four-year graduation rate (28 masked), enrollment and spending per student, and teacher counts. The suspension counts are incidents over the card's own enrollment, so a rate can pass 100 at a school that suspends often, and one year cannot feed the growth test, so they go to the research pack as per-100 rates with the graduation rate, the free-lunch, inexperienced-teacher and emergency-permit shares and spending per student. DOMO carries chronic absence for 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25 and a graduation rate for 2017-18, 2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25. The only Indiana file in the folder that carries either is the 2021-22 federal report card, a year DOMO already has, so nothing is filled: its federal four-year graduation rate is a different rate from DOMO's (the state's rate counts waiver diplomas; r = 0.906 on 387 high schools, medians 91.0 against 90.8, 22.5% within a point), and its "chronic absenteeism" count runs at 3.8% of enrollment for the 475 schools that carry one against DOMO's 27.4% and does not track it (r = 0.196), a different count that is excluded by name and kept in the research pack under its own label.
  • 2025-26 results come from SchoolDigger's bulk file, the same source as DOMO's subject file for every earlier year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Indiana. DOMO's subject file is SchoolDigger's test-score table, so the 2025-26 rows (17,144 school-grade-test rows for 1,777 schools) were read from SchoolDigger's newest bulk file for FranklinCovey in the same layout: the percent meeting the state's standard and the first four achievement levels, with SchoolDigger's grade and test labels. The same file's 2024-25 rows reproduce DOMO's: 100.0% of DOMO's 17,038 Indiana school rows for that year are in it, and the proficiency figure is identical on 100.0% of them.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 127 surveys across 52 Indiana schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 13 answered by staff, 10 answered by students. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (52 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 9 of the 56 schools counted as Leader in Me in Indiana meet the criteria, and a further 2 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. A school in its third year is measured from its baseline year; a school already in the process is measured from the start of the window.
Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

57 measures were screened, grouped into 13 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 21 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Indiana publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Indiana publishes no attendance rate for 2021-22 in the files in this build, so the attendance line has a one-year gap after the closures.
  • Indiana's state A-F grades ended with 2018-19, two of the last five years were held harmless, and the points behind the grade are published for two years only, so the state-grade measures are short lines; the attendance rate is the one Indiana measure the state has published on one definition across the whole window.
  • The federal accountability rating is four bands with no published points from 2018-19, so the card can only carry shares of schools meeting a bar, which move with the cut scores as much as with the schools.
  • The 2021-22 federal report card's suspension figures are counts of incidents, not of students, over one year, so they are pack values and never a measure.
