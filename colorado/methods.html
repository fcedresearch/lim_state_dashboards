Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 4 Colorado schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 21 of the 62 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 10 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Colorado's own measures join through the state district and school codes NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against a name-within-district join and DOMO's own chronic absence. A school with no state id keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Colorado measures.
  • Attendance is total days attended over total days possible and truancy unexcused days over days possible; a rate at or above 99.9% or of exactly zero, or a truancy rate of zero with no unexcused day recorded, is read as unreported. Colorado's chronic absenteeism (2025-26 only) shares no school-year with DOMO's, so neither fills nor overwrites the other.
  • The growth measure is the mean of a school's English language arts and mathematics median growth percentiles on the test its grades take; a masked subject (under 20 students) leaves the school with no combined figure. The framework's percent of points is carried only where a plan type was issued, never for an Alternative Education Campus, and the rolled-over 2019-20 and 2020-21 and the preliminary 2025-26 ratings are excluded by name. The framework's four-year graduation rate fills DOMO's graduation rate only where DOMO has none, on the class's own year.
  • Matriculation is the state's unduplicated rate on the class's own year, with the military (from 2021) and state postsecondary programs (from 2023) inside it as the state counts them; cohorts under 16 are masked. 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Colorado card and row.
  • 2025-26 grades 3-8 reading and math (from CDE's 2026 CMAS results file) extend the reading-and-math figure; proficiency is the percent met or exceeded expectations, as in prior years.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 42 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 1 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start -0.1 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 40% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 32 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 10 pairs (1 unmatched).

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 119,535 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 15.1% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 3.1% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.1% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 2.0% of rows. Read as no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 0.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. Colorado's file reports achievement levels that cannot stand as measures here: no combination of the reported levels reproduces PercentMetStandard on more than 15% of rows (a five-level state whose export carries four columns, usually) (no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate; levels add up on 0.0% of rows). The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Colorado that is 642 Title I schools, 253 charter schools, 25 magnet schools and 36 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Colorado's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English Language Arts, Reading and Writing; Math: Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Integrated Math I, Integrated Math II, Integrated Math III, Math, Mathematics; Social Studies: Social Studies; Science: Science.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Colorado publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 0 of 62 Leader in Me schools and 2 of 1,726 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 1 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 24.2% of Leader in Me schools and 20.6% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 52 of the 62 roster schools carry a date, and 21 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 3, 2019: 1, 2021: 2, 2022: 3, 2023: 2, 2024: 3, 2025: 5, 2026: 2. 10 roster schools have no expiration date (7 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 7 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 3.4 against comparison 2.82 (p = 0.783, 26 vs 1051 schools), and with them 3.47 against 2.82 (p = 0.72, 30 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Joining Colorado's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Colorado's files key on the state's four-digit district code and four-digit school code instead (Academy 20 is 1040 and Air Academy High School 0076), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school (its state school id reads CO-1040-0076), so that is the anchor: 1,778 id pairs for 1,778 of the 1,788 schools on this page, including 62 of 62 Leader in Me schools and 1,716 of 1,726 comparison schools (2016-17 to 2023-24; no schools renumbered inside that window). The map was audited a second way: the federal id's own LEA number resolved to the state district code through a separate field of the directory, and DOMO's school name matched to the state files' names within that district, speaks for 1,749 of the NCES-joined schools and agrees on 1,749. The state files' own name agrees with DOMO's on 1,741 of 1,751 crosswalked ids. An independent value check from a value the map was not built from: DOMO's own chronic absence against the state's attendance rate on the same school-years runs r = -0.923 on 2,953 school-years (Spearman -0.974), which is what one attendance measure against another should do when the ids line up. One state id claimed by two federal ids stays with one school: DOLORES MIDDLE SCHOOL (080339000431) shares state id 2055-2206 with the id DOMO reports latest.
  • One Leader in Me school carries a state id but no Colorado measure. Stansberry Elementary School (Thompson School District R-2j) has a school code in NCES's directory that no file in the folder lists (a school closed before the files begin), so it carries test scores only.
  • Attendance and truancy are day counts, and a perfect rate is unreported. CDE's attendance and truancy file gives each school's total days attended, excused and unexcused absence days and days possible, and the two rates (2019-20 to 2025-26, 13,310 school-years; the published rates rebuild from the day totals on 13,310 of them; the district and state total rows the early files carry are set aside). In 101 school-years a school reported attendance within a tenth of a point of its days possible and in 7 it reported attendance for a fraction of the year (under 20%, among them Melvin Hendrickson Development Center (0.0%, 47 students) in 2019-20; Hayden High School (8.0%, 10 students) in 2019-20; Idalia Elementary School (0.0%, 1 student) in 2019-20; Idalia Junior-Senior High School (0.0%, 78 students) in 2019-20); a rate at or above 99.9% or under 20% is read as unreported. The truancy rate is unexcused absence days over days possible, the state's own definition; in 331 school-years a school recorded no unexcused day all year and a truancy rate of exactly zero, read as unreported rather than as no truancy. Both are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence, and both run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test). Colorado's own chronic absenteeism rate appears in the 2025-26 workbook only (1,785 of 1,888 schools with a rate, the rest masked); DOMO's chronic absence covers 2023-24, 2024-25, so the two share no school-year and neither fills nor overwrites the other (read across years, the state's 2025-26 rate against DOMO's 2024-25 one runs r = 0.904 on 1,453 schools, the state averaging 26.5% against DOMO's 27.5%). The state's chronic absenteeism rate sits in the research pack.
  • Growth is the state's own median growth percentile, on the test each grade takes. CDE's growth files rank each student's score against students with the same prior scores and publish the school's median rank by subject: CMAS in grades 4 to 8 and the PSAT/SAT in grades 9 to 11 (2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26), and the WIDA ACCESS test for English learners. The statewide median is 50 in every subject and year by construction, so a school's figure reads directly as above or below typical growth. 8,377 school-test-subject cells under 20 students are masked and left missing. There was no spring 2020 test; the 2020-21 CMAS file carries two-year growth from 2019 by grade only, with no school figure, and is not read; the 2020 and 2021 English language proficiency files are read for the research pack only, as closure years. The 2021-22 growth is measured from the disrupted spring-2021 tests, as the state publishes it. The measure at the top of the page is the mean of a school's English language arts and mathematics medians: CMAS on the primary band, and on the secondary band CMAS, the PSAT/SAT, or the mean of the two where a school's secondary grades take both; a school with one subject masked carries that subject in the pack and no combined figure. As a check on the extraction, the growth file's medians equal the framework's own growth medians for the same year on single-level schools: CMAS English language arts 4,292 of 4,292, CMAS mathematics 4,264 of 4,264, PSAT/SAT reading and writing 1,393 of 1,393, PSAT/SAT mathematics 1,436 of 1,436.
  • The School Performance Framework: the percent of points behind the plan type. Colorado rates every school each fall on its framework: points for achievement and growth at each level the school serves and, at the high-school level, for postsecondary and workforce readiness, summed with weights into a percent of points that sets the plan type (Performance at 53% or more, then Improvement, Priority Improvement and Turnaround). The plan-type files carry the final plan type and the percent for 2010 to 2025 and the rating calculation files the indicators for 2021-22 to 2024-25; each year is read from the file whose own year it is, and the years the files repeat agree (65,595 of 65,645 plan words across 36 repeated year-file pairs), while the plan file's percent and the calculation file's agree on 6,137 of 6,137 school-years. No ratings were issued for 2014-15 (the assessment transition); the 2019-20 and 2020-21 plan types were rolled over unchanged from 2018-19 under the accountability pause and are excluded by name, as are the August 2026 preliminary ratings; 2021-22 was a transitional single-year framework whose plan types carried a participation note. The percent of points is carried only where a plan type was issued: a school with insufficient state data, newly opened or closed has none, and the 1,125 school-years of Alternative Education Campuses are rated on a framework of their own and are left out. In 2024-25 the calculation file carries both the single-year and the multi-year report; the official one is the row whose percent of points is the plan-type file's own (1,564 settled that way; 161 where both reports carry the same percent and 108 where neither carries the plan file's, schools without a plan type mostly, by the report span the plan file names). The percent of points is a measure at the top of the page, whole-school on each band; the card also carries the share of rated schools on a Performance plan and the achievement and growth indicators' percent of points.
  • Graduation: the framework's four-year rate fills where DOMO has none, on the class's own year. The rating calculation files carry each high school's four-year graduation rate for the class of the year before the report (the 2021-22 file carries the class of 2021). Against DOMO's graduation rate on the same school-years, the file labelled a year later matches: 2021-22: 100.0% of 231 school-years within half a point; 2022-23: 100.0% of 263 school-years within half a point; 2023-24: 99.3% of 294 school-years within half a point; so DOMO's year is the class's own year, and the framework's rate fills 222 school-years DOMO has no value for (class of 2022: 94, class of 2023: 55, class of 2024: 73), never a school-year DOMO carries. The framework's dropout rate, five-year rate, best-of-four scored rate and SAT means sit in the research pack.
  • Matriculation is on the class year, and the count widened twice. CDE's matriculation files count, per high school, the graduates enrolled the following summer or fall in a two-year institution, a four-year institution or a career and technical program, unduplicated, with the counts behind the rate (classes of 2019 to 2025; the published rate rebuilds from the counts on every row checked; cohorts under 16 are masked, 3,740 school-class-category cells). Military enlistment joined the count for the class of 2021 and state postsecondary programs (ASCENT, P-TECH and TREP) for the class of 2023, each named in the file: at the state level they add under a point and a half and two to three points, so the overall rate is carried as the state publishes it with the widening named here and on the card, and the four-year institution rate, the same in every class, is carried beside it. The framework's own matriculation figure for the same class agrees with the file on 100.0% of 1,458 school-classes. Only two Leader in Me high schools carry a class figure while counted, so the rate stays in the research pack and the school list rather than on the rows or the card; the classes of 2020 and 2021 are closure classes and sit in the research pack with every category.
  • Colorado's own measures are measures, not only levels. Attendance, truancy, the combined growth median and the framework's percent of points are per-school rates, so they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), each carried on every band a school reports; the matriculation rate stays in the research pack because fewer than three Leader in Me schools carry a value while counted. The card shows 9 measures as statewide levels by group: attendance, truancy, the CMAS English language arts median, the CMAS mathematics median, the language-proficiency median, the framework's percent of points, the Performance-plan share, the achievement indicator, the growth indicator (the matriculation rate, the four-year matriculation rate left off for thin data); group values are school means with a three-school minimum, described, never significance-tested, and the roster growing every year means composition as much as practice. The 2025-26 attendance and growth figures are on the rows and the card, because the page's test results reach 2025-26 with Colorado's own CMAS results; Colorado's 2025-26 chronic absenteeism rate stays in the research pack.
  • 2025-26 reading and math come from Colorado's own CMAS results and extend the reading-and-math figure to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Colorado. The 2025-26 rows (8,083 school-grade-subject rows for 1,298 schools) were read from the Colorado Department of Education's 2026 CMAS district and school results file (all students, grades 3 to 8) and joined to the DOMO id through the NCES directory's Colorado state id. Proficiency is the file's percent met or exceeded expectations, the figure DOMO carries for Colorado in every earlier year: all 8,035 of DOMO's 2024-25 grade 3-8 Colorado rows are one row of CDE's 2025 file, and every value agrees to the tenth of a point. 2026 is the same test on the same scale: the file is based on the 2020 Colorado Academic Standards, as the 2025 file is, and CDE prints each school's 2025 figure beside its 2026 one. Rows CDE suppresses to protect student privacy carry no value, and the Spanish language arts test is not read.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 76 surveys across 29 Colorado schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 18 answered by more than one group (blended), 18 answered by staff, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (29 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 8 of the 39 schools counted as Leader in Me in Colorado meet the criteria, and a further 0 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

60 measures were screened, grouped into 17 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 10 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Colorado publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Colorado publishes no school-level discipline file in this build; the truancy rate (unexcused absence) is the page's behavior measure and the one look-alike mode names, and it describes absences rather than conduct.
  • A median growth percentile is a median: at a small school a few students move it, and the state masks any cell under 20 students, so the smallest schools carry no growth figure at all.
  • The framework's percent of points folds achievement, growth and readiness together with weights the State Board sets; a school's figure can move when the framework's rules move (the 2021-22 transitional framework, the 2023-24 return of multi-year reports), not only when its students do.
  • The matriculation rate widened by definition for the classes of 2021 and 2023; a change across those classes is partly the count, not the graduates, and the four-year institution rate is the stable series.
  • Colorado's attendance and truancy rates are whole-school figures carried on each band a school reports, as the core carries chronic absence; a K-6 school's rate sits on its grade 6 secondary rows as well as its primary rows.
  • The state's own file for the newest year does not yet carry every test DOMO carried the year before (2025-26 10th Grade Mathematics (265 rows the year before); 2025-26 10th Grade Reading and Writing (300 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade Mathematics (259 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade Reading and Writing (299 rows the year before); 2025-26 11th Grade Science (270 rows the year before); 2025-26 5th Grade Science (902 rows the year before); 2025-26 8th Grade Science (455 rows the year before); 2025-26 9th Grade Mathematics (274 rows the year before); 2025-26 9th Grade Reading and Writing (302 rows the year before)). Those results usually come out later in the fall. So a school is never compared on one set of tests against a different set, a school that took one of those tests the year before has its newest-year results in that grade band and subject left out (423 rows at 95 schools) until the missing results are released and folded in; the rest of its results, and every earlier year, are unchanged.
