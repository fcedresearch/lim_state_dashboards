Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 4 Tennessee schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on September 17, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 38 of the 64 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 1 inside the grace window stay active; 12 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Tennessee's own measures join through the state system and school numbers NCES's directory carries beside the federal id, audited against the state files' own school names and against DOMO's chronic absence and graduation rate; a school with no state number keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no Tennessee measures.
  • A Tennessee Educator Survey construct is drawn only across years where the item is worded the same; a school's value is the share of its teachers agreeing or strongly agreeing over the four Likert options; a school number with two differing records in one year is set aside.
  • Teacher retention and the survey's satisfaction item are whole-school rates carried on each band a school reports; Ready Graduate, the ACT and college enrollment sit on the secondary band. State-suppressed cells stay missing, never zero; a retention value of exactly zero is read as unreported.
  • Out-of-school suspension from 2021-22 on is carried as the share of schools at 5% or more, because the state suppresses the rate itself for three schools in four; the 2015-16 and 2016-17 files are excluded by name.
  • The class of 2023 postsecondary file (a wider enrollment window) and the 2017-18 indicator file (decimal averages) are excluded by name; 2019-20 and 2020-21 are kept off every Tennessee card and row.
  • 2025-26 reading, math and social studies (from Tennessee's own school-level assessment file) extend the reading-and-math figure; proficiency is the on-track-and-mastered share and the four levels are carried where the state publishes all four. Science and its end-of-course tests (Biology I, Science) are excluded for 2025-26 alone, a standard-setting year: 6,962 rows.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 42 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 2 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start -0.3 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median -0.1), and 33% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 29 pairs (2 unmatched); Secondary: 13 pairs (0 unmatched).

4 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Hamblen County Alternative School, R T Fisher Alternative, Smyrna West Alternative School, Tipton County Alternative Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 202,736 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 66.1% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 0.1% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.7% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 0.9% of rows. Read as four levels, numbered from the bottom; proficiency is the top 2. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 28.0% of rows; the years and subjects that do not add up (2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math, 2014-15 Science, 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science, 2015-16 Social Studies, 2017-18 ELA, 2017-18 Math, 2017-18 Science, 2017-18 Social Studies, 2018-19 ELA, 2018-19 Math, 2018-19 Social Studies, 2021-22 ELA, 2021-22 Math, 2021-22 Science, 2021-22 Social Studies) carry no level values.
  • The achievement levels are on the page as measures of their own. Tennessee's file reports four achievement levels. They add to 100 and the state's proficiency rate equals levels 3 and 4 together on 28.0% of rows (checked school year by school year and subject by subject; a year or subject whose levels do not add up carries no level values: 2014-15 ELA, 2014-15 Math, 2014-15 Science, 2015-16 ELA, 2015-16 Math, 2015-16 Science, 2015-16 Social Studies, 2017-18 ELA, 2017-18 Math, 2017-18 Science, 2017-18 Social Studies, 2018-19 ELA, 2018-19 Math, 2018-19 Social Studies, 2021-22 ELA, 2021-22 Math, 2021-22 Science, 2021-22 Social Studies), so each level can stand as a measure: level 1 below the standard; level 2 approaching the standard; level 3 meeting the standard; level 4 exceeding the standard (Below, Approaching, On Track, Mastered in Tennessee's own terms, lowest first). The measure at the top of the page, meeting or exceeding the standard, is levels 3 and 4 together, which is the state's own proficiency rate. Level 2 is reported without a direction of its own, because it falls both when students move up and when they slip down.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In Tennessee that is 1,247 Title I schools, 141 charter schools, 126 magnet schools and 11 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in Tennessee's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English I, English II, English III, English Language Arts, Reading/Language; Math: Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Integrated Math I, Integrated Math II, Integrated Math III, Math, Mathematics; Science: Biology I, Chemistry, Science; Social Studies: Social Studies, US History.
  • Poverty is the lunch share, and it has a ceiling. The DOMO records carry the free-and-reduced-lunch share; Tennessee publishes no economic-disadvantage measure in this build. 1 of 64 Leader in Me schools and 252 of 1,817 comparison schools sit at 99% or above, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count; 241 comparison schools read above 100% (a count larger than enrollment) and are capped. 25.0% of Leader in Me schools and 32.3% of comparison schools are high-poverty on this measure. A state file with its own poverty measure replaces it when one lands in the state folder.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 52 of the 64 roster schools carry a date, and 38 were past it by more than 30 days on September 17, 2026.   • Statewide averages jumped, which usually means a new test. Statewide mean proficiency moved by eight points or more between consecutive years: ELA 2014-15 to 2015-16, 48.7 to 29.4 (-19.3); ELA 2015-16 to 2016-17, 29.4 to 39.8 (+10.4); Math 2014-15 to 2015-16, 56.7 to 19.5 (-37.2); Math 2015-16 to 2016-17, 19.5 to 46.1 (+26.6); Math 2016-17 to 2017-18, 46.1 to 35.6 (-10.5). A move that size across a whole state is a test change or new cut scores far more often than real learning, so a growth window that spans it should be read with care; the full-history window scores each school against its own state each year and absorbs it. Until the state's testing history is confirmed and entered in the build's state table, this note stands as the warning.
  • Joining Tennessee's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. Tennessee's files key on the state's system and school numbers instead (Hamblen County is system 320, Alpha Elementary its school 5), which share no digits with the federal id. The NCES Common Core of Data directory carries both ids for every public school, so that is the anchor: 1,873 id pairs for 1,873 of the 1,881 schools on this page, including 64 of 64 Leader in Me schools and 1,809 of 1,817 comparison schools. The map was checked against the state files' own school names, which it was not built from: they agree with DOMO's name on 1,859 of 1,861 crosswalked schools (2 read one by one: renamed schools and abbreviations). An independent value check from files the map was not built from either: the state's ESSA chronic-absenteeism indicator score against DOMO's chronic absence on the same school-years ranks them at Spearman -0.497 across 7,492 school-years (mean DOMO rate by score 0: 32.8%, 1: 21.5%, 2: 17.2%, 3: 15.0%, 4: 14.8%), and the graduation indicator against DOMO's graduation rate at 0.371 across 1,524.   • The Tennessee Educator Survey: what teachers say about their school. The state surveys every teacher each spring and publishes a school's results only where 45% or more of its teachers responded and the sample is large enough (674 schools were withheld in 2017 on those two rules, and later files simply omit them), so a school-year is present or absent, never partly suppressed. On the satisfaction item the Leader in Me line runs from 2018 (15 schools) to 2025 (15 schools), against 980 comparison schools in 2025.
  • Teacher retention and the survey's satisfaction item are measures, not only levels. Both are per-school rates, so they run through the same machinery as every other measure (balanced panels, the band filter, the matched view, the growth test), carried on each band a school reports as the core carries chronic absence. 138 retention cells the state withholds (fewer than five teachers, or an asterisk) stay missing, and 26 values of exactly zero are read as unreported rather than as a school that kept no teacher: 24 of them sit in 2023-24, nearly all charter schools in Memphis and Nashville, a reporting gap rather than a result.
  • Discipline is a share of schools, and two early files are out. From 2021-22 the state's discipline files suppress every school's suspension rate under 10 suspended students or 5%, and the count with it (2021-22: 473 of 1,835 schools carry a rate, 366 read 'less than 5%', 996 'fewer than 10 students', the 5%-or-more flag known for 1,593; 2022-23: 458 of 1,831 schools carry a rate, 397 read 'less than 5%', 976 'fewer than 10 students', the 5%-or-more flag known for 1,600; 2023-24: 536 of 1,830 schools carry a rate, 426 read 'less than 5%', 868 'fewer than 10 students', the 5%-or-more flag known for 1,676; 2024-25: 571 of 1,838 schools carry a rate, 428 read 'less than 5%', 839 'fewer than 10 students', the 5%-or-more flag known for 1,677). A rate known for a quarter of schools, all of them the high-suspending quarter, cannot feed a growth test honestly, so the published rates go to the research pack only and the card carries what the suppression rules leave knowable: whether 5% or more of a school's students were suspended (a published rate is 5 or more by construction, 'less than 5%' is under, and 'fewer than 10 students' is under at any school of 200 or more students; the 2021-22 and 2022-23 files carry no enrollment, so those years borrow the 2023-24 count with the bar raised to 250). 2017-18 and 2018-19 are complete (the rate is published for nearly every school) and are read directly. The 2015-16 and 2016-17 files are excluded by name: they carry one undivided 'suspension' rate that passes 100% at 7 schools in 2015-16 and 4 schools in 2016-17 (an incident count, not a share of students), and the 2015-16 file lists only the 946 schools that reported a suspension. The 2020-21 file is a closure year.
  • Ready Graduate, the ACT and college enrollment are the class's own year. TDOE publishes Ready Graduate a year late and labels each file with the later school year (the '2025-26' file is the class of 2025); every value here sits on its own graduating class, 2018 to 2025, and the published share rebuilds from the file's own counts on every row. The statewide mean rises from about 40.3% to 56.6% across the classes, so a school's own direction is read against that tide. The ACT files are the classes of 2021 to 2025. The postsecondary files are labelled by graduating class and carry the state's sentinels (-10, -5, -95 and the complementary -2), all read as missing; because the whole row is suppressed whenever any institution type falls under 5%, an unsuppressed college-going share exists for about 95 of 407 high schools in a year. The class of 2023 file (126 schools) counts enrollment through the following summer, fall or spring rather than the fall alone, a different measure, and is excluded by name. Tennessee's Leader in Me schools are almost all elementary and middle schools: across the whole window 2 Leader in Me high schools carry a Ready Graduate share while counted, 2 an ACT share and 0 an unsuppressed college-going share, under the three a line needs, so the three sit in the research pack and the school list rather than on the card or the rows.
  • The ESSA indicator scores are scores, not rates, and 2017-18 is out. The state's school indicator files carry each school's federal accountability score on six indicators, 0 to 4, binned against the school's own targets. The 2018-19 and 2021-22 to 2024-25 files carry whole-number scores; the 2017-18 file carries decimal averages under the first-year framework (1268 of 1649 growth scores are not whole numbers) and is excluded by name. The unlabelled file is 2018-19: it lists schools NCES first records in 2018-19 and none from 2019-20. Growth, achievement and chronic absenteeism are on the card; graduation, Ready Graduate and English-learner progress are in the research pack. No Tennessee file in the folder carries a chronic-absence or graduation rate itself, so the core's DOMO values for those stand and nothing is filled.
  • 2025-26 reading, math and social studies come from Tennessee's own results and extend the reading-and-math figure to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for Tennessee. The 2025-26 rows (19,217 school-subject-grade rows for 1,704 schools) were read from the department's school-level assessment file, posted in August 2026, and joined to the DOMO id through the same NCES state-id crosswalk the rest of this page uses. Proficiency is the file's own on-track-and-mastered share, which is what DOMO carries for Tennessee in every earlier year: all 26,204 of DOMO's 2024-25 Tennessee rows are one row of the department's 2024-25 file, and the proficiency figure and all four achievement levels agree to the hundredth of a point. The levels are carried on the 10,047 rows where the state publishes all four; Tennessee masks the neighbouring cells whenever one level falls under 5%, and DOMO's own rows carry levels on exactly the rows where all four are published. Science and the science end-of-course tests are the one part of the 2025-26 release left out: they are in a standard-setting year, and the department left them out of its own state-level results, so science on this page still ends at 2024-25. The grade-level changes these rows produce reproduce the department's published figures (third-grade reading 41.7 to 44.9, fourth-grade math 46.1 to 49.6).

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 59 surveys across 27 Tennessee schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 14 answered by more than one group (blended), 12 answered by staff, 10 answered by students, 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (27 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 3 of the 25 schools counted as Leader in Me in Tennessee meet the criteria, and a further 2 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. A school in its third year is measured from its baseline year; a school already in the process is measured from the start of the window.
Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

64 measures were screened, grouped into 20 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 12 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was; Tennessee publishes no locale or economic-disadvantage measure in these records, so those filters are not offered.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • The Tennessee Educator Survey is published only for schools where 45% or more of teachers responded, so the survey lines rest on the schools that reached the bar in each year (roughly six in ten statewide), and a school can be present one year and absent the next.
  • Tennessee's survey items change wording and numbering between years; the constructs on this page are drawn only across years where the item is worded the same, and the 2020 survey is not in the folder.
  • From 2021-22 the state suppresses a school's suspension rate under 10 students or 5%, so the rate is known only for the high-suspending quarter of schools; the page carries the share of schools at 5% or more instead.
  • Ready Graduate's statewide share rose from about 40% to 57% across the classes of 2018 to 2025, so a school's own rise is read against that tide.
  • The ESSA indicator scores are the state's 0-4 bins against each school's own targets, not rates; they are levels, described, never tested.
