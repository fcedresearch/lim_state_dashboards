Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 4 North Carolina schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • Exits are dated from each school's Leader in Me contract expiration date. A school more than 30 days past its expiration on October 01, 2026 has exited on that date: an expiration in January to June keeps that school year, one in July to December does not, and the years after count in neither group. 52 of the 195 roster schools are past their expiration and are listed by name below; 0 inside the grace window stay active; 26 carry no expiration date and are treated as active.
  • No roster school reappears in the comparison export after its roster years, so DOMO's own per-year flags add no exit beyond the expiration dates.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.
  • Subject-years excluded by name: Science 2024-25 (North Carolina replaced its science tests in 2024-25)
  • Poverty bands use North Carolina economically-disadvantaged share (NC DPI report cards, rcd_acc_eds) where the state publishes it, and DOMO's lunch share elsewhere.
  • North Carolina's own measures join through a district-and-name crosswalk from the state's six-character school codes to the DOMO id, audited against the state's average daily membership; a school the crosswalk cannot reach keeps its DOMO measures and simply has no North Carolina measures.
  • Chronic absence comes from the state's report cards for every year, because the DOMO records carry none for North Carolina; the graduation rate is DOMO's own where DOMO has it (the state's cohort rate to the decimal) and the state's table fills the years DOMO lacks. No DOMO value is overwritten.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are left off every North Carolina card: buildings were closed for much of both years and the statewide suspension rate fell by nine tenths.
  • 2025-26 values for EVAAS growth met or exceeded, the EVAAS growth index, the share of ACT subject tests meeting the benchmark, WorkKeys Silver or higher, the four-year cohort graduation rate and the Title I flag come from the state's 2025-26 School Assessment and Other Indicator Data workbook, read for no year the report-card tables carry, and only after the same reader gave back the tables' 2024-25 values from the 2024-25 workbook to the decimal; every other North Carolina measure stops where its report-card table stops.
  • The 2024-25 college-and-career-ready composite is excluded by name: the 2024-25 college-and-career-ready composite includes the new science tests, whose statewide figure fell 18 points in one year.
  • Charter schools are grouped as "Charter schools (statewide)" on the district filter (232 schools: the state's own charter flag, plus the one-school DOMO districts the crosswalk could not reach), so district insights never fire on one school.
  • 2025-26 grades 3-8 reading and math and the end-of-course tests (English II, NC Math 1, NC Math 3), from NC DPI's disaggregated data set, extend each subject to the newest year; proficiency is grade-level proficient (levels 3-5) and the below-grade-level measure is the not-proficient share, both as in prior years.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags, or a dated exit where the build has one. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 132 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 0 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points on the free-and-reduced-lunch share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 32% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 94 pairs (0 unmatched); Secondary: 38 pairs (0 unmatched).

7 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Durham's Performance Learning Center, Johnston County Schools Career Technical, Milton M Daniels Learning Center, Performance Learning Center, Stanly Academy Learning Center, The Community Learning Center @ Pinckney, The Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

Schools like a district's schools: look-alike mode

The matched view runs from each Leader in Me school out to one partner. Look-alike mode runs the same matching the other way: from the schools of a district we are talking to (usually one with no Leader in Me schools) out to every school in the state that looks like them, on both sides of the comparison.

What “like” means: the same grade band, poverty band, student make-up, Title I status, school size band, starting score. Each of the district's schools is matched at its first year with results inside the chosen window; a look-alike starts within 0.25 of a standard deviation of it on the share of students at or above grade level (and on the measure on screen, when that is a different measure), and within 10 points of poverty. Unknown traits are never used to exclude a school. If fewer than three Leader in Me schools match on every trait, the page relaxes size band, then Title I, then student make-up, then the second starting score, then widens the poverty caliper to 20 points, and it says which of those it did. Grade band, poverty band and the starting score on the share at or above grade level are never relaxed.

How the comparison is read: the look-alike Leader in Me schools are tested against the look-alike comparison schools with the same significance rule as everywhere else, and a look-alike school counts as Leader in Me if that is what it is by the end of the window. The district's own schools are drawn on the trend chart as their own line, for reference, and are never tested; with an anchor set, Compare to similar schools only is switched off, because the two controls answer different questions.

Why these traits: The traits are the ones the matched-pairs view already uses (grade band, poverty band, student make-up), which follow the What Works Clearinghouse practice of establishing baseline equivalence on demographics and the pre-test, plus NCES school-size bands. The caliper of a quarter of a standard deviation on the starting score is the WWC's outer limit for baseline equivalence; the ten-point poverty caliper is the matched-pairs rule. Under three schools on a side nothing is tested. Title I status comes from the NCES Common Core of Data, last published for 2021-22 and carried forward.

The school list download (CSV, or a one-page Word table) names every school in the view with its traits and its start, end and change on each measure; the research pack adds a look_alike section with the tests for every measure, grade band and window, and the member list with each school's closest anchor school.

Two trait filters added in September 2026

Title I
Whether the school operated a federal Title I program (schoolwide or targeted assistance), as the National Center for Education Statistics last published it in 2021-22; NCES stopped publishing Title I status after that year, so each school's last known status is carried forward. Title I is the federal designation for schools serving many low-income students.
School size
School size from the DOMO enrollment count, in the school-size bands the National Center for Education Statistics uses in its school surveys: under 100, 100 to 199, 200 to 499, 500 to 749, 750 to 999, and 1,000 or more students.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); a point needs three schools and the field-insight movers need ten. The MRA tab section below has the full rules.
  • What “meeting the standard” means here. The file's PercentMetStandard field, on 170,681 rows. Checked against the reported levels as the export numbers them: it equals levels 3 and 4 on 9.5% of rows; levels 2, 3 and 4 on 10.2% of rows; levels 1 and 2 (1 the top) on 2.8% of rows; levels 1, 2 and 3 (1 the top) on 2.8% of rows. Read as no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate. Checked school year by school year and subject by subject, the levels add to 100 and reproduce the proficiency rate on 0.0% of rows.
  • The achievement levels are not offered as measures of their own here. North Carolina's file reports achievement levels that cannot stand as measures here: no combination of the reported levels reproduces PercentMetStandard on more than 10% of rows (a five-level state whose export carries four columns, usually) (no combination of the reported levels reproduces the proficiency rate; levels add up on 0.0% of rows). The page carries the state's proficiency rate and the share in the lowest level; the remaining levels join once the state's level structure is confirmed.
  • School type comes from NCES, and its last published year is carried forward. Title I status, magnet status, charter status and school type are joined from the NCES Common Core of Data school files by the twelve-digit NCES id. NCES published Title I and magnet status through 2022 and stopped after that year, so each school's last known status stands for later years; charter status and school type run through 2024 and are carried forward likewise. In North Carolina that is 1,451 Title I schools, 220 charter schools, 168 magnet schools and 77 alternative schools among the schools on this page. A school type with fewer than three schools is not offered as a filter.
  • Which tests sit in each subject. Subject labels in North Carolina's file are mapped to reading, math, science, social studies and writing from one national table. ELA: English II, Reading; Math: Math I, Math III, Mathematics; Science: Biology, Science.
  • DOMO's lunch share is kept for the research pack, and it has a ceiling. On the page's own measure, 9.2% of Leader in Me schools and 13.8% of comparison schools are high-poverty (75% or more). DOMO's free-and-reduced-lunch share, kept in the research pack, reads 69.2% and 69.0% on the same schools; 81 of 195 Leader in Me schools and 980 of 2,567 comparison schools sit at 99% or above on it, which is what community eligibility does to a meal count, and 6 comparison schools read above 100% and are capped.
  • How exits are dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, so exits come from the roster's own contract expiration column: 169 of the 195 roster schools carry a date, and 52 were past it by more than 30 days on October 01, 2026. Exits by year: 2018: 2, 2021: 5, 2022: 6, 2023: 10, 2024: 15, 2025: 9, 2026: 5. 26 roster schools have no expiration date (25 with a start date) and stay active under the rules. Run without those 25 schools the headline reads Leader in Me 8.1 against comparison 8.37 (p = 0.73, 117 vs 2215 schools), and with them 8.15 against 8.37 (p = 0.75, 140 schools). Schools that left the roster entirely are still unmarked in the comparison group.
  • Poverty on this page is North Carolina's own measure, not the lunch share. North Carolina publishes an economically-disadvantaged share for every school every year (rcd_acc_eds, 2016-17 to 2024-25); each school carries its latest published year. On the 2,667 schools with both figures it correlates with DOMO's lunch share at r = 0.629, but the lunch share sits +29.3 points above it at the median (medians 98.5 against 55.8), which is what near-universal community eligibility does to a meal count: on the state's measure 9% of Leader in Me schools and 13% of comparison schools are high-poverty (75%+), against 69% and 70% on the lunch share. The state writes any share above 95 as 95 (3 schools sit there). The state's measure therefore sets the poverty bands, the poverty filter, the matching caliper and look-alike mode, as the September North Carolina build did. It covers 2,686 schools (195 Leader in Me); the 76 it does not cover keep DOMO's lunch share. The research pack carries both.
  • Joining North Carolina's own tables. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk. The state's report cards key on its own six-character school codes instead, so those are crosswalked by district and name, with charters joined by city and by statewide name and a code that changed settled by the years each id reports: 2,710 of 2,762 schools, including 195 of 195 Leader in Me schools and 2,515 of 2,567 comparison schools. The crosswalk was checked against a table it was not built from, the state's average daily membership against DOMO enrollment: correlation 0.9972 across 2,491 schools, half of them within 2.63% on size and 99.2% within 25%. Two schools keep the DOMO measures and no North Carolina measures because another DOMO id carries the same state code: Southside/Ashpole Elementary (Public Schools of Robeson County); Moss Street Elementary (Rockingham County Schools). Each is one school whose federal id changed, and the state's numbers stay with its current id.
  • Chronic absence and graduation come from the state. The DOMO records carry no chronic absence value for any North Carolina row (0 values across every row), so chronic absence comes from the state's report cards (rcd_chronic_absent, the share of students absent 10% or more of their enrolled days, 2017-18 on): 15,246 school-years for 2,668 schools on this page, 47,426 rows filled. The DOMO graduation rate is the state's four-year cohort rate to the decimal (within 0.15 on 98.0% of the 2,906 secondary school-years both carry), so the state's table supplies the years DOMO lacks (7,543 rows, 2025-26 from the state's indicator workbook, as the note on 2025-26 explains) and no DOMO value is overwritten. The state writes any rate above 95 as 95 (1,542 of 5,956 school-years on this page), which flattens growth at the top for both groups.
  • Suspension counts are self-reported. Suspensions per 1,000 come from what each school reported to the state (rcd_inc2), and districts differ in how they record, so a school's own direction over time is firmer ground than the gap between groups. The chart's per-100 figure weights each school by the state's own average daily membership that year and runs 2017-18 to 2024-25. 2019-20 and 2020-21 are left off: the statewide short-term suspension rate fell from 134 per 1,000 in 2018-19 to 13 in the remote year and was back at 147 in 2021-22.
  • EVAAS growth, ACT, WorkKeys, graduation and Title I status run to 2025-26 from the state's indicator workbook. The report-card tables behind the North Carolina card are the state's 2024-25 release and end at 2024-25. NC DPI's 2025-26 School Assessment and Other Indicator Data workbook, released in September 2026, carries six of the page's measures for 2025-26, and they are read from it for that year only: EVAAS growth met or exceeded from the School Growth tab's overall status for all students, 2,481 schools on this page; the EVAAS growth index from the School Growth tab's overall index for all students, 2,481 schools on this page; the share of ACT subject tests meeting the benchmark from the data-set tab's ACTALL code for all students, all four ACT subtest benchmarks, 644 schools on this page; WorkKeys Silver or higher from the data-set tab's WK code for all students, Silver or higher, 432 schools on this page; the four-year cohort graduation rate from the data-set tab's CGRS code for all students, the four-year cohort rate, 600 schools on this page; the Title I flag from the data-set tab's Title I Served flag, 2,525 schools on this page. Another 46 high schools on this page have a 2025-26 graduation rate in the workbook but no 2025-26 test results here, so there is no 2025-26 school-year for the rate to sit on, as in every earlier year. The same reader, applied to the 2024-25 workbook, gives back the report-card tables' own 2024-25 values to the decimal (2,590 of 2,590 schools for EVAAS growth met or exceeded; 2,590 of 2,590 schools for the EVAAS growth index; 695 of 695 schools for the share of ACT subject tests meeting the benchmark; 452 of 452 schools for WorkKeys Silver or higher; 691 of 691 schools for the four-year cohort graduation rate; 2,668 of 2,668 schools for the Title I flag), and the district and state rows for ACT, WorkKeys and graduation as well, so each 2025-26 point follows the definition of the years before it. The ACT measure counts subject tests, not students: of every English, math, reading and science test taken, the share at or above that subject's benchmark (the data set's ACTALL, 'All The ACT subtest benchmarks (sum of benchmarks met)', the code the report-card table's ACT measure matches). The graduation rate is the four-year cohort rate the workbook reports for its own school year, keyed to the spring of that year exactly as the table keys every earlier year (the 2024-25 workbook's rate is the table's 2024-25 rate, school for school). No value for 2024-25 or earlier comes from a workbook. These stay at 2024-25: the school performance grade and score and the low-performing designation (the workbook carries no grades; NC DPI published 2025-26 grades only on its testing dashboard); chronic absence, suspensions, Read to Achieve, college enrollment, beginning teachers, National Board certification and the economically-disadvantaged share (none is in the workbook, and the state's 2025-26 report-card tables are not yet released); the college-and-career-ready composite, which the workbook does carry but which still folds in the science tests North Carolina replaced in 2024-25, the reason 2024-25 is left out.
  • Charter schools are grouped. DOMO files each of North Carolina's charter schools as its own district, which would put 348 "districts", most of them one school, in the filter. The 232 charter schools (225 flagged as charters in the state's own school table, and 7 one-school DOMO districts the crosswalk could not reach, each filed under its own name) are grouped as "Charter schools (statewide)", leaving 118 district labels; district insights never fire on one school. The state publishes no agency-level figures for the group, so the research pack's published section is empty for it: each charter's own published figures are its school row.
  • The export's lowest-level column changes meaning in 2021-22. North Carolina reports five achievement levels and the DOMO export carries four columns, which is why the levels are not offered as measures of their own above. The lowest column also changes meaning: through 2018-19 PercentLevel1 is the state's Level 1 alone (averaging 20.6 across 53,146 rows, with Level 2 beside it at 20.2); from 2021-22 PercentLevel2 is zero on 100.0% of rows and PercentLevel1 is 100 minus the proficiency rate on 100.0% of 87,552 rows, the state's not-proficient share (levels 1 and 2 together, averaging 46.3). The percent-in-the-lowest-level measure is therefore Level 1 through 2018-19 and below grade level from 2021-22: one column, two definitions. The straight-numbers window sits inside the second era; the full-history window scores each school against its own year, which absorbs the step but does not make the two eras one series. The September builder rebuilt the measure as levels 1 and 2 throughout; the core builder reads the column as exported.
  • The 2024-25 college-and-career-ready composite is excluded by name, and 2025-26 is held for the same reason. The 2024-25 college-and-career-ready composite includes the new science tests, whose statewide figure fell 18 points in one year; the years before it are on the card, and the main charts use the lower grade-level bar. The 2025-26 indicator workbook carries the composite (its levels 4 and 5 give back the 2024-25 table to the decimal), but it still folds in the new science tests, so a 2025-26 point would follow 2023-24 on the old tests and is not drawn.
  • 2025-26 results come from North Carolina's own data set and extend grades 3-8 reading and math and the end-of-course tests (English II, NC Math 1, NC Math 3) to the newest year. DOMO's national subject file ends at 2024-25 for North Carolina. The 2025-26 rows (14,735 school-grade-test rows for 2,464 schools: 13,106 grades 3-8 reading and math, 1,629 end of course) were read from NC DPI's disaggregated performance data set (all students), released September 2, 2026, and joined to the DOMO id through the same agency-code crosswalk the rest of the North Carolina page uses. Every row counts regular assessments (students who took the NCEXTEND1 alternate are not in them), which is how DOMO's North Carolina rows count. Grade-level proficiency (levels 3-5) is the proficiency measure, as in every prior year; the not-proficient share is the below-grade-level measure. Results the state masks (above 95% or below 5%) carry no 2025-26 value, and a school whose 2024-25 rows in a grade band and subject did not include one of the end-of-course tests has no 2025-26 row for that test, so no test joins a school's set that its 2024-25 results did not include. Grades 5 and 8 science and Biology are not extended to 2025-26: North Carolina replaced its science tests in 2024-25, a year this page sets aside, so a 2025-26 science point would follow 2023-24 on the old tests. North Carolina's five achievement levels do not reconcile to the four DOMO columns, so 2025-26 carries proficiency and the below-grade-level share, not a full level breakdown, exactly as the years before it do.

The MRA tab

The Measurable Results Assessment is the survey Leader in Me schools take of their own students, staff and families. It has no comparison group, so the tab describes level and change inside Leader in Me schools and never an effect against other schools. 275 surveys across 126 North Carolina schools are on the tab, 2021-22 onward, current MRA version.

The instrument has 3 composites, 9 areas under them (level 2) and 25 submeasures (level 3), 37 measures in all: 13 answered by students, 12 answered by staff, 11 answered by more than one group (blended), 1 answered by families. Which respondent group answers a measure is read from the surveys themselves (a measure is credited to one group when it is blank on nearly every survey that group skipped). The export does not say which submeasures roll up into which area, so the tab groups measures by domain and level and does not assert a finer nesting.

  • Scale. Values are IRT scale points built so the average school lands near 75 with most schools within about 8 points of it. The tab draws a dashed line at 75 wherever it fits the axis and says so when it does not; nothing is drawn on a 0 to 100 axis.
  • What is kept. Only the current instrument version (2021-22 on). A value of exactly 100.0 is read as not reported. A survey with no respondents and no composite is set aside as a shell.
  • One survey per school and year. A school can take a fall checkpoint and a spring annual in one school year. The default keeps one per school and year, the spring one where both exist, then the survey with more measures filled, then the one with more respondents; the season filter can show spring only, fall only, or every survey (a school with two in a year is then averaged for that year). In this build no school carried two surveys in one school year.
  • The change. The summary's change is each school's own move from its first to its latest survey in the chosen years, one value per school, so a school surveyed in 2021-22 and 2025-26 counts once with a four-year span and a school surveyed in the last two years counts once with a one-year span; the sentence prints the spread of spans. It is tested with a paired (one-sample) t test on those moves, called significant at p at or below 0.05, and only tested with ten or more schools. The composition check under the trend prints the same move up to four ways: every school surveyed, the same schools at the two fixed ends of the range, each school first to latest, and spring administrations only, so a level move that a growing roster produced is visible next to the same-schools move.
  • Lines and bars. A point needs three or more schools in that year and group; a line needs two such points. The Lighthouse and years-in-process lines are subsets of the Leader in Me line, regrouped every survey year. The movers panel tests every measure in a domain the same way as the summary and marks the significant ones.
  • Filters. District, poverty band, student make-up, school size and school type come from each surveyed school's own records (126 schools carry them here): the roster and DOMO records for district, poverty and make-up, NCES for Title I, charter, magnet and alternative status, and our Salesforce funding source for Kern Family Foundation schools. Survey level is the level the survey was built for as the MRA records it.

Best-performing schools: criteria, measures screened, and results

A screen over every measure on this page that names the schools worth a case study or a mention, with the evidence printed beside each one. 45 of the 142 schools counted as Leader in Me in North Carolina meet the criteria, and a further 1 meet them but are past a contract expiration date within the last year and carry no rank. Runs are measured across 2021-22 to 2025-26, the window this page already compares on, and every school is read against the other schools in the state on the same measure over the same years, so a statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.

CriterionDefinition
Schools included in the screenEvery school counted as Leader in Me in the latest year of results. A school whose contract ran out inside the last year is screened and ranked separately: its results are still its results, but it is held out of the numbered list until the account is settled, and the contract filter brings it back into view. A contract more than a year past its date is out of the screen.
Threshold for a gain, relative to the stateThe same measure, the same years, every other school in the state. A gain counts when the school moved at least half a standard deviation further than they did, and when the move is at least a fifth of the spread between schools. A statewide test change or a good year for everybody cancels out.
Run length and start yearThree year-over-year steps ending in the latest year the state reports that measure, inside the same window the rest of this page compares on, so a run never crosses the closure years or a change in the state's tests. A run starts no earlier than the school's baseline year, the last year before Leader in Me counted for it, so a school cannot reach this list on years it spent outside the process. A school in its third year is measured from its baseline year; a school already in the process is measured from the start of the window.
Dip tolerance within a runA dip is allowed when it is shallower than half the total gain and shallower than one normal year's move, and when the school has gained a year back since. A run where a single year carries more than 70% of the move is set aside, because a jump that size is almost always a change in what was counted.
Declines that exclude a schoolAny measure that fell clearly over the same years: a full standard deviation behind what schools in the state did, and a real amount rather than a wobble. The bar for a decline is set higher than the bar for a gain on purpose. A gain has to stand out to count as evidence; a measure has to fall unmistakably before it takes a school off a recognition list. A measure is read across the school's own first and last reported years inside the window, so a measure the state publishes late cannot hide a school slipping. Anything that slipped by less than that is printed under the school as something to check before the story is told. The MRA is the exception: it is our own instrument rather than one of the state's reported factors, so a falling MRA never removes a school on its own. It is always printed under the school, because a falling MRA at a school we are about to celebrate is the first thing somebody needs to see.
Criteria for qualifyingGains on two or more outcomes, at least one of them in the state's own results, and nothing falling. A school already in the state's top tenth for three years qualifies on two of those plus one gain, and a school holding at the top across two different areas with nothing falling is listed last. Reading and math are two outcomes; reading proficiency, the top achievement level and the standard score are one, and any two measures that move together across the state's schools count once.
Rank order and pointsSchools that improved in every year since their Leader in Me baseline year come first, then gains in more than one area, then gains in one area, then schools holding at the top. Inside each group the points decide, and every school's points are printed beside it.

55 measures were screened, grouped into 18 separate outcomes so that two readings of the same test count once. Only measures whose direction this build has declared are screened: the row measures, the state's own published measures, and the MRA. Values that sit in the research pack without a declared direction are left out, because a recognition list cannot afford to read a rise in a share of enrolled pupils as a gain. The middle achievement levels are left out everywhere, because a school empties its middle band both when pupils move up and when they move down.

Known limits

  • Exits are dated from contract expiration dates rather than from the exports, which carry none. 26 roster schools have no expiration date and are treated as active; the methods note above prints the headline with and without them.
  • Enrollment, student make-up and the lunch share in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school years ago.
  • Title I status is NCES's last published year (2022) carried forward, so a school whose Title I status changed since then is read as it was.
  • Chronic absence and graduation rate run through their own last real year in the SchoolDigger records and are never carried forward.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Suspension counts are self-reported by schools, so districts differ in how carefully they record; a school's own direction over time is firmer ground than the gap between groups.
  • The state drops results for fewer than ten students and writes percentages under 5 or over 95 as 5 or 95, so suppressed cells arrive as absence, the graduation rate is capped at 95 for both groups, and the state's economically-disadvantaged share tops out at 95.
  • The state lists Title I status every year in its school table, but the Title I filter uses the NCES designation carried forward from 2021-22; the state's own per-year flag is on the report-card card as context.
  • Chronic absence is the state's own count for every year; the graduation rate is one series from two sources that agree to the decimal on the years both carry.
  • The North Carolina measures end in different years: EVAAS growth met or exceeded, the EVAAS growth index, the share of ACT subject tests meeting the benchmark, WorkKeys Silver or higher, the four-year cohort graduation rate and the Title I flag run to 2025-26; the performance grades, chronic absence, suspensions, Read to Achieve, college enrollment, teacher measures and the economically-disadvantaged share stop at 2024-25 until the state releases its 2025-26 report-card tables, so a card's latest-year comparison is not always the same year as the test-score charts'.
  • The export's lowest-level column is the state's Level 1 alone through 2018-19 and levels 1 and 2 together from 2021-22, so the percent-in-the-lowest-level measure is not one series across the full history; the straight-numbers window sits inside the second era.
  • The state's own file for the newest year does not yet carry every test DOMO carried the year before (2025-26 5th Grade Science (1,464 rows the year before); 2025-26 8th Grade Science (767 rows the year before); 2025-26 End of course Biology (641 rows the year before)). Those results usually come out later in the fall. So a school is never compared on one set of tests against a different set, a school that took one of those tests the year before has its newest-year results in that grade band and subject left out (0 rows at 0 schools) until the missing results are released and folded in; the rest of its results, and every earlier year, are unchanged.
